# Quanta Cura and the Syllabus of Errors (1864)

Encyclical of **8 December 1864** with **80 condemned propositions** attached, grouped under headings: pantheism–indifferentism; socialism, communism and secret societies; the Church and her rights; errors of liberalism.

## The interpretation problem
**A product of the Italian situation, read by non-Italians in their own terms.**

| Reader | "Progress, liberalism and modern civilization" meant |
|---|---|
| Italians | Closed monasteries, secular education, anti-clericalism |
| English | The Great Exhibition, Peel and Gladstone, an improved society |
| French | The principles of 1789 |
| Americans | All that was held sacred in the American system |

**Words capable of such varying interpretation were used unwisely in a document sent to London, New York, Paris and Berlin.** Archbishop John McCloskey of New York thought it placed American Catholics in antagonism to America's institutions; Lincoln pronounced himself scandalized; *Le Siècle* called it "the last challenge flung at the modern world by an expiring papacy."

## The rescue
**Félix Dupanloup of Orléans (1865)**: the pope spoke of the **thesis** (the eternal ideal society) and not the **antithesis** (what is possible in an actual society). Some 630 bishops thanked him.

## The paradox
Read in 1864 as evidence of an expiring papacy, **it became a symbol of papal moral authority after 1870.**
