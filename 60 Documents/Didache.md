# Didache

Chapter 7 preserves the oldest surviving baptismal instruction: baptise in living (running, cold) water; if unavailable, other water; if not cold, warm; if neither, pour water three times on the head in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Both baptiser and baptised are to fast beforehand. Two lessons: the Trinitarian formula is already fixed by c. 100, and the Church was already adapting the sign to circumstance without anxiety.

### The Eucharistic Prayers (Didache 9-10, 14)
Chapters 9-10 preserve the oldest surviving Christian Eucharistic prayers, strikingly close to Jewish table blessings, with thanksgiving over the cup before the bread and the gathering image: "As this broken bread was scattered over the hills and, when gathered, became one, so let your Church be gathered from the ends of the earth into your kingdom." Chapter 14 requires confession of sins before breaking bread "so that your sacrifice (thysia) may be pure," citing Malachi 1:11 — placing sacrificial vocabulary in the earliest stratum of Christian Eucharistic language.
