# Council of Trent Session XIV

Promulgated 25 November 1551, treating Penance together with Extreme Unction. Defines Penance as a true and proper sacrament instituted by Christ principally in John 20:22-23, and as the secunda tabula post naufragium for those who fall after baptism. Requires confession of all mortal sins of which the penitent is conscious after diligent examination, according to species and number with the circumstances that change the species. Teaches that attrition is a true gift of God disposing to grace within the sacrament; that absolution is a judicial act (actus iudicialis) and not a bare announcement of forgiveness — the premise from which integral confession follows; and that satisfaction is real and salutary while deriving all its power from Christ's merit.

### Extreme Unction
The same session that treats Penance also gives the doctrine and canons on Extreme Unction, in three chapters and four canons. The sacrament was "insinuated" by Christ in Mark 6:13 and "promulgated" by James 5:14-15; its matter is oil blessed by a bishop and its form the prayer of the priest; its effects are the grace of the Holy Spirit, the wiping away of sins and of the remnants of sin, the alleviation and strengthening of the soul, and sometimes the restoration of bodily health "when expedient for the salvation of the soul." Canon 4 anathematises the reading of "presbyters" in James as elders by age rather than ordained priests. Crucially, the decree says the sacrament is **especially** (praesertim) for those so dangerously ill as to seem near death — without defining that it is only for them, a reticence that left the legal door open for SC 73.
