# Rerum Novarum (1891)

Leo XIII's encyclical on capital and labour — **at heart a pastoral intervention to protect workers**.

## The core argument
**Just wages → savings → wider ownership of property → the gap between classes bridged → increased productivity → civil society renovated by Christianity.** Property, not class struggle, is the bridge.

## Where it came from
Not invented in Rome: it takes up **[[Wilhelm von Ketteler]]**'s programme (state limits on working hours, Sunday rest, factory inspection), the practice of **[[Leon Harmel]]** and the legislative work of Albert de Mun. See [[Social Catholicism]].

## Reception and effect
- First episcopal response: Victor-Joseph Doutreloux of Liège.
- Inspired Harmel's workers' study groups and the **Christian Worker Congress (1894–1900)**, renamed the **Congress of [[Christian Democracy]]** in 1896, and a trade union of 600,000 by 1899.
- Deep impact on **U.S. Catholic involvement in the labour movement**; stressed the ethical obligation to pay a **"living wage."**
- **No pope before Leo had produced such a consistent message, and every pope since has had to address the "social question."**
