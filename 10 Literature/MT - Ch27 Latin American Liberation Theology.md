# Ch. 27 — Latin American Liberation Theology
*Chapter author: Rebecca S. Chopp and Ethna Regan*

## Core Idea
Liberation theology reconstructs the doctrines of God, sin, redemption, and church around the historical, political liberation of the poor — recognised as the active subjects, not passive objects, of history — worked out through grassroots Base Ecclesial Communities and a "preferential option for the poor."

## Detailed Explanation
Emerging in the late 1960s against a backdrop of colonial economic dependency and brutal military dictatorships, the movement was catalysed by the 1968 Medellín conference of Latin American bishops, which officially committed the Catholic Church to the preferential option for the poor, and by Gustavo Gutiérrez's programmatic A Theology of Liberation (1973). It reads "liberation" as a tensive theological metaphor reframing salvation as historical and social, not merely private and spiritual, and uses Paulo Freire's conscientización (consciousness-raising pedagogy) to help the poor recognise themselves as history's active subjects rather than its passive victims. The movement paid a severe institutional and human cost: Leonardo Boff was silenced for ten months by the CDF (led by Joseph Ratzinger) in 1985, and Archbishop Óscar Romero and Jon Sobrino's fellow Jesuits were martyred in El Salvador.

## Example
Leonardo Boff's account of Base Ecclesial Communities (CEBs) as a genuine "reinvention of the church" — small, lay-led, scripture-reading communities of the poor functioning with minimal clerical hierarchy — gives liberation theology's abstract claim about "the poor as subjects of history" a concrete, replicable institutional form still active across Latin America today.

## Illustration
Traditional charity is like handing a hungry person a fish through a fence — the giver stays safely on one side. Liberation theology's preferential option for the poor is more like climbing over the fence and helping rebuild the pond together — it insists God's own solidarity with the poor requires structural, not just charitable, transformation.

## Key Terms
- **Opción Preferencial por los Pobres** (Spanish) — "preferential option for the poor": The foundational principle that God freely chooses to reveal Godself among the poor, requiring the Church's own structures and witness to align with them.
- **Conscientización** (Spanish/Portuguese, from Paulo Freire) — "conscientization / consciousness-raising": The pedagogical process by which the poor critically analyse their own socio-political situation and come to see themselves as active agents of history.
- **Comunidades Eclesiais de Base** (Portuguese (CEBs)) — "Base Ecclesial Communities": Small grassroots Christian communities of the poor practising contextual, lay-led faith — Boff's "ecclesiogenesis," the Church reinventing itself from below.
- **Pueblo Crucificado** (Spanish) — "crucified people": Jon Sobrino's category naming Latin America's exploited and murdered poor as those who, historically, continue to bear Jesus's own passion.

## Connections
Shares West German political theology's critique of the ahistorical bourgeois subject with Moltmann and Metz (Part II.A, Part II.D), though liberationists prioritise concrete praxis over academic fundamental theology. Adapts Rahner's transcendental theology of grace and "anonymous Christianity" (Part I) toward ecumenical openness to non-Christian liberation struggles. Criticised from outside by Schubert Ogden and disciplined institutionally by Joseph Ratzinger's CDF (also central to the Roman Catholic Theology after Vatican II chapter, Part II.D).
