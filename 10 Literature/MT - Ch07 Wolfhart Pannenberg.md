# Ch. 07 — Wolfhart Pannenberg
*Chapter author: Christoph Schwöbel*

## Core Idea
Pannenberg argues revelation is not a direct, vertical "Word" but God's indirect self-revelation through acts in universal history, fully understood only from history's eschatological end, proleptically anticipated in Jesus's resurrection.

## Detailed Explanation
Raised in Germany under the Nazi regime, Pannenberg developed a lifelong drive to secure the public rationality of Christian faith against ideological distortion. He studied under Barthians at Berlin, Göttingen, Basel, and Heidelberg, but discovered in Hegel's philosophy of history a resource Barth himself had refused. Where Barth grounds theology in God's direct Word received in faith, Pannenberg insists theology must argue "from below" — historically and publicly — so that Christian truth-claims can be tested in open dialogue with historical reason and the natural sciences, not sheltered from them. He taught alongside Moltmann at Wuppertal before becoming Professor of Systematic Theology at Munich (1967–1993).

## Example
Pannenberg's claim that Jesus's resurrection is a real, historically-arguable event (not merely a private faith-experience) put him in direct, public debate with historians and philosophers of history throughout his career — an unusually exposed position, since it means Christian faith's central claim is, for Pannenberg, in principle open to historical falsification rather than protected from it.

## Illustration
Reading the last page of a novel first, then re-reading the whole book, changes how every earlier chapter reads — minor details suddenly reveal their purpose. Pannenberg's "revelation as history" works the same way: Jesus's resurrection is history's "last page" arriving early, in advance, letting us read all of history's meaning proleptically, before the ending most of it is still waiting for.

## Key Terms
- **Offenbarung als Geschichte** (German) — "revelation as history": The programmatic claim that revelation comes indirectly, through historical deeds open to universal reason, fully legible only from history's end.
- **Prolepsis** (Greek root) — "anticipation": The mechanism by which history's final end is made retroactively present and effective already in Jesus's resurrection.
- **Christology from Below**: Starting with the historical man Jesus and arguing toward his divinity via the resurrection, rather than starting from the dogma of the Incarnation.
- **Vorgriff** (German) — "pre-apprehension": The pre-reflective human openness to the world and to self-transcendence that implicitly reaches toward God (a term shared with, but developed differently from, Rahner's use).

## Connections
Rejects Barth's model of direct verbal revelation and criticises Barth's Trinity for collapsing the three persons into "modes" of one divine subject. Colleague of Moltmann at Wuppertal, though Pannenberg's historical-rational method contrasts with Moltmann's more dialectical, praxis-oriented path to eschatology. Shares the term Vorgriff with Rahner (Part I) but roots it in universal history rather than transcendental anthropology.
