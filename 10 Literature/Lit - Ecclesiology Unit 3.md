# Unit 3 — Ecclesiologies of the Synoptics and Acts

## No Single Ecclesiologia Perennis
No single, timeless "perennial ecclesiology" (ecclesiologia perennis) exists in the New Testament. Each evangelist articulates the meaning of the community for his own pastoral situation.

## Mark: A Community Defined by Service
Mark's community is defined by service, not status: "whoever wishes to be great among you must be your servant... the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve" (Mk 10:42-45). Mark's disciples are consistently slow to understand, prone to fear and failure — a community of little faith that Jesus nonetheless keeps calling and re-forming. To follow Jesus in Mark is to take up the cross (Mk 8:34).

## Matthew: A Community Fulfilling the Law
Matthew's community fulfils rather than abolishes the Mosaic Law (Mt 5:17) and lives by the "new Torah" of the Sermon on the Mount. It is a mixed body — wheat and weeds together (Mt 13) — awaiting eschatological separation, and it is explicitly missionary (the Great Commission, Mt 28:19-20). Matthew is the only Gospel to use the word ekklesia (16:18; 18:17), in Peter's confession, the "power of the keys," and a community discipline procedure.

## Luke-Acts: The Little Flock, Universal and Spirit-Led
Luke's community is the "little flock" (Lk 12:32), vulnerable and humble. Universality — salvation for Jew and Gentile, centre and periphery — is Luke's programmatic theme, from Simeon's canticle to the Gentile mission in Acts. Luke-Acts has been called the "Gospel of the Holy Spirit": the Spirit is the primary actor driving the Church's expansion, from Pentecost to Philip, Peter, and Paul. Acts shows both emerging order (apostles, the Seven, elders — Acts 6, 15) and continuing charismatic freedom (the Spirit falling on unexpected people, as with Cornelius in Acts 10).

## Illustration
Picture three photographers taking pictures of the same mountain from three different base camps: Mark from the camp of suffering discipleship, Matthew from the camp of Torah and community order, Luke from the camp of mission and the Spirit. None gives the whole mountain; together they triangulate its true shape.
