# Unit 11 — Theological Significance

**1. In persona Christi Capitis.** The ordained acts "in the person of Christ the Head." This is the load-bearing concept, and it does two jobs: it explains how the priest's action is *Christ's* action (so that the sacrament does not depend on the minister's worth — *ex opere operato*), and it explains how a ministerial priesthood does not add to Christ's unique priesthood (Hebrews' *ephapax*), because it is the *same* priesthood made sacramentally present. *Omnium in Mentem* (2009) restricts the phrase to bishops and priests.

**2. Character.** An indelible configuration to Christ as minister, which makes orders **unrepeatable** and explains why laicisation removes the *exercise* but not the ordination itself. Grounded in Augustine's answer to the Donatists.

**3. The tria munera.** Teaching, sanctifying, governing — conferred by consecration (*LG* 21), and mapping onto Christ's own threefold office of prophet, priest, and king, which itself descends from Israel's threefold anointing (Unit 1).

**4. Apostolic succession.** Not a merely mechanical chain of hand-layings but the continuity of the apostolic *mission, faith, and communion*, of which the succession of bishops is the sacramental sign — the formulation that made ecumenical progress possible (BEM; ARCIC).

**5. The relation of the two priesthoods.** The formula *essentia et non gradu tantum* is a genuine achievement of precision, but it is also the tradition's most difficult claim to communicate, because "differs in essence" is heard as "is worth more." *LG* 10's second half — that they are **ordered one to another** — is the necessary corrective and is routinely dropped in popular presentation.

**6. Ordination is for a community.** Chalcedon c. 6's ban on absolute ordinations, surviving as **incardination**, encodes the principle that no one is ordained to a personal dignity. The Reformation attacked the benefice system for exactly the failure of this principle.

**7. Sacrifice and priesthood stand or fall together.** As Unit 3 shows historically and Trent Session XXIII states doctrinally ("since sacrifice and priesthood are by the ordinance of God so united"), the sacerdotal theology of orders is a corollary of the sacrificial theology of the Eucharist.
