# Unit 1 — Biblical Foundations I — Priesthood in Israel and the Priesthood of Christ

## Priesthood in Israel

Three streams feed the later Christian doctrine, and they do not simply converge.

**The Levitical/Aaronic priesthood** (Exod 28-29; Lev 8-9) is hereditary, tribal, and defined by **cultic function**: offering sacrifice, teaching *torah*, and discerning through Urim and Thummim. It is not a personal charism but a caste office.

**The Melchizedek tradition** (Gen 14:18; **Ps 110:4** — "You are a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek") supplies a priesthood that is *not* Levitical, without genealogy, combining priesthood and kingship. Hebrews will make this the whole basis of Christ's priesthood — precisely because it bypasses Levi.

**The priestly people.** **Exodus 19:6** — "you shall be to me **a kingdom of priests** and a holy nation." This is addressed to *all Israel*, not to a caste, and it is the Old Testament root of what 1 Peter and later Vatican II will call the **common priesthood**. Any treatment of Holy Orders that forgets this text will get the relation of ordained to baptised wrong from the start.

To these add the **threefold anointing** of Israel — **prophet, priest, king** — which the tradition will apply first to Christ and then, in the *tria munera*, to the ordained.

## The Priesthood of Christ in Hebrews

Hebrews is the New Testament's only sustained priestly theology, and its argument is radical.

- Christ is **high priest after the order of Melchizedek** (Heb 5:6-10; 7:1-28), *not* of Levi — and the author notes pointedly that Jesus "belonged to another tribe... Judah, and in connection with that tribe Moses said nothing about priests" (7:13-14).
- His priesthood is **unique and non-transferable**: *aparabatos* (7:24), "permanent," a term that can mean untransferable — he holds his priesthood permanently because he continues for ever.
- His sacrifice is ***ephapax*** — **once for all** (7:27; 9:12; 10:10). This is the text on which the entire Reformation objection to the sacrificing priesthood will rest.

**The exegetical problem this creates, which you must be able to state.** Hebrews argues that Christ's priesthood *replaces* and *ends* the old cultic priesthood. It never suggests a continuing order of Christian priests. So a Catholic theology of Holy Orders cannot simply appeal to Hebrews — it must explain how a ministerial priesthood participates in Christ's one priesthood **without adding to it or repeating it**. That explanation is the concept of *in persona Christi* (Unit 11), and it is doing genuinely heavy lifting.

## The Twelve and the Last Supper

The Gospels present Jesus **choosing the Twelve** (Mk 3:13-19) — an act of deliberate constitution, the number signifying the twelve tribes and thus the reconstituted Israel. To them he gives authority to preach, heal, and cast out demons; at the Last Supper, ***"Do this in remembrance of me"*** (Lk 22:19; 1 Cor 11:24-25); after the resurrection, the commissions of **Matthew 28:19-20** and **John 20:21-23** ("As the Father has sent me, so I send you... whose sins you forgive").

Trent will read *"Do this"* as instituting the priesthood alongside the Eucharist. The honest exegetical note: the phrase is a command to *repeat the rite*, and its extension to the ordination of a priestly order is a theological inference the tradition draws rather than a datum the text states.
