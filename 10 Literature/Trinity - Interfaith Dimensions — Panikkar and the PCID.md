# Part 8 — Interfaith Dimensions — Panikkar and the PCID

## Raimon Panikkar's Cosmotheandric Vision

*The Trinity and the Religious Experience of Man* proposes spiritual experience, not abstract doctrinal formulas, as the more fruitful path into interfaith dialogue, defining spirituality as "one typical way of handling the human condition." Panikkar maps three distinct spiritualities onto the three trinitarian persons:

- **The Father / Apophaticism** — the silent, self-emptying apophatic dimension, classically manifest in the Buddhist experience of *nirvāna*; the divine is "utterly other," so silence is the purest response; aligns with asceticism and apophatic mysticism.
- **The Son / Personalism** — religions of personal divine revelation — Judaism, Islam, Christianity — mediating between God and humanity through devotion, love, mercy, and reconciliation; Panikkar reads Christ's *kenosis* through Buddhist *sunyata*, and locates theistic (*bhakti*) Hinduism here too.
- **The Holy Spirit / Immanence** — the "immanent dimension," found in Advaita Hinduism's non-dual union with the Absolute, and in Shaivite *Śakti* and Tantric Buddhist traditions of inner *kundalini* energy.

He names the scheme **"cosmotheandric"**: the three modalities are mutually corrective pointers toward a single divine mystery, making the world's religions "complementary paths to the cosmotheandric reality."

**Gavin D'Costa's critique:** Panikkar's model represents "an inversion of the *vestigia [Trinitatis]* tradition" — instead of other religions containing partial reflections of the Christian Trinity, Panikkar implies Christianity itself possesses only partial vestiges of a broader cosmotheandric reality. Consequence: "the symbolic triadic structure subordinates the historical particular narrative of revelation."

## Risto Jukko: The PCID and Christian-Muslim Trinitarian Dialogue

*Trinity in Unity in Christian-Muslim Relations* examines the Pontifical Council for Interreligious Dialogue (PCID). Rather than treating the Trinity as an obstacle in interreligious dialogue, the Magisterium treats the triune God as the "clear theological foundation" and only viable basis for it. The historic friction with Islam: Muslims regard the Trinity as violating divine unity (*shirk*); Christians find Islamic *tawhid* theologically incomplete because it excludes Christ.

- **The "Two Hands of God":** following the patristic image of Son and Spirit, the PCID never separates their economic work; the Spirit, as "the Spirit of Christ," is active saving both Christians and non-Christians, playing "an analogous role within other cultures and religions... helping them... to become more Christ-like."
- **Clarifying true monotheism:** the three Persons "do not diminish divine unity but characterize it" — one identical essence, one understanding, one will — "explicitly rejects any form of tritheism or polytheism."
- **The Trinity as archetype of dialogue:** because the triune God is himself "the mystery of communion and dialogue," it becomes the ultimate model for Christian relations with non-Christians, fulfilling Augustine's maxim: "you see the Trinity if you see the charity" (cf. Part 3, Gioia).
- **Theology of interreligious prayer:** reflecting on the 1986 Assisi Day of Prayer — "everywhere and at all times we are all 'guests of the Divine'" — the Spirit as "living breath of prayer," always anchored Christologically to the paschal mystery.

## Divergent Uses of the Trinity in Interfaith Engagement

Panikkar relativizes Christian particularity beneath a deeper triadic pattern common to all religions; the PCID insists a fully Trinitarian orientation actually *safeguards* Christian particularity while enabling genuine openness — warding off "theocentric" exclusivism, "Christomonist" reduction, and "pneumatological" reduction. The PCID reads other religions as potential *praeparatio evangelica* and ***semina Verbi*** ("seeds of the Word") — fragmentary echoes of the Trinity's prior cosmic activity — without collapsing the historical particularity Panikkar's model relativizes.
