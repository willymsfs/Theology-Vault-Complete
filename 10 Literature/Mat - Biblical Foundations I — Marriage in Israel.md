# Unit 1 — Biblical Foundations I — Marriage in Israel

## The Two Creation Texts

**Genesis 1:27-28** — "God created humankind in his image... male and female he created them. And God blessed them and said, 'Be fruitful and multiply.'" Marriage here is set within the *imago Dei* and oriented to fruitfulness. Note that the image is predicated of humanity *as male and female*, a point the personalist tradition (Unit 9) would develop heavily.

**Genesis 2:18-24** — the older and richer account. Three phrases carry the weight:

- ***lo tov*** — "**It is not good** that the man should be alone." The only thing declared *not good* in a creation account otherwise pronounced good.
- ***'ezer kenegdo*** — usually rendered "a helper fit for him," but the Hebrew is stronger and more mutual than English "helper" suggests: *'ezer* is used overwhelmingly of **God** as Israel's help (Ps 121:2), so it carries no connotation of subordination; *kenegdo* means "corresponding to, opposite him" — a counterpart who stands face to face.
- ***basar echad*** — "they become **one flesh**," which Jesus and Paul will both take as the decisive text.

The verse also states the social principle: "a man **leaves** his father and mother and **cleaves** (*dabaq*) to his wife" — remarkable in a patrilocal society, where in practice the woman left her family.

## Marriage as Covenant Image

The prophets make marriage the **governing metaphor for the covenant** between YHWH and Israel — and they do so at its most painful:

- **Hosea 1-3** — the prophet commanded to marry Gomer and to take her back after infidelity. God's fidelity is figured precisely as a *wronged spouse who does not divorce*.
- **Jeremiah 2-3; Ezekiel 16 and 23; Isaiah 54:5-8** develop the same figure, with Isaiah's "your Maker is your husband."
- **Malachi 2:14-16** — the wife is "your **companion** and your **wife by covenant** (*berith*)," and God "hates divorce" (or, on an alternative reading of a difficult text, "he who divorces covers his garment with violence").

**Teaching point.** The covenant metaphor runs *both* ways, and this is where the sacramental theology of Ephesians 5 begins: because marriage is used to explain the covenant, the covenant can be used to explain marriage.

## The Complicating Evidence

An honest treatment states what the Old Testament also contains:

- **Polygamy** among the patriarchs and kings, never explicitly condemned in narrative.
- **Levirate marriage** (Deut 25:5-10) — a brother's duty to raise offspring for the deceased.
- **Divorce permitted** — **Deuteronomy 24:1-4** presupposes it, on the ground of the husband finding ***'erwat dabar***, "some indecency," in his wife. The phrase is genuinely obscure, and by the first century it had produced the two rabbinic schools that frame the Gospel debate: **Shammai** restricting it to sexual immorality, **Hillel** extending it to almost any displeasure.

**Tobit** deserves mention for its portrait of marriage as prayerful and companionate: Tobias and Sarah pray before consummation — "I now am taking this my beloved to wife, not because of lust, but with sincerity" (Tob 8:7) — a text used in the wedding liturgy for centuries.
