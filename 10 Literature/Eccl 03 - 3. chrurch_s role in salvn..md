# 3. chrurch_s role in salvn..pdf

**TITLE**: The Church: Its Role In God's Plan Of Salvation. By Fr. Dr. P. N. Wachege (Senior Lecturer, Department of Religious Studies, University of Nairobi, Kenya) [62]. Published in *African Ecclesial Review* (AFER), Vol. 39, No. 1, February 1997, pages 28–50 [63, 64].

**MAIN SUBJECT**: The role of the Church as the authorized agent of salvation, catechizing, and evangelization, analyzed through major historical shifts in theology, the collapse of exclusive axioms, Karl Rahner's "Anonymous Christianity," and the "Family of God" model proposed by the 1994 African Synod [63, 65, 66].

**KEY TOPICS Covered**:
*   The Church as the Sacrament of Salvation (Kung, Schillebeeckx, de Lubac, Dulles) [62, 64].
*   Small Christian Communities (SCCs) as the basic pastoral priority in Eastern Africa (AMECEA) [65, 67].
*   The model of the Church as the "Family of God" endorsed by the 1994 African Synod [65, 66, 68].
*   The prominent, salvific position of the Blessed Virgin Mary in Catholic tradition [69].
*   The historical and dogmatic evaluation of the exclusive axiom "Outside the Church, No Salvation" (*Extra Ecclesiam Nulla Salus*) [70, 71].
*   The debate surrounding Karl Rahner’s "Anonymous Christianity" and its critics (Küng, Boublik) [72, 73].
*   Jacques Dupuis's four progressive historical stages of Church attitudes toward other religions [74, 75].
*   The objectives and theological instructions of the 1994 African Synod [67, 76].

**CONCEPTS EXPLAINED**:
1.  **Family of God**: The central ecclesiological model proposed by the 1994 African Synod, emphasizing solidarity, communal care, warm interpersonal relationships, and an understanding of salvation as a shared, collective journey rather than an individualistic concern [65, 66, 68, 77].
2.  **Extra Ecclesiam Nulla Salus**: The traditional, exclusive theological axiom ("Outside the Church, No Salvation") championed by St. Cyprian and Fulgentius which historically condemned all those outside the visible Catholic fold to "eternal fire" [70, 78].
3.  **Anonymous Christianity**: Karl Rahner's pastoral theory asserting that because God's salvific will is universal, individuals who respond to God's grace in their conscience are saved in Christ "anonymously" without needing explicit Christian symbols, although explicit Christianity remains the most direct path of revelation [176–178, 181].
4.  **Anonymous Catechumenate**: A concept proposed by theologian V. Boublik as an alternative to Rahner's theory, describing the lifelong desire of adherents of other religions to encounter the Savior, which critics find less presumptuous [73].
5.  **Synodos**: A Greek-derived word meaning "journeying together" (from *syn* and *hodos*), used to challenge the African Church to walk collaboratively toward redemption as a family [67, 79].

**ISSUES ADDRESSED**:
1.  **The Ruthlessness of Exclusive Dogmas**: Overcoming the historical harm caused by exclusive dogmas like *Extra Ecclesiam Nulla Salus* to foster healthy ecumenical and interreligious dialogue [70, 74].
2.  **The Presumption of "Anonymous" Labeling**: Addressing Hans Küng's sharp critique that labeling a sincere Muslim, Jew, or atheist an "anonymous Christian" is a presumptuous, imperialistic "theological sleight of hand" that shuts down genuine interreligious dialogue [183–185].
3.  **The Historical Condemnation of African Culture**: Overcoming the colonial legacy where missionaries condemned African religions, cultures, and ancestral philosophies as "savage" or "primitive" without critically examining their positive salvific elements [80, 81].
4.  **The Pitfall of Family Paternalism**: Preventing the "Family of God" model from devolving into excessive paternalism or maternalism, which can cause members to remain spiritually immature, irresponsible, and overly dependent on the collective [82].

**IMPORTANT QUOTES**:
*   "The Church is the community of those who have committed themselves to the Person and cause of Jesus Christ and who bear witness to it as hope for all men and women." (Page 28 / Passage 159) - Context: Citing Hans Küng's definition of the Church.
*   "Within this Family framework, therefore, one may not just talk of 'my Saviour', 'my salvation', 'I-myself-am saved'. Instead, one should talk of 'our Saviour', 'our Salvation', 'we-ourselves-are saved'." (Page 30 / Passage 165) - Context: Urging a transition from individualistic Western views of salvation to a communal, family-centered African theology.
*   "The axiom, 'Outside the Church, No Salvation' no longer applies. Christianity and other religions have salvific elements which... do convey Salvation." (Page 39 / Passage 189) - Context: Explaining the drastic shift in Catholic dogmatic theology toward general revelation.
*   "To bring the partner to the discussion into our own circle in this way closes the dialogue before it has even begun." (Page 38 / Passage 185) - Context: Quoting Hans Küng's objection to Karl Rahner's "Anonymous Christian" terminology as patronizing to non-Christians.
*   "Our communion in the Church, our walking together, is not our initiative or our doing. We are people called together by our Father" (Page 45-46 / Passage 203) - Context: Quoting Cecil McGarry on the synodal, Trinitarian roots of the Church's nature.

**SUMMARY**:
Wachege analyzes the Church’s essential role as the primary agent of salvation in a world characterized by changing generations and religious pluralism [63, 65]. He outlines a massive theological shift within Catholicism from the exclusive, historical condemnation of non-Catholics under the *Extra Ecclesiam Nulla Salus* axiom to a highly optimistic, positive attitude toward other religions [70, 75]. While presenting Karl Rahner’s "Anonymous Christianity" as an important, comfort-giving pastoral theory, he highlights Hans Küng's strong warnings against its potential theological imperialism [72, 83, 84]. Grounding his vision in the 1994 African Synod, Wachege champions the "Family of God" model and Small Christian Communities (SCCs) as the most effective, culturally authentic vehicles for African inculturation, evangelization, and liberating dialogue [65, 67, 68].
