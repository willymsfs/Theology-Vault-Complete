# Unit 12 — The Ecumenical Dimension

## Apostolicae Curae (Leo XIII, 13 September 1896)

Declared Anglican ordinations **"absolutely null and utterly void"** (*irritas prorsus fuisse et esse, omninoque nullas*), on two grounds:

- **Defect of form** — the Edwardine Ordinal's essential words failed to signify definitely the grace and power of the priesthood as an order for consecrating and offering the true Body and Blood.
- **Defect of intention** — the Ordinal's composers deliberately removed sacrificial language, evidencing an intention not to do what the Church does.

## Saepius Officio (1897) — The Anglican Reply

The Archbishops of Canterbury and York responded with an unusually sharp historical argument: if the standard applied to the Edwardine Ordinal were applied to the ancient Roman rite, that rite would also fail, since it too lacks explicit sacrificial specification in its consecratory prayer. In effect: *you are judging us by a criterion your own tradition does not meet.*

**The state of the question today.** Two developments have complicated *Apostolicae Curae* without reversing it:
1. **Sacramentum Ordinis (1947)** removed the *traditio instrumentorum* from the essential rite — weakening one line of the original argument.
2. **Old Catholic participation** in Anglican episcopal consecrations since the Bonn Agreement (1931) has introduced lines of succession Rome regards as valid, raising the question of whether the original defect has been materially remedied.

Against this, the **ordination of women** in most Anglican provinces has, from Rome's perspective, created a **new** obstacle — which is why *Apostolicae Curae* is now rarely the practical centre of the discussion.

## ARCIC and BEM

- **ARCIC, *Ministry and Ordination* (Canterbury, 1973)** with its 1979 Elucidation claimed "substantial agreement" on the doctrine of ministry, treating ordination as sacramental, conferring a permanent gift by prayer and the laying on of hands, and the ministry as *representative* of Christ to the community.
- **BEM (Lima, 1982)**, ministry section, achieved convergence on the **threefold pattern** as a legitimate development to which divided churches might return, and reframed **apostolic succession** as the succession of the apostolic *tradition* of which episcopal succession is a sign — the formulation that allowed non-episcopal churches to engage the question at all.

## The Orthodox Position

Catholic and Orthodox mutually recognise each other's orders as valid. The remaining differences are jurisdictional and ecclesiological (papal primacy) rather than sacramental, and the Eastern discipline on married clergy (Unit 4) is fully accepted by Rome as legitimate.
