# Unit 7 — Vatican II and the 1972 Reform

## Sacrosanctum Concilium 73 — The Decisive Text

> "'**Extreme unction**,' which may also and more fittingly be called '**Anointing of the Sick**,' is not a sacrament for those only who are at the point of death. Hence, as soon as any one of the faithful **begins to be in danger of death from sickness or old age**, the fitting time for him to receive this sacrament has certainly already arrived."

Three moves in two sentences:

1. **The name changes** — and the Council says the new name is *more fitting* (*aptius*), a quiet judgement that the medieval name had distorted the theology.
2. **The negative correction** — "not for those only who are at the point of death."
3. **The threshold is set at the *beginning* of danger**, from **sickness or old age** — the latter being an important extension, since old age is not an illness.

**SC 74** orders a **continuous rite** in which anointing is conferred after confession and before viaticum. **SC 75** directs that the number of anointings be adapted and the prayers revised to fit the varied conditions of the sick.

**LG 11** supplies the ecclesial theology: "By the sacred anointing of the sick and the prayer of her priests, **the whole Church commends those who are ill to the suffering and glorified Lord**, that he may raise them up and save them. And indeed she exhorts them to contribute to the good of the People of God by **freely uniting themselves to the passion and death of Christ**." Note the two directions — the Church acts *for* the sick, and the sick contribute *to* the Church. The sick person is an agent, not merely a recipient.

## Sacram Unctionem Infirmorum (Paul VI, 30 November 1972)

The Apostolic Constitution promulgating the reform. Its decisions:

- **The essential rite** is reduced to anointing the **forehead and the hands** (from the medieval anointing of the five senses, loins, and feet). In case of necessity, a single anointing on the forehead — or any suitable part of the body — suffices.
- **The matter** is olive oil or, where necessary, **another plant oil** — a significant practical concession for regions where olive oil is unavailable.
- **The new form**, replacing the medieval formula:

> *"Through this holy anointing may the Lord in his love and mercy help you with the grace of the Holy Spirit. **Amen.** May the Lord who frees you from sin save you and raise you up. **Amen.**"*

Observe how closely the second sentence tracks **James 5:15** (*sōsei... egerei* — save and raise up). The reform's method was, once again, *ressourcement*: go behind the medieval development to the scriptural text.

## The Rite (Ordo Unctionis Infirmorum, 1972)

The English-language edition is titled ***Pastoral Care of the Sick: Rites of Anointing and Viaticum***, and the title itself signals the reform's intent — the sacrament sits within an ongoing *pastoral care*, not a single emergency intervention. The book provides for visits to the sick, communion of the sick, anointing (within or outside Mass, individually or communally), viaticum, and the commendation of the dying.

## Canonical Norms (CIC 1983, cc. 998-1007)

| Canon | Provision |
|---|---|
| **c. 998** | Definition: for the faithful who are dangerously ill from sickness or old age |
| **c. 1003** | Every priest, and only a priest, validly administers it |
| **c. 1004 §1** | May be given to one who, **having reached the use of reason**, begins to be in danger from sickness or old age |
| **c. 1004 §2** | May be **repeated** if the person recovers and again falls ill, or if the danger becomes graver |
| **c. 1005** | In **doubt** whether the person is ill enough, has died, or has the use of reason — **administer it** |
| **c. 1006** | To be given to those who requested it at least implicitly when they were in control of their faculties |
| **c. 1007** | **Not** to be conferred on those who obstinately persist in manifest grave sin |

**CCC 1499-1532** gives the systematic treatment, and **CCC 1514-1515** repeat the pastoral broadening: before surgery, in serious illness, in the frailty of old age, and repeatedly through a long illness.
