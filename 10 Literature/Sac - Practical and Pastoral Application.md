# Unit 12 — Practical and Pastoral Application

## In the Indian Context

- **Baptism and caste.** Galatians 3:27-28 — "no longer slave or free" — collides directly with the persistence of caste among Christian communities. George Soares-Prabhu's judgement is uncompromising: caste in the Church makes the Eucharist "a sacrilege" and "parody." The same logic applies with full force to baptism: *if the font does not dissolve caste, what exactly did it do?* This is the most urgent practical application of baptismal theology in South Asia.
- **Baptism and family rupture.** In a context where conversion can mean loss of family, inheritance, and legal scheduled-caste benefits, the catechumenate's ancient function — *slow formation with a supporting community* — is not antiquarianism but pastoral necessity.
- **Inculturation.** *SC* 65 explicitly permits elements of local initiation practice. Michael Amaladoss's caution is well taken: before creating an Indian rite there must first be "a minimum of psycho-sociological research on life situations and living symbols" — inculturation must begin from real living symbols, not from a scholar's desk.

## Common Pastoral Scenarios

| Scenario | Response |
|---|---|
| Emergency baptism of a dying infant | **Anyone** may baptise, including a non-Christian, using water plus the Trinitarian form plus intention to do what the Church does (CIC c. 861 §2) |
| Doubt whether a person was validly baptised | **Conditional** baptism: "If you are not baptised, I baptise you..." (c. 869) |
| A convert baptised in a Trinitarian Protestant church | **Not** rebaptised — received by profession of faith and confirmation (Trent Sess. VII can. 4; *UR* 3) |
| A convert baptised in a non-Trinitarian body | Baptism **invalid** — baptise absolutely |
| Baptised with "We baptise you" | **Invalid** (CDF 2020) — baptise absolutely, and review all subsequent sacraments |
| Parents want baptism but show no sign of practising | c. 868 §1 2° requires "founded hope" of Catholic upbringing; defer, do not refuse — pastoral accompaniment, not gatekeeping |

## Preaching and Catechesis

The strongest single pastoral move available is Luther's: teach people to say ***"Baptizatus sum"*** — "I **am** baptised," present tense. Most Catholics treat baptism as something that *happened* to them as an infant, a fact of biography. The whole tradition — patristic, scholastic, and Reformation alike — treats it as a standing identity that can be *returned to* daily. A parish that succeeds in shifting that one verb tense has done more baptismal catechesis than a dozen preparation courses.
