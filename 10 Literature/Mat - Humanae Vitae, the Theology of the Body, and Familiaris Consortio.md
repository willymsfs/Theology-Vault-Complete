# Unit 9 — Humanae Vitae, the Theology of the Body, and Familiaris Consortio

## The Birth Control Commission and Humanae Vitae (1968)

John XXIII established a commission on population and birth control in 1963; Paul VI expanded it. Its **majority report** (1966) argued that the regulation of conception by other means could be consistent with the "totality" of the marriage; a **minority report** argued that reversing the constant teaching would undermine magisterial authority itself.

Both were leaked and published. **Paul VI decided against the majority.**

***Humanae Vitae*** (25 July 1968) rests on a single principle stated at **HV 12**: there is an "**inseparable connection, established by God, which man on his own initiative may not break, between the unitive significance and the procreative significance** which are both inherent to the marriage act." From this follows the exclusion of contraception (HV 14) while permitting recourse to infertile periods (HV 16).

**The reception crisis was the gravest of the post-conciliar period.** Numerous episcopal conferences issued statements — the Canadian bishops' Winnipeg Statement being the best known — emphasising the role of conscience in a way widely read as softening the teaching. Theological dissent was public and organised. Whatever one's judgement of the doctrine, the *pastoral* fact is that it precipitated a large-scale, quiet disengagement from the Church's sexual teaching among Western Catholics, and — as noted in the Penance course — from the confessional as well.

## The Theology of the Body (John Paul II, 1979-84)

129 Wednesday catecheses constituting the most substantial papal treatment of the subject ever produced. Its principal ideas:

- **The nuptial meaning of the body** — the human body has an inherent capacity to express self-giving love, and is thus a *sign* of the person's vocation to communion.
- ***Communio personarum*** — the human person is fulfilled in the mutual self-gift of persons, an image of the Trinity's own communion (connecting directly to the Trinity course).
- **Original solitude, unity, and nakedness** — a phenomenological reading of Genesis 2-3.
- **The language of the body** — the marital act *speaks*, and contraception introduces a falsification into that language: the body says "I give myself totally" while the act withholds fertility.

**Assessment.** The Theology of the Body is a genuine achievement, offering a positive personalist grounding for a teaching that had rested largely on natural-law argument. Its critics note that it is a work of poetic phenomenology rather than of argument, that it does not directly engage the majority report's reasoning, and that its reception has been strongest among those already convinced.

## Familiaris Consortio (1981)

The post-synodal exhortation following the 1980 Synod on the family. Three contributions to master:

1. **The family's four tasks**: forming a community of persons, serving life, participating in the development of society, and sharing in the life and mission of the Church.
2. **The law of gradualness** (FC 34) — a careful distinction: there is a "law of gradualness" in the sense that people advance step by step toward the good, but **not a "gradualness of the law"** as if the moral norm admitted degrees. This distinction returns at the centre of the *Amoris Laetitia* debate.
3. **The divorced and remarried** (FC 84) — they remain members of the Church and should be encouraged to participate in its life, but may not receive communion, since their state "objectively contradicts" the union of Christ and the Church; unless they can live "as brother and sister."
