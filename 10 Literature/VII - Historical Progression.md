# Part 1 — Historical Progression

## Pre-Conciliar Background (the "Long Nineteenth Century" to 1958)

Catholic officialdom emerged from the French Revolution in a defensive posture that persisted for roughly a century. **Pope Pius IX's Syllabus of Errors (1864)** crystallized this stance, condemning modern liberties and secular culture. **Vatican I (1869-70)** defined papal primacy and *ex cathedra* infallibility in *Pastor Aeternus*, but was suspended by the Franco-Prussian War and the fall of Rome before it could address the episcopate or laity — leaving Catholic ecclesiology one-sidedly centralized for nearly a century.

Beneath this defensive surface, several renewal currents gathered strength:
- **The Liturgical Movement** — Dom Prosper Guéranger's 1833 re-founding of Solesmes fed into Pius XII's *Mediator Dei* (1947) and the Holy Week reforms of 1951-55.
- **Ressourcement** (see Part 4) — de Lubac, Daniélou, Chenu, Congar.
- **Transcendental Thomism** — Joseph Maréchal (1922), developed by Karl Rahner.
- **The Lay Apostolate** (Catholic Action) — expanding under Pius XI and Pius XII, though tightly clerically controlled.
- **The Ecumenical Movement** — tentatively explored despite Pius XI's prohibition in *Mortalium Animos* (1928).

## Announcement, Preparation, and Convocation (1958-1962)

Elected 28 October 1958 at age 76 — widely expected to be transitional — **John XXIII** stunned the Church on **25 January 1959**, announcing an ecumenical council at St Paul Outside-the-Walls.

- **Ante-Preparatory Phase** (17 May 1959 - 5 June 1960): Cardinal Tardini's commission solicited *vota* from bishops worldwide — over **2,800 proposals**, most requesting condemnation of Communism and new Marian definitions.
- **Preparatory Phase** (5 June 1960 - 11 October 1962): *Superno Dei Nutu* established ten Curia-dominated preparatory commissions. John XXIII also created the **Secretariat for Promoting Christian Unity (SPCU)** under **Cardinal Augustin Bea**, bypassing normal Curial channels.
- **25 December 1961:** *Humanae Salutis* formally convokes the Council.
- **11 September 1962:** John XXIII's radio address frames the dual task — *ad intra* and *ad extra*.

## Session I (11 October - 8 December 1962): "The Lines Are Drawn"

Opened with ***Gaudet Mater Ecclesia*** ("Mother Church Rejoices"), rejecting the "prophets of gloom," declaring the Council's Magisterium **pastoral**, calling for "aggiornamento."

- **13 October 1962 — Commission Elections Revolt:** Cardinals Liénart and Frings moved to postpone elections so national conferences could compile independent candidate lists — breaking Curial control at the very first working session.
- **20-21 November 1962 — Rejection of *De Fontibus Revelationis*:** 1,368 of 2,209 fathers (62%) voted to halt debate — short of the two-thirds threshold, but John XXIII personally overrode the rules the next day and ordered a rewrite by a joint commission (Ottaviani + Bea).
- **4-6 December 1962 — Structural Pivot:** Cardinal Suenens proposed restructuring around *ad intra*/*ad extra*; the **Coordinating Commission** was established to prune roughly seventy schemata to seventeen.

No documents were promulgated in Session I, but a coherent progressive majority had crystallized.

## Intersession and the Transition of Pontificates (1963)

The nine-month "second preparation" saw the Coordinating Commission drastically revise the surviving schemata. John XXIII privately established the **Papal Commission on Birth Control** (March 1963) and published his final encyclical, ***Pacem in Terris*** (11 April 1963).

John XXIII died **3 June 1963**. **Giovanni Battista Montini** was elected **Paul VI** on **21 June 1963**, immediately committing to continue the Council, and appointed **four Cardinal Moderators** — Suenens, Döpfner, Lercaro, Agagianian.

## Session II (29 September - 4 December 1963): "A Majority Prevailing"

Paul VI's opening allocution set a fourfold agenda: the Church's self-awareness, internal renewal, Christian unity, dialogue with the modern world.

- **30 October 1963 — Collegiality Straw Votes:** Over three-quarters of fathers affirmed episcopal consecration confers the fullness of orders and the episcopal college holds supreme divine authority in union with the Pope.
- Draft chapters on ecumenism (Jewish-Catholic relations, religious liberty) proved so controversial they were bracketed off for later sessions.
- **4 December 1963:** First two documents promulgated — ***Sacrosanctum Concilium*** and *Inter Mirifica*.

## Session III (14 September - 21 November 1964): "Triumphs and Tribulations" — including *Black Week*

Paul VI welcomed the first **women lay auditors** in conciliar history. The session closed with the notorious **"Black Week" (16-21 November 1964)** — see Part 6 for full detail — in which the Pope, seeking to placate the conservative *Coetus Internationalis Patrum*, personally imposed the *Nota Praevia* on collegiality, postponed the religious-liberty vote, inserted nineteen unvoted amendments into the ecumenism decree, and unilaterally proclaimed Mary "Mother of the Church."

Three documents were promulgated on **21 November 1964**: ***Lumen Gentium***, ***Unitatis Redintegratio***, and *Orientalium Ecclesiarum*.

## Session IV (14 September - 8 December 1965): "Bringing the Ship into Port"

Paul VI opened by establishing the permanent (but consultative) **Synod of Bishops** via *Apostolica Sollicitudo*. Eleven remaining documents were promulgated across three Public Sessions:
- **28 October 1965 (5 documents):** *Nostra Aetate*, *Christus Dominus*, *Perfectae Caritatis*, *Optatam Totius*, *Gravissimum Educationis*.
- **18 November 1965 (2 documents):** ***Dei Verbum***, *Apostolicam Actuositatem*.
- **7 December 1965 (4 documents):** ***Dignitatis Humanae***, *Ad Gentes*, *Presbyterorum Ordinis*, ***Gaudium et Spes***.

Paul VI withdrew **priestly celibacy** and **artificial contraception** from floor debate entirely, reserving both to himself. On **7 December 1965**, Paul VI and Ecumenical Patriarch Athenagoras I simultaneously lifted the mutual excommunications of **1054**. The Council formally closed **8 December 1965**.
