# Ch. 10 — Anglican Theology
*Chapter author: Peter Sedgwick*

## Core Idea
Twentieth-century English Anglican theology cultivated a "wisdom theology" — prayer, liturgy, and patristic retrieval over rigid system-building — rooted in the incarnational, kenotic legacy of Lux Mundi (1891), later radically challenged by a sharper critique of the secular social order.

## Detailed Explanation
Anglican theology began the century holding an established, culturally privileged position in a rapidly secularizing and class-divided nation, shaped by Bishop Charles Gore, William Temple, and later Michael Ramsey's socially engaged "incarnational" theology. Mid-century brought a radical liberalizing turn — John A. T. Robinson's bestselling Honest to God (1963) and Don Cupitt's later "non-realist" theology — followed by a more rigorous philosophical and historical retrieval led by Donald MacKinnon, Stephen Sykes, Daniel Hardy, and Rowan Williams, who pushed Anglican theology to reclaim the tragic, conflictual dimensions of Jesus's story against a too-comfortable establishment self-image. Stephen Sykes's The Identity of Christianity is the sharpest internal critique: Anglican "comprehensiveness" (holding Anglo-Catholic, Evangelical, and Liberal wings together) risks becoming, he argued, a polite cover for simple incoherence.

## Example
John A. T. Robinson's Honest to God (1963) — a bishop's paperback that became an unexpected national bestseller — is the clearest real-world instance of this chapter's story: it popularised Bonhoeffer's "religionless Christianity" and Tillich's "ground of being" for ordinary British readers, provoking a public debate about God-language that went far beyond academic theology.

## Illustration
Anglican "comprehensiveness" is like a large extended family gathered at one table who disagree sharply about politics and religion but still insist on eating dinner together every week rather than splitting into separate households. Sykes's critique is the question every such family eventually faces: is staying at one table a genuine, hard-won unity, or just an unwillingness to admit the disagreements are serious?

## Key Terms
- **Wisdom Theology**: A style of theology oriented to finding the right path for a national church culture, working through essays and liturgy rather than comprehensive dogmatic systems.
- **Anglican Comprehensiveness**: Holding Anglo-Catholic, Evangelical, and Liberal traditions together within one church body; criticised by Stephen Sykes as sometimes covering for a lack of theological definition.
- **Kenotic Identification** (Greek root: kenosis, self-emptying): God's self-emptying solidarity with human tragedy and historical suffering, authorising the Church's active social and political engagement.
- **Acoluthetic Reason** (Greek root: akolouthein, to follow): Reason understood as "following after" and being shaped by the contingent, tragic pattern of Jesus's actual life, rather than reasoning independently of it.

## Connections
Stephen Sykes urged Anglicans toward Barth's intellectual rigour and self-definition; Donald MacKinnon and Daniel Hardy (also the author of the Barth and Torrance chapters) engaged deeply with Barth. Moltmann's "solidarity of God with suffering" strongly shaped 1970s-80s Anglican social theology, though Rowan Williams worried it risked sacralizing the status quo. Robinson's Honest to God fused Bonhoeffer and Tillich (both Part I) and drew on Martin Buber's I-Thou philosophy (the Buber companion volume).
