# Unit 12 — Theological Significance — Systematic Synthesis

Four inseparable dimensions. A common exam error is to treat these as competing emphases; they are aspects of one reality.

**1. Presence.** Christ is present *substantially* under the species — but also, per **SC 7**, in the minister, the word, the sacraments, and the praying assembly. The Eucharistic presence is unique in *mode*, not the only presence.

**2. Sacrifice.** Not a repetition of Calvary but its sacramental re-presentation: same victim, same priest, different manner of offering (Trent XXII), understood through *anamnesis* in the Hebraic sense.

**3. Communion.** *Koinōnia* with Christ and, inseparably, with one another (1 Cor 10:16-17). The *res tantum* of the sacrament is the unity of the Body — which is why Paul's rebuke about "discerning the body" is simultaneously sacramental and social.

**4. Eschatology.** "Until he comes" (1 Cor 11:26); the *pignus futurae gloriae*, pledge of future glory. The Eucharist is a foretaste of the Isaiah 25 banquet, which is why the liturgy is joyful and not merely solemn.

## The Trinitarian Structure

Every anaphora is addressed **to the Father**, **through the Son**, **in the Holy Spirit**. The epiclesis makes the Spirit's role explicit; the doxology states the structure outright. This maps precisely onto the doctrine of inseparable operations from the Trinity course: the Eucharist is a single divine act in which the persons are distinguished by their proper mode of action, not divided.

## The Paschal and Incarnational Logic

Ignatius's anti-Docetic argument still holds: **Eucharistic realism is a corollary of Incarnational realism**. A God who takes flesh is a God who can give himself under the species of bread. Conversely, Irenaeus's argument runs forward: if matter can bear Christ now, matter can be raised then. The Eucharist sits at the exact centre of a materialist — in the good sense — Christian faith.
