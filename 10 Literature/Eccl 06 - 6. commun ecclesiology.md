# 6. commun ecclesiology.pdf

**TITLE**: "Journet, Congar, and the Roots of Communion Ecclesiology." By Dennis M. Doyle (Ph.D., Catholic University of America; Associate Professor at the University of Dayton). Published in *Theological Studies*, Vol. 58, 1997, pages 461–479.

**MAIN SUBJECT**: A comparative analysis of two preconciliar theologians, Charles Journet and Yves Congar, tracing how their respective metaphysical/mystical and historical/ecumenical approaches formed the foundational roots of modern "communion ecclesiology" at the Second Vatican Council.

**KEY TOPICS**:
*   The definition of communion ecclesiology as a relationship-centered approach to the Church.
*   The historical significance of Johann Adam Möhler’s seminal 1825 work *Unity in the Church*.
*   Charles Journet's three-volume systematic treatise *L'Église du Verbe incarné* (1941–1969).
*   The application of Aristotelian causality (formal, material, efficient, final causes) to ecclesiology.
*   The structural distinction and unity between the "juridical Church" and the "Church of charity."
*   Yves Congar's ecumenical and historical starting point in *Chrétiens désunis* (1939).
*   Congar’s theological exploration of lay participation, charisms, and structural reform.
*   The dogmatic dispute concerning the "sinlessness" vs. the "purification" of the Church.
*   The distinct pneumatological contributions of both theologians to the "formal cause" of the Church.

**CONCEPTS EXPLAINED**:
1.  **Communion Ecclesiology**: A theological approach that begins with the concept of "communion" (*koinonia*) among God and human beings, applying this analogously to sacraments, ministry, ecumenism, and the relationship between the Church and the world (Page 461).
2.  **Aristotelian Causality of the Church**: A systematic framework used by Journet and preconciliar manuals to describe the Church's dimensions: its formal cause (Holy Spirit/hierarchy), material cause (laity), remote efficient cause (Trinity), immediate efficient cause (Christ), and final cause (beatific vision) (Page 465–466).
3.  **The Church of Charity**: A concept distinguishing the spiritual community of grace and love from the legal, outer administration, asserting that while both form one interlocked reality, the juridical structures are strictly subordinated to the service of charity (Page 466–467).
4.  **Creeping Infallibility**: A warning conceptualized by Congar regarding the growing preconciliar tendency to treat the ordinary, day-to-day teaching of the hierarchy as absolute and unchangeable, thereby suppressing authentic dialogue and historical development (Page 472).
5.  **Quasi-Formal Cause**: Congar's definition of the Holy Spirit's role in the Church, where the Spirit acts as an internal, dynamic principle that indwells and shapes the Church without composing its essence in a way that ignores human agency or historical limits (Page 473).

**ISSUES ADDRESSED**:
1.  **The Overcoming of Preconciliar Juridicism**: Overthrowing dry, mechanical manualist apologists who presented the Church solely as an unequal, hierarchically organized juridical society (Page 464–465).
2.  **Balancing Transcendent Ideals with Concrete History**: Bridging the tension between an abstract, "sinless" ideal of the Church's inner mystery and the messy, historically conditioned realities of its daily operations (Page 462).
3.  **The Debate on the Church's Sinfulness**: Resolving whether the Church as such can be said to sin (Journet’s view that only its members sin, leaving the Church's essence untouched, vs. Congar's view that the Church is in constant need of historical purification and repentance) (Page 477–478).
4.  **Clerical Hierarchy vs. Active Lay Agency**: Confronting the passive status of laypeople in traditional manuals, resulting in Congar's retrieval of the priesthood of all believers and the structural necessity of lay-led ministries (Page 471–472).

**IMPORTANT QUOTES**:
*   "Congar's emphasis on historical development is complemented by Journet's focus on the mystical and essential." (Page 461 / Editor's Note) - Context: Setting up the comparative framework of the two theological tracks.
*   "In him [Möhler] I found a source, the source, which I needed." (Page 463 / Footnote 9, quoting Congar) - Context: Describing the profound ecumenical and historical influence of Johann Adam Möhler's early romantic ecclesiology on Congar's career.
*   "The formal cause of the Church is the Holy Spirit. To the hierarchy he assigned the role of immediate efficient cause." (Page 466) - Context: Outlining how Journet modified Neo-Scholastic Aristotelian categories to center the Church on the Spirit.
*   "The hierarchy, for Journet, is fundamentally a service to help promote the mystical communion." (Page 467) - Context: Explaining that Journet sought to subordinate legal power to the theological life of grace.
*   "Present structural arrangements were not to be taken as absolutes." (Page 472) - Context: Describing Congar's radical insistence on the historical conditioning and mutability of church offices.
*   "The Mystical Body is realized once our life belongs to Christ." (Page 474, quoting Congar) - Context: Illustrating how Congar connected systematic ecclesiology directly with concrete, lived spirituality.

**SUMMARY**:
Dennis M. Doyle compares the preconciliar ecclesiologies of Charles Journet and Yves Congar to prove that the "communion ecclesiology" of Vatican II emerged from two distinct but complementary lines of reform. Journet worked within a speculative, Neo-Scholastic Thomistic framework, using the philosophy of causality to redefine the formal cause of the Church as the Holy Spirit, thereby shifting theological focus from rigid juridical manualism to an interior, mystical communion. Congar, by contrast, pursued a historically conscious, ecumenical methodology, integrating a dynamic Pneumatology that viewed the Church as an unfinished historical pilgrim in need of structural reform, lay activation, and purification. Doyle concludes that while Journet provided the necessary metaphysical defense of the Church's transcendent holiness, Congar provided the historical realism needed to engage the modern world, making both thinkers essential for a holistic, non-reductive Catholic spirituality.
