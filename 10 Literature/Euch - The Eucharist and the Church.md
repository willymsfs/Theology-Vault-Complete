# Unit 11 — The Eucharist and the Church

## De Lubac's Reversal

**Henri de Lubac's *Corpus Mysticum* (1944)** is one of the twentieth century's great works of historical theology, and its central finding is a genuine surprise.

Before roughly the twelfth century, the terms were used as follows:

| Term | Patristic and early medieval referent |
|---|---|
| ***corpus verum*** ("true body") | **The Church** |
| ***corpus mysticum*** ("mystical body") | **The Eucharist** — the body in the mode of mystery/sacrament |

In the wake of the Berengarian controversy, and the resulting need to stress the Eucharistic realism, the terms **exchanged places**: *corpus verum* migrated to the Eucharist, and *corpus mysticum* came to name the Church.

**Why it matters.** De Lubac argues the swap had a cost: the Eucharist ceased to be primarily *the sacrament of the Church's unity* and became an object of individual devotion and defence, while the Church became an abstract "mystical" reality rather than the visible fruit of the sacrament. His famous formula reverses the drift: ***"The Eucharist makes the Church"*** as much as the Church makes the Eucharist. This thesis lies behind *Lumen Gentium*, behind communion ecclesiology, and behind almost all subsequent Catholic sacramental ecclesiology.

## Eucharistic Ecclesiology in the East

**John Zizioulas** (*Being as Communion*) grounds the local church's full ecclesial reality in the Eucharistic assembly gathered around its bishop: wherever the Eucharist is celebrated, there is the whole Church, not a fragment of it. This is the same argument that generates his "monarchy of the Father" Trinitarian ontology and — as Miroslav Volf argues — the asymmetry of his ecclesiology (see the Trinity course, Units 6-7). **The Eucharist is where Zizioulas's Trinitarian theology and his ecclesiology meet.**

## Magisterial Reception

- **LG 3, 7, 11, 26** — the Eucharist as the sacrament by which "the unity of the Church is both signified and brought about"; every legitimate local celebration makes Christ present and gathers the Church.
- **1985 Extraordinary Synod** — *communio* named the central idea of Vatican II's ecclesiology.
- ***Ecclesia de Eucharistia*** (2003) — "the Church draws her life from the Eucharist."

## Intercommunion

The hardest practical consequence. The Catholic position (UR 8; CIC c. 844) holds Eucharistic sharing to be a **sign of a unity already achieved** rather than a *means* toward unity, and so ordinarily reserved to those in full communion — with defined exceptions for Eastern Orthodox and, in danger of death or grave necessity, other properly disposed Christians whose churches the Holy See judges to have valid sacraments.

**The counter-argument**, pressed by many ecumenists and some Catholic theologians, is that the Eucharist is also *medicine* and *means* — Christ ate with sinners before they were reformed — and that the discipline makes a sacrament of unity into a marker of division. This is a genuinely open pastoral debate, and a good essay states both sides at full strength.
