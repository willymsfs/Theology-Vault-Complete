# Unit 8 — From Trent to Vatican II — and Sacramentum Ordinis

## Vatican I and the Pressure on Episcopacy

**Pastor Aeternus** (1870) defined papal primacy of jurisdiction and infallibility. Because the Council was suspended before it could treat the episcopate, the result was an **ecclesiological imbalance**: a fully articulated theology of the papacy standing beside an undeveloped theology of the bishop. Completing that unfinished business is exactly what *Lumen Gentium* ch. 3 sets out to do — which is why collegiality was the Council's fiercest battleground (see the Vatican II and Ecclesiology courses).

## Pius XII, Sacramentum Ordinis (30 November 1947)

The most important pre-conciliar document on this sacrament, and a model of magisterial correction.

**The problem.** Florence (1439) had said the matter of priestly ordination was the *traditio instrumentorum* — the handing over of chalice and paten. But historical scholarship had established beyond doubt that this was a **medieval Western innovation**, absent from the East entirely and from the West's first millennium, where the essential act had always been the **imposition of hands**.

**The ruling.** Pius XII declared, *"by Our Apostolic Authority and from certain knowledge,"* that:

- **The matter** of the ordination of deacons, priests, and bishops is the **imposition of hands alone**.
- **The form** consists of the words of the consecratory preface that determine the effect — and he specified precisely which words in each rite.
- The *traditio instrumentorum* is **not required for validity**.

**Two lessons for essays.** First, this is a clean instance of the magisterium *correcting* an earlier magisterial text in light of historical evidence — and the correction runs in the direction of the more ancient and more ecumenically shared practice. Second, it removed at a stroke one of the two pillars of *Apostolicae Curae*'s argument against Anglican orders (Unit 12), since the Edwardine Ordinal's omission of the *traditio* could no longer count against it.

## The Theological Ferment

- **Mystici Corporis** (1943) and **Mediator Dei** (1947) both had to address the **priesthood of the faithful**, affirming it while insisting it differs from the ministerial priesthood — laying the groundwork for *LG* 10's formula.
- **Yves Congar's** *Jalons pour une théologie du laïcat* (1953) required a corresponding theology of the ordained defined by *service* rather than by status over against the laity.
- The **liturgical movement** recovered the ancient ordination prayers, and the **worker-priest experiment** in France (1943-54, suppressed, later partially revived) posed sharply the question of what a priest *is* when detached from parochial cult.
