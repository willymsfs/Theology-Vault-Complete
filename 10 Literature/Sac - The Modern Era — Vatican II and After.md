# Unit 5 — The Modern Era — Vatican II and After

## The Liturgical Movement and Vatican II

***Sacrosanctum Concilium*** (4 Dec 1963) legislates the reform:

- **SC 64** — "**The catechumenate for adults, comprising several distinct steps, is to be restored** and brought into use at the discretion of the local ordinary."
- **SC 65-66** — revision of both the adult and simple rites; permission for elements of local initiation practice to be admitted (**a direct opening for inculturation**).
- **SC 67-69** — revision of the rite for infants, "to reflect the fact that they are infants."
- **SC 70** — baptismal water may be blessed within the rite outside Eastertide.

***Lumen Gentium*** supplies the ecclesiology:

- **LG 10-11** — baptism confers the **common priesthood of the faithful**, from which all lay apostolate flows. This is the same structural move as placing "People of God" before "Hierarchy": **baptismal dignity is prior to office.**
- **LG 14-16** — the three concentric circles of belonging.

***Unitatis Redintegratio* 3 and 22** — the ecumenical breakthrough: baptism "constitutes a **sacramental bond of unity** linking all who have been reborn by it." Those baptised outside full communion are "truly incorporated into Christ" and are rightly called Christians.

## The Reformed Rites

| Document | Date | Content |
|---|---|---|
| ***Ordo Baptismi Parvulorum*** (Rite of Baptism for Children) | 15 May 1969 | First rite in history composed specifically *for infants*, with real roles for parents |
| ***Ordo Initiationis Christianae Adultorum*** (OICA / RCIA) | 6 Jan 1972 | Restores the patristic catechumenate |

**The RCIA structure** — four periods punctuated by three thresholds:

| Period | Threshold rite that ends it |
|---|---|
| **Precatechumenate** (inquiry, evangelisation) | **Rite of Acceptance** into the Order of Catechumens |
| **Catechumenate** (formation, often a year or more) | **Rite of Election / Enrolment of Names** (First Sunday of Lent) |
| **Purification and Enlightenment** (Lent; **scrutinies** on Lent III, IV, V; *traditiones*) | **Celebration of the Sacraments of Initiation** at the Easter Vigil |
| **Mystagogy** (Easter to Pentecost) | Integration into ordinary parish life |

**Crucially, RCIA restores the ancient unity: for adults, Baptism, Confirmation, and Eucharist are celebrated together, in that order, at the Vigil, by the same minister.** Hold onto this — it makes the modern "Confirmation problem" visible.

| **BEM**, *Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry* (Lima) | **WCC Faith and Order, 1982** | The most important ecumenical text; gives **five images**: participation in Christ's death and resurrection; conversion, pardoning, cleansing; gift of the Spirit; incorporation into the body of Christ; sign of the Kingdom |
| ITC, *The Hope of Salvation for Infants Who Die Without Being Baptised* | **2007** | Concludes there are "serious theological and liturgical grounds for hope" — limbo is a *theological hypothesis*, never doctrine, and is set aside |
| CDF, *Responsa* on the formula "**We** baptise you" | **6 Aug 2020** | Declared **invalid**; such baptisms must be repeated absolutely. The minister speaks *in persona Christi* — "**I** baptise" — the community does not baptise itself |

**Teaching point.** The 2020 *Responsum* is the best modern illustration of *ex opere operato*. Well-meaning ministers substituted "we" to stress community; Rome's reply was that this obscures the fact that **Christ is the agent**. Hundreds of Catholics in the USA discovered their baptisms — and consequently their confirmations, marriages, and in a few cases ordinations — were invalid. Doctrine has consequences.
