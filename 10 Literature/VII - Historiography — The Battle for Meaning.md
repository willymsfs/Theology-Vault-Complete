# Part 7 — Historiography — The Battle for Meaning

## The Two Foundational Positions

**The Bologna School** (Giuseppe Alberigo, Giuseppe Ruggieri) reads the Council as a historical **"event"** whose "spirit" is the true interpretive key, emphasizing genuine **discontinuity** with the "long nineteenth century." Produced the standard five-volume *History of Vatican II*.

**Pope Benedict XVI's "hermeneutic of reform in continuity"** (22 December 2005 Curia address): true reform combines continuity *and* discontinuity at different levels, within the life of a single "subject-Church"; rejects reading the Council as a "constituent assembly."

## Other Major Positions

- **SSPX/Lefebvrian rejection** — agrees the Council was a rupture, condemns it as heretical capitulation to modernity.
- **Küng's "betrayed revolution"** — the Council's promise was fatally weakened by Curial compromise.
- **The neoconservative narrative** (Weigel, Neuhaus, Novak) — an "Americanist" reading centred on religious freedom.
- **Matthew Lamb and Matthew Levering** (*Vatican II: Renewal within Tradition*) — the clearest "continuist" camp, rejecting rupture-readings outright.

## John W. O'Malley, SJ — Form-Analysis

O'Malley's thesis (*What Happened at Vatican II*; *"Vatican II: Did Anything Happen?"*) is distinct from both Bologna and Ratzinger: the Council's **most undeniable discontinuity is its literary genre and vocabulary**, not its propositional content. Prior councils (Nicaea to Vatican I) issued **canons** ending in *anathema*; Vatican II issued **not a single anathema**, adopting the classical **epideictic (panegyric) genre** — persuasion, praise, invitation. Vocabulary sorts into *horizontal/reciprocity words* ("People of God," "dialogue"), *humility words* ("pilgrim," "servant"), *openness words* ("development," "aggiornamento"). Governing formula: ***verba et res* cannot be separated — style is itself doctrine.** Proposes a **"seventh interpretive norm"** supplementing the 1985 Synod's six (below): interpreters must also account for genuine discontinuity. His foils are **Cardinal Camillo Ruini** and **Archbishop Agostino Marchetto**; he defends Alberigo by comparing his rigor to Hubert Jedin's classic history of Trent.

## Thomas G. Guarino — Analogical Reasoning and "Masking"

*The Disputed Teachings of Vatican II* argues the Council's core teachings represent authentic **homogeneous development** (Vincent of Lérins's *profectus*) — but honestly names **"bare reversals"** of prior *ordinary* magisterial teaching (e.g. reversing Pius XII's *Mystici Corporis* on episcopal jurisdiction; reversing Leo XIII on Protestant evangelization). Guarino's distinctive claim: the Council **"masked"** these reversals rather than admitting them openly. Locates the key innovations in **analogical, participatory reasoning** — non-Catholics *participate* imperfectly in truths fully realized in Catholicism.

## Other Historiographical Voices

- **Piotr H. Kosicki** — Eastern European Catholics under Communism were **not** a passive "Church of Silence"; John XXIII's council opened space for active local reception of *aggiornamento* even under totalitarian constraint.
- **Austen Ivereigh** (ed., *Unfinished Journey*) and **Richard Gaillardetz** (*An Unfinished Council*) — borrow Hermann Pottmeyer's image of an **"unfinished building site."** Gaillardetz's **seven pillars** for completed reception: (1) kerygmatic/Trinitarian theology of revelation, (2) dialogical engagement, (3) priority of baptism, (4) renewed pneumatology, (5) episcopal collegiality, (6) missionary Church, (7) pilgrim-people image.
- **David Tracy, Hans Küng, Johann B. Metz** (eds., *Toward Vatican III*) — a prospective reform agenda: Tracy calls for disciplined retrieval amid global pluralism; Küng pushes structural papal reform; Metz calls for "mystical-political radicalization" shifting the Church's centre to the Global South.

## The 1985 Extraordinary Synod's Six Official Interpretive Principles

1. Each passage must be read in the context of the whole conciliar corpus.
2. The **four Constitutions** are the primary hermeneutical key to the decrees and declarations.
3. Pastoral import must never be separated from, or opposed to, doctrinal content.
4. No opposition may be made between the "spirit" and the "letter" of the Council.
5. The Council must be read in continuity with the great tradition and prior ecumenical councils.
6. The Council must be accepted as illuminating the problems of our own day.

*(O'Malley's "seventh norm" is best taught as a direct response to principle 5.)*

## Systematic Reception Theory

- **Ormond Rush's reception triad:** (1) hermeneutics of the **author** (the conciliar event and intent), (2) hermeneutics of the **text** (internal, intertextual logic), (3) hermeneutics of the **receiver** (how local churches actually understand and apply the texts over time).
- **Gilles Routhier:** reception is an **active, creative** process — local, inculturated churches must organically make the conciliar gifts their own.

## The Institutional "Scale-Back" Narrative

1980s-90s institutional reception actively narrowed several conciliar themes under Cardinal Ratzinger's influence: *The Ratzinger Report* (1985); "People of God" losing official momentum; John Paul II's *Apostolos Suos* (1998) curtailing episcopal conferences' doctrinal authority.

## A Clean Four-Phase Chronology of Contested Reception

1. **Acknowledged, Received, Refused (1965-1980):** early commentary from *periti*, not bishops; theological unanimity fractures fast — *Humanae Vitae* (1968) and the defeated *Lex Ecclesiae Fundamentalis* proposal make Rome wary of implementation; Lefebvre's SSPX rejects the Council outright.
2. **Celebrated and Enforced (1980-1990):** debate shifts to **Roman doctrinal policy** under John Paul II and CDF Prefect Ratzinger (from 1981); the 1985 Synod's six principles arrive alongside *The Ratzinger Report*, the 1989 profession of faith, and the trajectory toward *Apostolos Suos*.
3. **Historicized (1990-2000):** Alberigo's Bologna school produces the five-volume *History of Vatican II*.
4. **Toward a New Fight (2005-present):** Benedict XVI's election reignites debate — the CDF's 2007 clarification of *subsistit in*, *Summorum Pontificum* (2007), and the 2009 lifting of the Lefebvrian bishops' excommunications together read as a Roman "attitude review."

**Faggioli's added warnings:** the Council's status as the first truly global "World Church" council makes interpretive drift especially dangerous; the 24/7 media/blogosphere cycle risks reducing the debate to "gossip" or partisan jargon; as the generation that lived through the Council passes away, the rising generation risks inheriting only a politically flattened caricature.

## The Contemporary Popular Debate

**Bishop Robert Barron's "middle path"** narrates the *Concilium*/*Communio* split with unusual clarity: pre-conciliar thought was dominated by "hyper-rationalistic, deductive, culturally defensive manualist Thomism" (Garrigou-Lagrange); *ressourcement* "won a resounding victory" — but its victors split almost immediately. **Concilium camp** (Küng, Schillebeeckx, Rahner) sought "permanent suspense" pushing further reform under an open "spirit of the Council." **Communio camp** (de Lubac, Ratzinger, von Balthasar) resigned, accusing Concilium of constituting a "rival magisterium." Barron blames Vatican II's evident struggles in the West on the sexual revolution and poor implementation, not the documents — citing Nicaea's sixty-year path to triumph through the Arian crisis as precedent for patience.

**The traditionalist counter-critique** (*The Chasm of Ressourcement*) argues four points: (1) *communicatio in sacris* loosened Eucharistic discipline; (2) a "dilution of hell" and evangelistic urgency (citing Ralph Martin's critique of von Balthasar's hopeful universalism); (3) the 1986 Assisi interreligious gathering as the troubling logical endpoint of conciliar openness; (4) Newman's development-of-doctrine concept misused as cover for illegitimate novelty.

**John Paul II's own apologia** (*Unity and Truth*, on Assisi) answers directly: religious division is a consequence of the **Fall**, not acceptable plurality; *Nostra Aetate*'s "rejects nothing true and holy" coexists with an unequivocal reassertion that **Christ is the sole fountain of grace**; dialogue does not replace or exclude proclamation; Assisi involved parallel, not syncretistic, prayer.
