# Unit 9 — The Liturgical Movement and Vatican II

## Precursors (1830s-1950s)

| Figure / Act | Date | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Prosper Guéranger** | 1833 | Refounds Solesmes; recovery of the Roman liturgy and Gregorian chant |
| **Pius X**, *Tra le Sollecitudini* | 1903 | Coins the phrase **"active participation"** (*partecipazione attiva*) as the primary source of the Christian spirit |
| **Pius X**, *Sacra Tridentina* | 1905 | Encourages **frequent, even daily, communion** — reversing centuries of infrequent reception |
| **Pius X**, *Quam Singulari* | 1910 | First Communion at the age of discretion |
| **Lambert Beauduin** | 1909 | Malines address launching the pastoral Liturgical Movement |
| **Odo Casel** | 1920s-30s | ***Mysteriengegenwart*** — "mystery presence": the saving acts of Christ are made really present in the liturgical mystery. The single most influential theological idea behind the reform |
| **Pius XII**, *Mystici Corporis* | 1943 | Ecclesiology of the Body of Christ |
| **Pius XII**, *Mediator Dei* | 1947 | The first papal encyclical on the liturgy; endorses the Movement's aims while restraining its excesses |
| **Holy Week reform** | 1951-1955 | Restored Easter Vigil to the night; the reform that proved large-scale change was possible |

## Sacrosanctum Concilium (1963)

The principles a BTh student should be able to cite by number:

- **SC 7** — Christ is present in the liturgy in **four modes**: in the person of the minister, in the Eucharistic species, in the sacraments, in his word, and when the Church prays and sings. *This is doctrinally significant*: it situates the Eucharistic presence within a broader theology of presence without diminishing it.
- **SC 10** — the liturgy is "the **summit** toward which the activity of the Church is directed" and "the **fount** from which all her power flows" (cf. **LG 11**, "source and summit of the Christian life").
- **SC 14** — ***participatio actuosa***, "full, conscious, and active participation," is "the aim to be considered before all else."
- **SC 47-48** — the Eucharist as paschal banquet and sacrifice; the faithful should not be "strangers or silent spectators."
- **SC 55** — communion from hosts consecrated *at that Mass*; communion under both kinds permitted in defined cases.
- **SC 56** — the two parts, Word and Eucharist, "form but one single act of worship."
- **SC 57** — restoration of **concelebration**.

## The Missal of Paul VI (1969/1970)

The chief structural changes: the vernacular; a three-year Sunday lectionary vastly expanding the Scripture read; **four Eucharistic Prayers** replacing the sole Roman Canon (Prayer II based on the *Apostolic Tradition*, Prayer IV on Antiochene/Eastern models); a clearly articulated **epiclesis**; the restored **Prayer of the Faithful**; simplification of accretions; and the priest facing the people (a practical change, never actually mandated by the Council's text).

## Mysterium Fidei (Paul VI, 3 Sept 1965)

Issued *during* the Council to check speculative drift. It insists that **transubstantiation names an ontological change**, and that terms like *transignification* and *transfinalization* (Unit 10) are inadequate **if offered as substitutes** rather than as complements — because a change of meaning and purpose presupposes, and cannot replace, a change of being.

## Later Magisterial Documents

| Document | Date | Note |
|---|---|---|
| *Ecclesia de Eucharistia* (John Paul II) | 2003 | "The Church draws her life from the Eucharist"; strong on the Eucharist-Church relation and on intercommunion discipline |
| *Redemptionis Sacramentum* (CDW) | 2004 | Disciplinary instruction against abuses |
| *Sacramentum Caritatis* (Benedict XVI) | 2007 | Post-synodal exhortation; the three-fold structure *mysterium credendum / celebrandum / vivendum*; develops ***ars celebrandi*** |
| *Summorum Pontificum* (Benedict XVI) | 2007 | Wider permission for the 1962 Missal as "extraordinary form" |
| *Traditionis Custodes* (Francis) | 2021 | Substantially restricts that permission, affirming the reformed rite as "the unique expression of the *lex orandi* of the Roman Rite" |
| *Desiderio Desideravi* (Francis) | 2022 | Letter on liturgical formation |
