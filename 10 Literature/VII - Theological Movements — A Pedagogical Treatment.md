# Part 4 — Theological Movements — A Pedagogical Treatment

Three interlocking currents dismantled the neo-scholastic manualist paradigm and reshaped modern Catholic identity: **Ressourcement**, **Nouvelle Théologie**, and **Aggiornamento**.

## Ressourcement

**Define it.** French, "return to the sources" (Latin *ad fontes*). A method of renewal bypassing recent scholastic manuals to draw directly from Scripture, the early liturgies, and the Greek and Latin Fathers.

**Origins.** Two nineteenth-century developments made it practically possible: **Abbé Jacques-Paul Migne's** cheap mass-printing of the *Patrologia Latina* (217 vols, 1844-55) and *Patrologia Graeca* (166 vols, 1857-66), and **Dom Prosper Guéranger's** 1833 re-founding of Solesmes, birthing the liturgical movement.

**Key figures:**
- **Henri de Lubac, SJ** — co-founded the *Sources chrétiennes* series (1942) with Daniélou.
- **Jean Daniélou, SJ** — his 1946 manifesto *"Orientations présentes de la pensée religieuse"* called for theology nourished by Bible, liturgy, and Fathers together.
- **Marie-Dominique Chenu, OP** — argued (1937, *Une école de théologie*) that Aquinas must be read in his own thirteenth-century context, not through later neo-scholastic commentary.
- **Yves Congar, OP** — drawing on Möhler, argued genuine reform comes through return to sources, not rupture (*Vraie et fausse réforme dans l'Église*, 1950).

**Significance for the Council.** Supplied the actual literary and conceptual texture of the major documents: *Lumen Gentium*'s "People of God" and "mystery"; *Dei Verbum*'s single-source revelation model; *Sacrosanctum Concilium*'s recovery of ancient liturgical participation.

**Lasting impact.** Permanently ended the neo-scholastic manual's monopoly on Catholic theological method; remains the shared method underlying otherwise opposed post-conciliar camps (Neo-Augustinian and Neo-Thomist alike draw on it).

## Nouvelle Théologie

**Define it.** Not a self-chosen school name but a **pejorative label** for the *ressourcement* renewal; practitioners preferred "theology of history" or "source theology," and rejected the label as a fictional monster (*la tarasque*).

**Origins.** Coined in 1942 by **Msgr Pietro Parente**, an assessor of the Holy Office, in *L'Osservatore Romano*, justifying placing Chenu's *Une école de théologie* and Louis Charlier's *Essai sur le problème théologique* on the Index that same year — accused of subjectivism, historicism, dogmatic relativism.

**Proponents and opponents.** Proponents: de Lubac, Congar, Bouillard, Chenu. Chief opponent: **Réginald Garrigou-Lagrange**, whose 1946 article *"La nouvelle théologie où va-t-elle?"* concluded it led "straight back to Modernism" — shaping **Pius XII's 1950 encyclical *Humani Generis***.

**Significance for the Council.** The post-*Humani Generis* purge stripped de Lubac, Bouillard, Chenu, and Congar of their teaching posts. John XXIII's convocation **vindicated them directly**: he named these very theologians as conciliar *periti*, and the Council discarded the neo-scholastic preparatory schemata in favor of their historical, biblical, patristic method.

**Lasting impact.** Three of the condemned became cardinals: **Daniélou (1969), de Lubac (1983), Congar (1994)** — one of the most dramatic theological vindications in modern Church history.

## Aggiornamento

**Define it.** Italian for "updating" — John XXIII's own program for the Council. Where *ressourcement* looks backward to the sources, *aggiornamento* looks forward to the present.

**Origins.** Articulated in *Gaudet Mater Ecclesia* (11 October 1962): *"the deposit of faith... is one thing, and the manner in which these truths are enounced is another, always with the same meaning and the same judgment"* (*eodem sensu eademque sententia*).

**In practice:**
- **Liturgical reform:** vernacular replacing Latin.
- **Religious liberty:** abandoning the Constantinian *Staatskirchentum* ideal.
- **Dialogue with modernity:** the "see-judge-act" method (from the Young Christian Workers movement).
- **Ecumenism/interfaith relations:** from *Mortalium Animos*'s prohibition to *Nostra Aetate*'s recognition of "riches of Christ" in other traditions.

**Lasting impact.** The Council's most publicly recognizable — and most contested — legacy word, since the entire post-conciliar hermeneutical war (Part 7) is essentially a dispute over how far "updating" was meant to extend.

## How the Three Fit Together

*Ressourcement* and *aggiornamento* are not competitors: *ressourcement* was the theological **means**, *aggiornamento* the pastoral **end**. The Council's paradox is that its most "modernizing" moves — vernacular liturgy, collegiality, religious freedom — were achieved not by innovating outward from neo-scholasticism but by reaching backward, past it, to a richer and older tradition.

## The Neo-Augustinian / Neo-Thomist Fault Line

Underlying nearly every major debate (most visibly *Gaudium et Spes*) was a scholarly divide: **Neo-Augustinians** (Ratzinger, de Lubac, von Balthasar) saw the Church as an island of grace amid a sinful world, wary of theological optimism about modernity; **Neo-Thomists** (Chenu, Congar, Rahner, Schillebeeckx) favored confident engagement with the "signs of the times." Resolved in *Gaudium et Spes* through a shared **Christological anthropology** (GS 22). This exact fault line re-emerges as the deep structure beneath *Concilium* versus *Communio* (Part 7).
