# Unit 11 — Live Debates and Contested Questions

| Debate | Positions |
|---|---|
| **Infant vs believer's baptism** | Catholic/Orthodox/Lutheran/Reformed: covenant, *oikos* formulae, original sin, prevenient grace, faith of the Church. Baptist/Anabaptist: no NT example of a certainly infant baptism; faith must precede the sign; Tertullian was the first witness *against* the practice |
| **What does Confirmation add?** | If baptism already gives the Spirit, is Confirmation redundant? Answers: *robur* for mission (Aquinas); making explicit the ecclesial-apostolic dimension (Acts 8); the pneumatological completion of a christological beginning |
| **The sequence problem** | Restored Order (Confirmation before Eucharist, restoring antiquity and Eastern practice) vs. Current Practice (Confirmation as adolescent commitment). Note the honest objection to the "maturity" reading: *CCC* 1308 |
| **Ecumenical recognition** | Baptism is mutually recognised across most of Christianity (*UR* 3, 22; BEM 1982) — but rebaptism by some evangelical and Pentecostal bodies remains a live wound |
| **Salvation of the unbaptised** | *Extra ecclesiam nulla salus* (Lateran IV) vs. baptism of desire (Aquinas), *LG* 16, and **ITC 2007** on infants |

## The Deeper Structural Question

Every one of these debates is a variation on a single question: **how tightly does God bind himself to the signs he has given?** Bind too tightly and you get magic, limbo for unbaptised infants, and an anxious pastoral practice of emergency baptism by syringe. Bind too loosely and the sacraments become optional illustrations of a grace available anyway, and the Church's practice loses its urgency. The tradition's answer — *the sacraments are necessary for us, not for God* — is a balance, not a formula, and every era has to strike it again.
