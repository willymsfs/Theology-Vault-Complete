# Ch. 05 — Karl Rahner
*Chapter author: Karen Kilby*

## Core Idea
Rahner's "transcendental" or "anthropological" theology argues every human act of knowing carries a pre-reflective, unthematic openness to the infinite mystery of God (Vorgriff), so that grace is a universal structural feature of human existence and even non-Christians who respond to it in love can be called "anonymous Christians."

## Detailed Explanation
A German Jesuit who studied philosophy under Martin Heidegger at Freiburg, Rahner fused Thomist metaphysics with modern transcendental (Kantian-influenced) method: instead of asking directly "what is God," he asks what conditions must already be true of human knowing for God-talk to be possible at all, and finds that every act of finite knowledge presupposes a prior, unthematic "reaching toward" (Vorgriff) the infinite horizon of Being. A hugely influential peritus at Vatican II, Rahner's optimistic universalism (his claim that grace already, universally, structurally graces all human experience — the "supernatural existential") shaped the Council's more hopeful stance toward other religions and toward the modern world generally, though it drew sharp later criticism, notably from von Balthasar (Chapter 6), for allegedly reducing revelation to a feature of human subjectivity.

## Example
Rahner's "anonymous Christian" theory was tested in real ecumenical and interreligious practice after Vatican II: it supplied much of the theological confidence behind Nostra Aetate's more positive posture toward other religions, and continues to be invoked (and contested) whenever Catholic theologians ask how salvation reaches the billions who have never heard the Gospel preached.

## Illustration
Imagine everyone is born already facing, however dimly, toward a distant mountain (the mystery of God) even before anyone has told them its name. Rahner's claim is that this orientation is simply built into being human — so someone who lives generously and self-sacrificially, without ever hearing the word "Christ," is, in Rahner's account, already turned toward that same mountain, even "anonymously."

## Key Terms
- **Vorgriff** (German) — "pre-apprehension / anticipatory grasp": The unthematic, built-in openness of human reason toward the infinite horizon of Being (God) that underlies every act of finite knowledge.
- **Übernatürliches Existenzial** (German) — "supernatural existential": Rahner's term for grace as a permanent, universal structural modification of human nature itself, not an occasional add-on.
- **Anonymer Christ** (German) — "anonymous Christian": Rahner's controversial claim that a person of sincere love and self-sacrifice, even outside explicit Christian faith, already implicitly accepts the grace of Christ.
- **Transcendental Method**: Investigating the subjective conditions that make human experience of revelation possible, rather than starting directly from revealed content.

## Connections
Fiercely opposed by von Balthasar (next chapter), who judged Rahner's anthropological turn a dilution of Christian particularity; his trinitarian axiom ("the economic Trinity is the immanent Trinity") influenced Moltmann and Pannenberg (Part II.A); his Christology is famously set against Barth's in Bruce Marshall's comparative study Christology in Conflict.
