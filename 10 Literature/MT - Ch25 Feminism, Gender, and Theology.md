# Ch. 25 — Feminism, Gender, and Theology
*Chapter author: Rachel Muers*

## Core Idea
Feminist theology exposes and disrupts the structural sin of patriarchy and the idolatry of an exclusively male God-image, insisting theology is always done from a situated, accountable standpoint — never from a neutral, ungendered viewpoint — and works to reconstruct the divine-human relation around mutuality rather than domination.

## Detailed Explanation
Feminist theology grew alongside "second-wave" feminism as women gained wider access to theological education and ordained ministry in Europe and North America. It was then decisively expanded and challenged by women theologians from Africa, Asia, and Latin America, who criticised the white, middle-class bias of early Western feminist theology and insisted theological method be accountable to concrete struggles against colonial, economic, and patriarchal oppression together, not gender oppression alone. The field spans from critical retrieval of biblical and patristic resources to root-and-branch reconstruction of doctrine — the body, embodiment, and even ecology becoming central theological loci rather than marginal concerns.

## Example
Mary Daly's trajectory from The Church and the Second Sex (1968), a reform-minded critique of Catholic patriarchal structures, to Beyond God the Father (1975), a full "post-Christian" rejection of the male "Sky-Patriarch-Punisher" God, is the volume's clearest real-world case of how feminist critique can move from internal reform to a decisive parting of ways with institutional Christianity altogether.

## Illustration
Imagine a family photograph album where, for generations, only the fathers and sons were ever allowed to be photographed holding the family Bible — the mothers and daughters cooked the meal being blessed, but never appeared in the picture of blessing itself. Feminist theology's basic move is to ask: who has been left out of the photograph of God-language and church authority, and what happens to the picture once they are put back in?

## Key Terms
- **Kyriarchy** (Greek root: kyrios, lord/master): Elisabeth Schüssler Fiorenza's term for the interlocking system of lordship — patriarchal, colonial, and class-based domination together — showing gender is never the only axis of structured inequality.
- **Han-puri** (Korean: 한풀이) — "purging of han (unresolved grief/resentment)": A Korean shamanistic ritual of telling histories of unjust, unavenged suffering, retrieved by Chung Hyun Kyung to describe the Spirit's liberating, healing work among oppressed Asian women.
- **Sensible Transcendental** (French: transcendantal sensible): Luce Irigaray's concept of transcendence achieved through, not despite, concrete sexed embodiment — connected to theologies of the Incarnation.
- **Eros / Erotic Power**: Carter Heyward's reclamation of the erotic as God's own creative, relational work in human relationships, against a patriarchal tradition that marginalized desire.
