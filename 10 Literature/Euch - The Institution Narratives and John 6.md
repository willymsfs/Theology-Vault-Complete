# Unit 2 — The Institution Narratives and John 6

## The Four Accounts

The New Testament preserves **four** accounts of the institution, falling into **two textual traditions**:

| Tradition | Texts | Distinctive features |
|---|---|---|
| **Markan / Matthean** (Palestinian) | Mk 14:22-25; Mt 26:26-29 | "This is my blood of the covenant, poured out for many"; more Semitic; no command to repeat |
| **Pauline / Lukan** (Antiochene) | 1 Cor 11:23-26; Lk 22:14-20 | "This cup is the **new** covenant in my blood"; includes ***"Do this in remembrance of me"*** |

**1 Corinthians 11:23-26 is the earliest written account** (c. AD 54), predating all four Gospels — and Paul introduces it with technical rabbinic language of tradition: *"I received (parelabon) from the Lord what I also delivered (paredōka) to you."* This is a formula for handing on a fixed, pre-existing tradition, not personal reminiscence.

## Exegetical Points a BTh Student Must Master

**1. "This is my body" (*touto estin to sōma mou*).** Joachim Jeremias's classic study argues the Aramaic substratum would have had no copula at all — simply *"This — my body."* This does not by itself settle the Reformation debate (Zwingli read the absence of a verb as licensing "signifies"; Catholics and Lutherans read it as straightforward identification), but any serious treatment must acknowledge that the Greek *estin* renders an Aramaic construction where no verb stood.

**2. *Anamnesis* is not mere recollection.** The Greek *anamnēsis* translates the Hebrew ***zikkaron***, which in the Passover context means an **active memorial that makes the past event present and effective for the present generation** — as the Passover Haggadah says, "in every generation each person must regard himself as though he personally had come out of Egypt." This is the pivot on which the whole Catholic theology of Eucharistic sacrifice turns, and the point on which modern ecumenical convergence has been greatest.

**3. Covenant blood.** "This is my blood of the covenant" quotes **Exodus 24:8** verbatim; "the new covenant in my blood" invokes **Jeremiah 31:31**. The Supper is therefore a covenant-ratification rite.

**4. The eschatological vow.** *"I will not drink again of the fruit of the vine until that day when I drink it new in the kingdom of God"* (Mk 14:25) — every Eucharist is oriented forward, which Paul makes explicit: *"you proclaim the Lord's death **until he comes**"* (1 Cor 11:26).

## John 6 — The Bread of Life Discourse

John has no institution narrative (he gives the footwashing instead), but chapter 6 is the New Testament's most sustained Eucharistic theology.

The discourse moves in stages: the multiplication of loaves (6:1-15), the manna comparison (6:31-33 — "not Moses who gave you the bread from heaven"), the self-identification "**I am the bread of life**" (6:35), and then a decisive shift of vocabulary at 6:51.

**The vocabulary shift is the key exegetical observation.** From 6:54 onward John abandons the ordinary verb for eating (*esthiō*) and uses ***trōgein*** — a blunt, physical verb meaning "to gnaw, munch, chew." And he switches from *sōma* (body) to ***sarx*** (flesh). The realism intensifies exactly where the audience objects — and Jesus does not soften it. The result is defection: *"This is a hard saying; who can listen to it?"* (6:60), and "many of his disciples drew back."

**Teaching point.** John 6 is the strongest scriptural warrant for Eucharistic realism precisely because the evangelist *escalates* the offensive language at the moment when a symbolic clarification would have retained the crowd.

## Eucharist in the Rest of the New Testament

- **Luke 24:13-35 (Emmaus)** — "he was known to them in **the breaking of the bread**." Word first, then sacrament: the shape of the Mass in narrative form.
- **Acts 2:42, 46** — "the breaking of bread" as one of the four constitutive marks of the earliest community.
- **1 Cor 10:16-17** — "The cup of blessing... is it not a *koinōnia* in the blood of Christ?... Because there is one bread, we who are many are one body." **The Eucharist makes the Church** — de Lubac's thesis in embryo.
- **1 Cor 11:17-34** — the Corinthian abuses. Paul's rebuke turns on **"discerning the body"** (11:29), which he has just used ecclesially: to eat unworthily is to fail to recognise both the sacramental body and the assembled poor. The social and the sacramental are inseparable.
