# Unit 13 — Practical and Pastoral Application

## The Indian and Asian Context

- **Caste and the Eucharist.** This is the decisive pastoral issue in South Asia. **George Soares-Prabhu**'s judgement is uncompromising: caste practised within the Church makes the Eucharist "a **sacrilege**" and a "**parody**." The theological argument is airtight and is Paul's own: if the one bread makes the many one body (1 Cor 10:17), then separate queues, separate cemeteries, and separate chapels do not merely sit awkwardly beside the Eucharist — they contradict what the sacrament states. Note that Paul's rebuke at Corinth was itself about **social division at the table** (1 Cor 11:20-22), making this the most exactly parallel New Testament situation available.
- **Table fellowship as precedent.** Jesus's practice of eating with tax collectors and sinners dismantled purity boundaries; Soares-Prabhu treats the table fellowship as an "acted parable" of the Abba experience. The Eucharist stands in continuity with that practice, not apart from it.
- **Inculturation.** **Michael Amaladoss**'s methodological caution applies here as to initiation: adaptation must begin from "psycho-sociological research on life situations and living symbols," not from a scholar's design. The Indian Rite debates (the 1969 "Twelve Points," the Indian Anaphora) are the concrete test case.
- **The FABC** has developed a Eucharistic vision centred on the family and the laity in Asia, integrating the triple dialogue with cultures, religions, and the poor.

## Common Pastoral Scenarios

| Scenario | Response |
|---|---|
| Coeliac communicant | Low-gluten hosts are permitted; **gluten-free hosts are invalid matter** (bread must be made of wheat). Communion under the species of wine alone is the standard solution |
| Alcoholic priest or communicant | *Mustum* (grape juice whose fermentation has been suspended) is permitted with the ordinary's permission |
| Communion for the sick and dying | **Viaticum** is the proper name and has priority; it is the sacrament of the dying, "food for the journey" |
| Extraordinary ministers | Genuinely *extraordinary* — supplementary to ordinary ministers (bishop, priest, deacon), per *Redemptionis Sacramentum* |
| A person in an irregular marital situation requests communion | Requires pastoral accompaniment and discernment (cf. *Amoris Laetitia* ch. 8 and the debate it generated); not a matter to be settled at the communion rail |
| A non-Catholic Christian presents themselves | See the intercommunion discussion in Unit 11; CIC c. 844 governs |
| Eucharistic fast | One hour before communion (water and medicine excepted); the sick and their carers are dispensed |

## Catechesis and Preaching

The recurring pastoral failure is to teach the Eucharist as *object* rather than as *act* — the host in the tabernacle rather than the assembly's participation in Christ's self-offering. Both are true, and *Sacramentum Caritatis* deliberately holds them together with its threefold *mystery to be believed, celebrated, and lived*. The practical test Chrysostom set still applies: a parish that adorns the altar while ignoring the hungry has not understood the sacrament, however orthodox its doctrine of the presence.
