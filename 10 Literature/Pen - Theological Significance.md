# Unit 12 — Theological Significance

**1. Christological.** The sacrament continues Jesus's own ministry. The scribes' question at Mark 2:7 — "who can forgive sins but God alone?" — is answered not by denying the premise but by asserting that the Son of Man forgives *on earth*, visibly, through a human agent. Sacramental confession is the extension of that visibility.

**2. Pneumatological.** John 20:22 binds the power to forgive to the gift of the Spirit, in a scene deliberately echoing the creation of Adam. Forgiveness is **new creation**, not merely the cancelling of a debt.

**3. Ecclesial.** *LG* 11: reconciliation with God *and* with the Church. Sin wounds the body; the *res et sacramentum* of penance is precisely *reconciliatio cum Ecclesia*. This is why the sacrament is celebrated with a minister of the Church rather than in private prayer alone, and why Rite II exists.

**4. Anthropological and medicinal.** The two governing metaphors — **tribunal** and **hospital** — both have deep roots (the juridical from canonical penance and Trent, the medicinal from the penitentials' *contraria contrariis sanantur* and Lateran IV's "skilled physician"). Neither alone is adequate: a purely juridical account produces scrupulosity, a purely therapeutic one dissolves the reality of guilt.

**5. Eschatological.** Purgatory, temporal punishment, and satisfaction locate the sacrament within a larger account of purification that does not end at death.

**6. On the seal.** The ***sigillum confessionis*** is, in Catholic teaching, **absolute and without exception** — the confessor may not reveal what he has heard for any reason whatever, and may not even use the knowledge in a way that could disadvantage the penitent. *CIC* c. 983 §1 declares it "**inviolable**"; c. 1386 §1 imposes *latae sententiae* excommunication reserved to the Apostolic See for direct violation. There is no provision for dispensation, and no exception for danger to life or for civil law. Its theological ground: what is said in confession is said **to God**, the priest hearing it not as a man but as God's minister — he genuinely does not "know" it as ordinary human knowledge.
