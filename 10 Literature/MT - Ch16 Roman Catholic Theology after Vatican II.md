# Ch. 16 — Roman Catholic Theology after Vatican II
*Chapter author: Paul D. Murray*

## Core Idea
Vatican II (1962–65) shifted Catholic theology from a deductive, static Neo-Scholasticism into a diverse, historically conscious discipline in creative tension between revisionist/progressive correlation (the journal Concilium) and postliberal/confessional tradition-retrieval (the journal Communio).

## Detailed Explanation
The Council transformed Catholicism into a genuine "world church," carrying forward the pre-conciliar nouvelle théologie (de Lubac, Part I) and transcendental Thomism (Rahner, Part I) into a newly pluriform landscape. Edward Schillebeeckx (Belgian Dominican) developed a "negative contrast experience" of suffering and injustice as a boundary-condition pointing implicitly toward God's redemptive grace, correlating tradition with contemporary social experience. Hans Küng's radical critique of papal infallibility (Infallible? An Enquiry) led Rome to revoke his teaching license in 1979 — with Rahner himself publicly criticising Küng for abandoning the Catholic "rule of faith." Nicholas Lash developed a postliberal, "grammatical" critique of church practice as a discipline of self-correcting idolatry-critique. Karol Wojtyła (Pope John Paul II), shaped by surviving Nazi occupation and Soviet repression, brought a personalist phenomenology into papal teaching against what he called the secular "culture of death."

## Example
The 1979 revocation of Hans Küng's canonical mandate to teach as a Catholic theologian — following his sustained public challenge to the dogma of papal infallibility — is the clearest concrete instance in this chapter of how post-conciliar Catholic theology's new pluralism still met firm institutional limits.

## Illustration
Concilium and Communio can be pictured as two siblings who grew up in the same house (pre-conciliar nouvelle théologie) but, once grown, furnish their own homes very differently: one (Concilium) keeps knocking down walls to let the neighbourhood in, the other (Communio) keeps restoring the original architecture — both insist they are being faithful to the family's true character.

## Key Terms
- **Aggiornamento** (Italian) — "bringing up to date": Vatican II's programmatic opening of the Church to modern values, historical-critical method, and human rights.
- **Negative Contrast Experience**: Schillebeeckx's term for an experience of severe suffering or injustice that provokes immediate moral protest and implicitly points toward God's redemptive grace.
- **Koinonia** (Greek) — "communion / fellowship": Vatican II's central ecclesiological paradigm (elaborated by Yves Congar), shifting the Church's self-understanding from monarchical hierarchy to sacramental communion of local churches.
- **Preferential Option for the Poor**: The lens, drawn from Latin American liberation theology, adopted by much post-conciliar Catholic theology for judging economic structures and inspiring concrete acts of justice.

## Connections
Rooted directly in de Lubac, Rahner, and von Balthasar (Part I). The Concilium/Communio split mirrors the wider Protestant division between Tracy's correlation and Lindbeck's postliberalism (previous chapter). Johann-Baptist Metz's "political theology," retrieving the memory of Auschwitz, directly shaped Gustavo Gutiérrez and Latin American liberation theology (Part V).
