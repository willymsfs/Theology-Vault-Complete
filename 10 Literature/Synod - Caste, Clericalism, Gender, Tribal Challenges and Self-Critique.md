# Synodality — Caste, Clericalism, Gender, Tribal Challenges and Self-Critique

Source: *Synodality* (Indian Edition, 2025), via NotebookLM.

## Caste
Upper-caste clergy dominance in appointments and parish assignment; casteism named as one of the most
toxic Indian variants of clericalism, carried into seminaries; the documented finding that Dalit
clergy, once ordained, often adopt the dominant clerical mindset rather than remaining in solidarity
with their background.

## Clericalism and gender
Priests as "givers" of grace and laity as "receivers"; the gap between consultative sessions and
actual decisions producing disillusionment ("let the priest decide"). Women constitute the active
majority of parish life yet are structurally excluded from decision-making; patriarchal control
extends into religious congregations — see [[Chinliankhup Stanislaus]] on the same limit inside tribal
governance itself.

## Tribal-specific pressures
Land alienation and displacement; the collision of tribal customary law (consensus, elder councils)
with canon law's centralised structures (c. 129); Latin liturgical uniformity suppressing genuine
inculturation (Sarhul, Karam dismissed as "animistic"); anti-conversion legislation and Hindutva
surveillance in Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh and Madhya Pradesh restricting open synodal assembly.

## Critiques of synodality itself
[[Shalini Mulackal]] (p. 352): Vatican II's unreconciled dual ecclesiology produces systemic paralysis.
[[James Ponniah]] (p. 86): the *niti*/*nyaya* gap — see [[Niti and Nyaya]]. [[Francis Thonippara]] (p.
246): the majoritarian risk — see [[Vox Populi Vox Dei - The Majoritarian Risk]]. [[Susairaj]] (p.
439): the 80/5 formation-spending ratio. Elite capture: the 2021-2024 consultation was largely reached
by the educated, English-speaking, middle-class parish elite, bypassing poor Dalit Christians and
illiterate Adivasi Catholics through online questionnaires. Eurocentric framing: the theological
vocabulary of synodality reflects Western European concerns, while the Indian and tribal Church
wrestles with land, hunger, caste atrocity and persecution.
