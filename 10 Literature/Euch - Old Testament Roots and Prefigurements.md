# Unit 1 — Old Testament Roots and Prefigurements

The Eucharist is not an isolated institution but the convergence of several Old Testament streams. A BTh student should be able to name the six principal ones and say what each contributes.

## The Six Principal Types

| Type | Text | Contribution to Eucharistic theology |
|---|---|---|
| **Melchizedek's offering** | Gen 14:18; Ps 110:4 | Bread and wine offered by a priest-king of an order older than Levi; taken up in Heb 5-7 and named in the Roman Canon |
| **Passover** | Exod 12 | Sacrificial lamb, blood that saves, a *meal* that is also a memorial of deliverance, repeated annually by household |
| **Manna** | Exod 16; Ps 78:24-25 | Bread from heaven given daily in the wilderness; the explicit foil of John 6:31-58 |
| **Sinai covenant blood** | Exod 24:8 | *"Behold the blood of the covenant"* — Jesus's own words at the Supper quote this verse directly |
| **Todah sacrifice** | Lev 7:11-15; Ps 116 | The thanksgiving offering: unbloody, accompanied by bread and a cup of salvation, offered by one delivered from death |
| **Showbread** | Exod 25:30; Lev 24:5-9 | *Lehem happanim*, "bread of the Presence" — twelve loaves perpetually before the Lord, eaten only by priests |

## Two Prophetic Anticipations

- **Malachi 1:11** — "from the rising of the sun to its setting my name is great among the nations, and in every place incense is offered to my name, and **a pure offering**." This verse is the single most-cited Old Testament text in patristic Eucharistic theology (Didache 14, Justin, Irenaeus), because it promises a universal, non-Temple sacrifice.
- **Isaiah 25:6** — "the LORD of hosts will make for all peoples a feast of rich food, a feast of well-aged wine." The **eschatological banquet**, which supplies the "until he comes" horizon of every Eucharistic celebration.

Add **Proverbs 9:1-6**, where Wisdom builds her house, mixes her wine, and calls the simple to eat her bread — the sapiential stream that John 6 draws on.

## The Jewish Liturgical Matrix

The Eucharistic Prayer did not appear from nowhere. Its shape descends from the Jewish ***berakah*** (blessing) and specifically from the ***birkat ha-mazon***, the grace after meals, which has a three-fold structure: **praise** of God the creator, **thanksgiving** for the land and covenant, and **supplication** for Jerusalem and the coming of the Messiah. Compare any classical anaphora and you will find the same three movements — praise, thanksgiving/memorial, petition — with the institution narrative and epiclesis inserted.

**Illustration.** Think of the Jewish table blessing as a house's foundation and load-bearing walls. Christianity did not demolish the house to build a new one; it added a room — the memorial of Christ's death and the invocation of the Spirit — onto a structure that was already standing. This is why the earliest Christian Eucharistic prayers sound so Jewish.
