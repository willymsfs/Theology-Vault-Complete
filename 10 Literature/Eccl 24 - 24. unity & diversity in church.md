# 24. unity & diversity in church.pdf

**TITLE**: "Unity and Diversity in the Church: Transformed identities and the peace of Christ in Ephesians." By William S. Campbell (Reader in Biblical Studies, University of Wales, Lampeter) [89]. Published in *Irish Biblical Studies* (presented at the Oxford Centre for Mission Studies, September 25, 2007) [90].

**MAIN SUBJECT**:
An investigation of the negotiation of ethnic identity (specifically Jewish and Gentile) in the Letter to the Ephesians, arguing that Christ's reconciling work does not erase or fuse cultural differences but transforms them to enable a concrete, sociopolitical peace (*shalom*) [89, 91].

**KEY TOPICS**:
*   Paul's theology of ethnic identity (circumcision vs. uncircumcision) in Romans and 1 Corinthians [92, 93].
*   The macro-image of the "new temple" and the demolition of the dividing wall in Ephesians [94].
*   The historical and political context of Ephesus around 90 CE under Emperor Domitian [94-96].
*   The unique use of Solomonic temple-dedication imagery in Eph 2:13-22 [97-99].
*   Deconstructing the anti-Jewish "third race/third entity" (*genus tertium*) model of the Church [100-102].
*   The political character of Ephesians as an "alternative society" to the Roman Empire [103, 104].
*   The strategic proliferation of positive and negative "sun-compounds" to define group-level relationships [105-107].
*   The contemporary relevance of the Ephesians peace vision in deeply divided societies (such as Northern Ireland) [108, 109].

**CONCEPTS EXPLAINED**:
1.  **En Tē Klēsei (Remaining in the Calling)**: Paul's theological principle (1 Cor 7:20) asserting that a believer's original, historical identity (ethnicity, socio-economic status) is not to be denied or forsaken upon conversion, as both circumcision and uncircumcision are relativized before the call of God [92, 93].
2.  **One New Anthrōpos**: The key metaphorical image in Eph 2:15 describing the reconciliation of Jew and Gentile; Campbell argues it represents a harmonious relationship between distinct entities rather than a literal, culture-free "fusion" or a "third race" [91, 100, 110].
3.  **Solomonic Shalom**: The biblical concept of total corporate well-being and peace, modeled on King Solomon's historical unification of Israel, which Ephesians adapts to describe the reconciliation of Jews and non-Jews in a newly built temple [97, 111].
4.  **Sun-Compounds**: The extensive use of Greek verbs and nouns prefixed with *sun-* (such as *sumpolitai*, *sunoikodomeisthe*) in Ephesians, strategically deployed to emphasize joint, corporate group-level reconciliation [105].
5.  **Hidden Transcript (Anti-Imperial Subversion)**: The subtle political code in Ephesians that designates God (not the Emperor) as Father of all, and Christ (not Augustus) as the true peace creator and Savior of the world, presenting the Church as an alternative society to Roman domination [103, 104].

**ISSUES ADDRESSED**:
1.  **The Anti-Judaism of the "Third Entity" Theory**: Challenging the long-standing academic view that the Church is a "third race" that replaces or supersedes Israel, a view which historically fueled Christian anti-Semitism and denied Jews the right to exist [100-102].
2.  **The Anachronistic Separation of Christ from the Law**: Dismantling the assumption that accepting Christ required Jewish believers to abandon the law, highlighting historical evidence of thriving Jewish-Christian communities in Ephesus [95, 112].
3.  **The Pressures of Domitian's Imperial Cult**: Navigating the intense socio-political constraints of the *fiscus judaicus* and state suspicion of disloyalty, which forced local house-churches to strategically align with or distance themselves from Jewish synagogues for survival [96, 104].
4.  **The Individualistic Reduction of Christ's Peace**: Correcting the modern, privatized reading of Christian peace as a sentimental, internalized emotion, and reclaiming it as a structural, political challenge to transform divided communities [108, 109].

**IMPORTANT QUOTES**:

*   "The author’s endeavour ought not to be read as a levelling and abolishing of all ethnic differences… but as a repudiation of the ethnocentric perspective which perceives the differences as grounds for estrangement and discrimination." (Page 196 / Passage 196) - Context: Quoting T. L. N. Yee to prove that Ephesians does not seek to homogenize culture.

**SUMMARY**:
William S. Campbell examines the Letter to the Ephesians to argue that its vision of Christian unity does not erase previous ethnic identities but rather reconciles differences without hostility [89, 91]. Rejecting the anti-Jewish "third race" or "third entity" model of the Church, he asserts that Ephesians maintains a deep, foundational continuity with Israel, utilizing Solomonic temple-building imagery to describe the integration of Gentiles as joint-heirs alongside Jewish believers [97, 100, 101, 113]. Under the intense imperial pressures of Domitian's reign, the Church emerged as a highly political, alternative society that directly challenged Roman domination [103, 104]. By analyzing the letter's extensive use of "sun-compounds" and its description of the Church as an ongoing building site, Campbell concludes that Christ's peace (*shalom*) is not a sentimental, private emotion but a real, demanding social and political reconciliation that turns difference from a source of conflict into a celebration of grace [105, 106, 108, 109].
