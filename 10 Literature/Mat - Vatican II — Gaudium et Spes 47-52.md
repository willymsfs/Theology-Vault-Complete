# Unit 8 — Vatican II — Gaudium et Spes 47-52

The Council's treatment of marriage is contained in *Gaudium et Spes* Part II, chapter 1, and it changed the register of Catholic teaching decisively.

## Covenant, Not Contract

**GS 48**: "The intimate partnership of married life and love (***intima communitas vitae et amoris coniugalis***) has been established by the Creator... **a conjugal covenant (*foedus*) of irrevocable personal consent**."

Two deliberate shifts:
- ***Foedus* (covenant) replaces *contractus* (contract)** as the primary category. The 1917 Code had spoken of a contract; the biblical and personalist language of covenant now governs.
- The emphasis falls on **personal consent** and the **communion of persons**, not primarily on the exchange of rights over bodies (the *ius in corpus* of the older canonical formulation).

## The Ends of Marriage — What the Council Did Not Say

**This is the single most examinable point in the unit.** *Gaudium et Spes* **deliberately declines to repeat the primary/secondary hierarchy of ends.**

- **GS 48** states that marriage and conjugal love "are by their very nature ordained toward the begetting and educating of children," and calls children "the supreme gift of marriage."
- But **GS 50** adds immediately: "marriage is **not instituted solely for procreation**"; its indissoluble character and the whole partnership "**persist even when offspring are lacking**," and conjugal love retains its "character of being a whole" regardless.

The drafting history is explicit: amendments seeking to restore the "primary end" language were **rejected**. The Council chose to state the goods of marriage **without ranking them**.

**The consequence appears in the 1983 Code, c. 1055 §1**, which defines the matrimonial covenant as ordered "**to the good of the spouses (*bonum coniugum*) and the procreation and education of offspring**" — the two placed in a single sentence with the good of the spouses named *first*, and no hierarchy asserted. Compare the 1917 Code's c. 1013 §1 and the change is unmistakable.

## Conjugal Love

**GS 49** offers what earlier magisterial texts had not: a substantial theology of **conjugal love** as such — "eminently human," involving "the good of the whole person," expressed and perfected "in a unique way" by the marital act, which is "noble and honourable" and signifies and promotes "that mutual self-giving by which spouses enrich each other."

## The Domestic Church

**LG 11** calls the family "the **domestic church**" (*ecclesia domestica*) — the parents being "by word and example the first heralds of the faith to their children." This recovers a patristic image (Chrysostom's "little church") and grounds the whole modern theology of the family as an ecclesial reality, developed further in *Familiaris Consortio* 21 and *Amoris Laetitia*.

## The Reformed Rite

The **Rite of Marriage** (*Ordo celebrandi Matrimonium*, 1969; revised 1991) restored a Liturgy of the Word, a fuller nuptial blessing recovering ancient Trinitarian and epicletic language, and provision for celebration within Mass — as well as adaptation to local custom, which is significant for the Indian context (Unit 13).
