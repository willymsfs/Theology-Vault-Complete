# Unit 11 — Vatican II and the 1973 Rite of Penance

## The Conciliar Mandate

***Sacrosanctum Concilium* 72** is brief and consequential: "**The rite and formulas for the sacrament of penance are to be revised so that they more clearly express both the nature and the effect of the sacrament.**"

***Lumen Gentium* 11** supplies the theological substance the revision would express — those who approach the sacrament "obtain pardon from the mercy of God for the offence committed against him **and are at the same time reconciled with the Church**, which they have wounded by their sins."

**This is the recovery of the ecclesial dimension**, and it is the interpretive key to the whole reform. The scholastic tradition had it (*reconciliatio cum Ecclesia* was the *res et sacramentum*), but centuries of private practice had obscured it in ordinary consciousness. Sin is never merely a private transaction between the individual and God; it damages the body.

## The Ordo Paenitentiae (2 December 1973)

**The three forms:**

| Rite | Structure | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Rite I** | Reconciliation of **individual** penitents | The ordinary form |
| **Rite II** | Communal celebration with **individual confession and absolution** | Liturgy of the Word, communal examination, then individual confession; strongly recommended, and the clearest expression of the ecclesial dimension |
| **Rite III** | Communal celebration with **general confession and absolution** | Strictly restricted — see below |

**The restriction on Rite III** (elaborated in **CIC cc. 961-963** and tightened by John Paul II's motu proprio ***Misericordia Dei***, 2002): general absolution requires either **danger of death** with insufficient time, or a **grave necessity** judged by the diocesan bishop — and the number of confessors being insufficient merely because of a large gathering or a great feast does **not** constitute grave necessity. Those absolved are obliged to confess their grave sins individually **as soon as possible**.

**The renewed structure of Rite I** deliberately restores elements long lost: **welcome and a blessing**, a **reading from Scripture** (frequently omitted in practice, but integral to the rite as revised), the confession, the penance, the **act of contrition**, the absolution, a **proclamation of praise**, and dismissal.

**The new formula of absolution** replaced the terse *Ego te absolvo* with a fully Trinitarian prayer, of which the older formula is now the conclusion:

> *"God, the Father of mercies, through the death and resurrection of his Son has reconciled the world to himself and sent the Holy Spirit among us for the forgiveness of sins; through the ministry of the Church may God give you pardon and peace, and I absolve you from your sins in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit."*

Notice how much doctrine this recovers: the Father's initiative, the paschal mystery, the Spirit's mission, **the ministry of the Church**, and only then the indicative absolution.

## Later Magisterial Documents

| Document | Date | Content |
|---|---|---|
| ***Reconciliatio et Paenitentia*** | 1984 | John Paul II's post-synodal exhortation; important for its careful treatment of **social sin** (§16), which it accepts in an analogical sense while insisting that sin is always ultimately a personal act |
| ***Veritatis Splendor*** | 1993 | §§65-70 critique **fundamental option** theory (Unit 12) |
| ***Misericordia Dei*** | 2002 | Restates the discipline on individual confession and restricts general absolution |
| ***Misericordiae Vultus*** / ***Misericordia et Misera*** | 2015 / 2016 | Francis's Jubilee of Mercy documents; institute the **Missionaries of Mercy** and extend to all priests the faculty to absolve the sin of abortion |

## The Crisis of Practice

Any honest treatment must name it: **the collapse in the frequency of confession** across most of the Western Church from the late 1960s is among the most dramatic shifts in Catholic practice in modern history — in many countries from near-universal regular use to a small minority.

Diagnoses vary and are genuinely contested: the weakening of the sense of sin; the shift from a juridical to a therapeutic culture; the *Humanae Vitae* crisis and the resulting quiet disengagement from the confessional over contraception; loss of the mortal/venial distinction; poor experiences of the sacrament; the general decline of practice; and — a point often overlooked — that the near-universal reception of communion at every Mass, following Pius X's reforms, removed the practical link by which confession had been tied to communion.
