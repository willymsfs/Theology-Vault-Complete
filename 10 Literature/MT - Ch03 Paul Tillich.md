# Ch. 03 — Paul Tillich
*Chapter author: David H. Kelsey*

## Core Idea
Tillich's "method of correlation" pairs the existential questions raised by modern secular culture with theological answers drawn from Christian symbols, and redefines God not as a being among beings but as "Being-Itself," the ground of being.

## Detailed Explanation
Raised as a conservative German Lutheran pastor's son, Tillich served as a WWI chaplain; the carnage he witnessed transformed him into a religious socialist who described his whole vocation as living "on the boundary" between the Christian tradition and secular culture. Exiled by the Nazis in 1933, he taught at Union Theological Seminary, Harvard, and Chicago, becoming one of the most publicly influential American theologians of the mid-century. Where Barth insisted revelation cannot be correlated with human experience, Tillich made correlation his method: culture asks the questions (meaninglessness, guilt, death), and Christian symbols supply answers that make existential sense of them — without collapsing theology into psychology or reducing God to a projection of human need.

## Example
Tillich's own account of "ultimate concern" was tested publicly when critics accused him of reducing God-talk to psychological need; his defence — that naming God "Being-Itself" protects, rather than dissolves, God's transcendence, since no finite object (including "a being" in the ordinary sense) can be ultimate — became a touchstone reference point for later debates about God-language in a secular age, notably in John Robinson's popular Honest to God (1963).

## Illustration
If most theology asks "what has God said?" and starts from the answer, Tillich starts from the question — like a doctor who first carefully diagnoses exactly what is wrong with a patient (anxiety, meaninglessness, guilt) before prescribing a remedy that actually fits the diagnosis, rather than handing out the same medicine regardless of the complaint.

## Key Terms
- **Method of Correlation**: Tillich's systematic method: existential questions posed by culture are correlated with theological answers given in Christian symbols.
- **Being-Itself** (Sein-Selbst, German) — "the ground of being": Tillich's name for God: not one being among others but the unconditioned power and ground underlying all existence.
- **New Being** (Neues Sein, German) — "new being": The healing, reconciling power mediated through Jesus as the Christ, who overcomes estrangement without himself falling into it.
- **Ultimate Concern** (unbedingtes Sorgen, German) — "unconditional concern": Tillich's definition of faith: the state of being grasped by that which concerns one unconditionally — the 'dimension of depth' in every human act.

## Connections
Tillich is Ford's paradigm "type three" theologian (theology of correlation with culture), directly contested by Barth's "type two" refusal of any such correlation. His "ground of being" language was popularised by John Robinson's Honest to God, and his correlation method deeply shaped the American "revisionists and liberals" (Tracy, Kaufman, Farley) treated in Part II.C.
