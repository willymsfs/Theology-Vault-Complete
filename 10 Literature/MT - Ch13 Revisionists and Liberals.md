# Ch. 13 — Revisionists and Liberals
*Chapter author: James J. Buckley*

## Core Idea
American revisionist and liberal theologians (Edward Farley, Gordon Kaufman, Schubert Ogden, David Tracy, and the process theology movement) reject dogmatic "externalism," reconstructing Christian doctrine — often picturing God as changing, responsive, even suffering — using modern philosophy, evolutionary science, and historical consciousness, in order to make theology's truth-claims publicly, rationally accessible.

## Detailed Explanation
Rooted in Friedrich Schleiermacher's nineteenth-century liberal turn and Alfred North Whitehead's philosophical "naturalism," this North American lineage includes Edward Farley's critique of the "house of authority" (the traditional package of inerrancy and ecclesial infallibility treated as finished, unquestionable propositions); Gordon Kaufman's Kantian reconstruction of humans as "biohistorical" evolutionary beings and God as a constructed symbol of serendipitous creativity; Schubert Ogden's process-philosophical case (via Whitehead and Hartshorne) for God as a genuinely responsive companion; and David Tracy's "method of correlation," placing Christian tradition and the modern situation into sustained, mutually critical dialogue. Process theology's "dipolar" God — an eternal, potential nature alongside a temporal, changing nature that genuinely suffers with the world — is the movement's most distinctive metaphysical proposal.

## Example
Process theology's claim that God is the "fellow-sufferer who understands" (Whitehead's phrase) gave process-influenced pastoral counselling and hospital chaplaincy a theological alternative to a strictly impassible, unmoved God — a God who is affected by and genuinely grieves human suffering alongside the sufferer, not merely observing it from untouched eternity.

## Illustration
A "dipolar" God is like a musician who has both a fixed, unchanging mastery of music theory (the primordial nature) and a live, improvised performance that genuinely responds in real time to what the audience and the other musicians do (the consequent nature) — the theory never changes, but the actual music played is always new, responsive, and never fully predetermined in advance.

## Key Terms
- **House of Authority**: Edward Farley's term for the traditional dogmatic package (inerrancy, ecclesial infallibility) treating revelation as a finished, unquestionable set of propositions.
- **Biohistorical Beings**: Gordon Kaufman's account of humans as evolutionary, self-reflexive organisms shaped by language, culture, and nature rather than as possessors of a static metaphysical soul.
- **Dipolar Theism**: The process-theological model of God as having both an eternal, potential nature and a temporal, changing nature that genuinely suffers with the actual world.
- **Mutual Immanence**: The reciprocal relationship in process metaphysics whereby the world acts upon God and God indwells the world as its responsive "fellow-sufferer."

## Connections
Schleiermacher is this whole movement's acknowledged "grandfather." All four figures adapt and extend Tillich's method of correlation (Part I) with different modern tools — phenomenology (Farley), linguistic analysis and Bultmann (Ogden), Kant (Kaufman), Lonergan and Ricoeur (Tracy). Barth (Part I) is their sharpest critic, rejecting any external cultural "context" as arbiter of Christ's meaning. Ronald Thiemann and Alasdair MacIntyre mount non-foundationalist and tradition-based critiques from directions closer to postliberalism (Part II.D).
