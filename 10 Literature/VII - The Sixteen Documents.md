# Part 2 — The Sixteen Documents

The Council produced **sixteen official documents** in three descending tiers of authority: four Constitutions, nine Decrees, three Declarations.

## The Four Constitutions in Depth

**Lumen Gentium** — the crowning theological achievement. Recovers a patristic vision of the Church as "mystery" and "People of God" (Ch. 2 placed *before* Ch. 3 on hierarchy, asserting baptismal equality first), and defines **episcopal collegiality** — completing what Vatican I left unfinished.

**Dei Verbum** — arguably the most fundamental doctrinal document, since revelation is the foundation of all doctrine. Rejects the post-Tridentine "two-source theory," affirming Scripture and Tradition flow from a single divine wellspring. Scripture declared the "soul of theology."

**Sacrosanctum Concilium** — the first document debated and approved, because its renewal drafts were in the best shape. Urges active, conscious, full participation; introduces the vernacular; simplifies rites; moves from scholastic terminology toward the wider patristic tradition.

**Gaudium et Spes** — the longest document, unique in its pastoral, dialogical genre ("Schema 13"/"XVII"). Outlines a Christian anthropology and addresses marriage, family, culture, war. Uses the inductive "see-judge-act" method. Resolved the Neo-Augustinian/Neo-Thomist tension via a Christological anthropology (GS 22: Christ reveals humanity to itself).

## The Nine Decrees in Depth

**Unitatis Redintegratio** (Ecumenism) — commits the Church to the modern ecumenical movement; recognizes "elements of sanctification and truth" (LG 15, UR 3) in other Christian communities.

**Christus Dominus** (Bishops) — 3 chapters (universal, diocesan, regional levels). Depends entirely on *Lumen Gentium*'s theology of the episcopate. Bishop as sacramentally-empowered collaborator, not a papal "branch manager." Major fruit: **national episcopal conferences** and the **Synod of Bishops** — though progressives wanted a permanent deliberative body sharing papal governance, and Paul VI made it consultative only.

**Apostolicam Actuositatem** (Laity) — 6 chapters. The lay apostolate flows **directly from baptism**, not delegation from clergy; affirms the laity's "secular character." Dismantled the top-down "Catholic Action" model; paved the way for lay ecclesial ministry (see Part 5's "Rising Laity" material).

**Ad Gentes** (Missions) — Trinitarian foundation: the Church is "by its very nature missionary" as continuation of the Father's sending of Son and Spirit. Incarnational, non-imposing inculturation. The Curia tried to rush it into 13 "dry bones" propositions; bishops flatly rejected this. Third World bishops used the debate to reform the centralized *Propaganda Fide*.

**Presbyterorum Ordinis** (Priests) — shift from an isolated "cultic model" of priesthood to a relational "servant leader" model, embedded in a web of relationships with bishop, fellow priests, laity. Celibacy removed from debate entirely by papal letter.

**Optatam Totius** (Seminary Training) — holistic formation integrating philosophy/theology with Scripture, pastoral experience, psychological evaluation. Decentralized seminary design to national conferences against the historic control of the Congregation for Seminaries.

**Perfectae Caritatis** (Religious Life) — combines *ressourcement* (founder's original charism) with *aggiornamento*. Received over **14,000 amendments**. Women's religious communities implemented it far beyond what the hierarchy anticipated.

**Orientalium Ecclesiarum** (Eastern Churches) — Eastern Catholic communities are **autonomous, equal, self-governing churches**, not merely different rites. Criticized for lingering "Latinizing" tendencies. Broke ground by relaxing *communicatio in sacris* with Orthodox Christians.

**Inter Mirifica** (Media) — widely judged the Council's **weakest text**; an instrumental, "neutral tool" view of media, rushed through to give the Council an early finished document. Prompted the far stronger 1971 *Communio et Progressio*.

## The Three Declarations in Depth

**Dignitatis Humanae** (Religious Liberty) — the most bitterly contested document. Moved from "error has no rights" (Ottaviani, Ruffini) to freedom grounded in **personal human dignity**, chiefly through John Courtney Murray's drafting. Ratzinger later called it a "counter-syllabus."

**Nostra Aetate** (Non-Christian Religions) — began as *De Judaeis*, a post-Shoah statement on antisemitism commissioned from Cardinal Bea; expanded under political pressure to address Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism broadly. Shortest document, arguably most historically consequential. Uses *patrimonium spirituale* ("spiritual patrimony") for the unique bond with Judaism.

**Gravissimum Educationis** (Education) — holistic concern for the whole person; affirms parental right to choose schools. American bishops (Spellman) wanted an explicit state-funding mandate; got only a compromise.

## Rejected or Radically Rewritten Schemata

- ***De Fontibus Revelationis* -> *Dei Verbum***: Ottaviani's draft treated Scripture/Tradition as two separate sources, condemned modern biblical criticism. Rejected 20-21 Nov 1962; rebuilt by periti including Daniélou, Rahner, Ratzinger.
- ***De Ecclesia* -> *Lumen Gentium***: original clerical, juridical draft set aside after Session I; intersession subcommission adopted **Gérard Philips's** alternative text, pruning eleven scholastic chapters to four.
- **The "Döpfner Plan"**: 1964 proposal to compress minor schemata into brief bulleted "propositions"; bishops resisted, texts expanded back into full decrees.
- **Schema XVII/XIII -> *Gaudium et Spes***: no pre-conciliar model existed; four complete rewrites from the "Roman text" to Suenens's "Malines text."

## The Revision Process

Drafting/distribution -> *aula* debate (10-minute speeches, written *animadversiones*) -> preliminary vote on the schema as a working basis -> commission rewriting with *periti* -> section-by-section voting (*placet*/*non placet*/*placet iuxta modum* with a written *modus*) -> commission processes the *modi* -> final whole-text vote -> papal approval *una cum Patribus* -> solemn promulgation.
