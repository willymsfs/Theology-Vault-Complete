# Synodality — The Consultative Trap and Ecumenical Comparison (MTh)

Source: *Synodality* (Indian Edition, 2025), via NotebookLM.

## The canonical bottleneck
[[Bishop S. Antonysamy]] (pp. 277-287) traces the mechanism: canon 129 §1 restricts *potestas
regiminis* to those in sacred orders; Parish Pastoral Councils (c. 536), Parish Finance Councils
(c. 537) and Diocesan Pastoral Councils (c. 511) are consequently consultative only, so a unanimous
council can be legally overridden by the cleric who convened it. [[Merlin Rengith Ambrose]] (pp.
297-299) carries Sabine Demel's diagnosis: this reduces lay co-responsibility to a clerical concession
rather than a baptismal right (c. 208), and proposes forming laity as co-believers rather than
collaborators.

## "Not a parliament" — defence and counter-critique
Pope Francis and the synodal documents insist a synod seeks consensus under the Spirit, not
majoritarian voting. [[James Ponniah]] (pp. 96-97) and [[Shalini Mulackal]] (p. 351) counter that
without binding structures, "not a parliament" can be weaponised to bypass accountability while
appearing to listen.

## Ecumenical comparison
[[Simon Pinto]] (pp. 371-372) sets Orthodox *sobornost* (no bishop acts outside the college or the
consent of the flock) beside the Anglican three-house system, where Bishops, Clergy and Laity hold a
concurrent *deliberative* vote — a live demonstration that lay deliberative authority is compatible
with apostolic ministry claims. [[Varghese Koluthara]] (pp. 303-313) shows the CCEO already grants
Eastern Catholic Patriarchal Synods legislative, executive and judicial power the Latin Synod of
Bishops lacks.
