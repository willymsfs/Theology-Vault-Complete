# 7. Communion ecclesiology source of hope.pdf

**TITLE**: "Communion Ecclesiology: Source of Hope, Source of Controversy." By Susan K. Wood (Associate Professor of Theology, Saint John's University, Collegeville, Minnesota). Published in *Pro Ecclesia*, Vol. II, No. 4, pages 424–432 [1, 2].

**MAIN SUBJECT**: An analysis of communion ecclesiology as the dominant theological paradigm of the post-conciliar era, examining its Trinitarian and sacramental foundations, its ecumenical possibilities, and its internal institutional controversies [1, 3].

**KEY TOPICS**:
*   The emergence of communion as the central, unifying image of the Second Vatican Council [1].
*   Integration of the "Body of Christ" and the "People of God" ecclesial models [2].
*   The scriptural distinction of the *totus Christus* between Ephesians/Colossians and 1 Corinthians/Romans [4].
*   Eucharistic realism as both the symbolic sign and the constitutive source of ecclesial unity [5, 6].
*   The role of the college of bishops in representing the communion of altar communities [7].
*   The non-absorptive, ecumenical "communion of communions" model [8].
*   The grassroots, word-centered ecclesiology of Basic Christian Communities (BECs) [9, 10].
*   The Vatican’s doctrinal cautions regarding the local-universal relationship [10, 11].
*   The systematic integration of Avery Dulles's separate "Models of the Church" into a coherent whole [12].

**CONCEPTS EXPLAINED**:
1.  **Totus Christus (The Whole Christ)**: A biblical and patristic framework retrieving the understanding that all of humanity, through grace in a covenant relationship, participates so deeply in Christ as the head that they are joined together as a single ontological being [2].
2.  **Eucharistic Realism**: The doctrinal assertion that the eucharistic bread and wine are the real presence of Christ, acting as the prior, objective, and effective condition that physically brings about the unity of the communicants in the body of Christ [5, 6, 13].
3.  **Trinitarian Perichoresis**: The dynamic, eternal, and mutually indwelling relationship of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, which acts as the organic model for human ecclesial life, catching believers up into the actual life of the Trinity [14].
4.  **Communion of Communions**: An ecumenical model where separated historical traditions are recognized as authentic "particular churches" centered on their respective eucharistic altars, allowing for unity without structural absorption [8].
5.  **Ontological Priority of the Universal Church**: The doctrinal assertion (articulated by the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith) that the universal Church precedes any local or particular manifestation, guarding against autonomous, localized congregationalism [10, 11, 15].

**ISSUES ADDRESSED**:
1.  **The Altar/Clerical Axis vs. the Word/Laity Axis**: Resolving the tension between a highly structured, bishop-centered eucharistic communion and a subjective, word-centered lay communion found in grassroots basic communities lacking ordained priests [7, 9, 10].
2.  **The Relationship between Local and Universal Church**: Addressing the theological paradox in CDF letters that overstates the priority of the universal Church, potentially creating an abstract, "invisible" Church that jeopardizes the concrete sacramentality of the local community [11, 16].
3.  **Confusion with Avery Dulles's Communitarian Typology**: Correcting the common misconception that communion ecclesiology is merely Dulles's "communion model" (which lacked institutional structure and resembled a simple, informal network of friendly relationships) [12, 17, 18].
4.  **The Non-Episcopal Character of Grassroots Praxis**: Critiquing base communities (such as Leonardo Boff’s "ecclesiogenesis") for sometimes downplaying the intrinsic necessity of the episcopacy and the Eucharist in their quest for structural liberation [19, 20].

**IMPORTANT QUOTES**:
*   "Within this integration the image of communion retrieves a biblical and patristic idea of the whole Christ, the totus Christus..." (Page 424 / Passage 96) - Context: Showing how "communion" reconciles the scriptural metaphors of Lumen Gentium.
*   "There exists an intrinsic relationship between the historical Christ, the sacramental Christ of the Eucharist and the ecclesial Christ." (Page 424 / Passage 98) - Context: Highlighting the ontological density that links the sacrament with the community.
*   "The primary emphasis of communion consists in the elements of grace and sacrament... Organizational structures or the comradeship of good will among church members are secondary..." (Page 425 / Passage 101) - Context: Separating communion ecclesiology from mere sociological or political associations.
*   "Ecumenically, 'full ecclesiastical communion' will be achieved, not when ecclesial communities separated from the Apostolic See are absorbed into a monolithic Roman church, but when unity among all the particular churches is achieved." (Page 427 / Passage 108) - Context: Offering an elastic, non-monolithic model of ecumenical dialogue.
*   "In its concern to guard against a certain autonomy of the local churches, the Congregation may have overstated the relationship between the universal church and the particular churches." (Page 429 / Passage 114) - Context: Critiquing the CDF's 1992 letter "Some Aspects of the Church Understood as Communion."
*   "Communion ecclesiology is the focus of ecclesiology in our time. It is a biblical category that has systematic value since it can integrate themes of eschatology, Christology, Trinity, sacraments, and practical matters of church life." (Page 431 / Passage 121, 122) - Context: Concluding with a synthesis of communion's systematic power.

**SUMMARY**:
Susan K. Wood explores communion ecclesiology as the preeminent framework of modern theology, showing how it synthesizes the biblical models of the "Body of Christ" and the "People of God" into an integrated, sacramental whole [2, 12, 21]. By grounding this model in Trinitarian *perichoresis* and eucharistic realism, she demonstrates its profound capacity to aid ecumenical rapprochement through flexible structures like the "communion of communions" [8, 13, 14]. However, she details the deep institutional controversies this model generates, specifically the tension between the Vatican's defense of the ontological priority of the universal Church and the grassroots, lay-led praxis of Latin American Basic Ecclesial Communities [10, 11, 19]. Wood concludes that because communion is intrinsically sacramental, its horizontal structures of governance must remain dynamically anchored to vertical, transcendent communion with God, requiring a rigorous recovery of the theology of sacramentality [21].
