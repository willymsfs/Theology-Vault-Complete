# Unit 12 — Nullity, Impediments, and Canon Law

## Nullity Is Not Divorce

The distinction must be stated exactly: **a declaration of nullity states that what appeared to be a marriage never in fact was one**, because something essential was missing from the start. It does not dissolve a marriage; it declares that no bond ever came into existence. Children of a putative marriage remain legitimate (c. 1137).

## Grounds of Nullity

| Category | Canons | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Defect of consent** | cc. 1095-1103 | Lack of sufficient use of reason; **grave lack of discretion of judgement** concerning essential rights and duties; **incapacity to assume** the essential obligations for causes of a psychic nature (c. 1095 — by far the most commonly invoked ground today); error about a quality directly and principally intended; **total or partial simulation** (excluding indissolubility, fidelity, or children); force or grave fear |
| **Impediments** | cc. 1083-1094 | Age; antecedent and perpetual impotence; **prior bond**; disparity of cult; sacred orders; perpetual public vow of chastity; abduction; crime; consanguinity; affinity; public propriety; legal adoption |
| **Defect of form** | c. 1108 | Marriage not celebrated before the local ordinary, parish priest, or delegated priest or deacon, and two witnesses |

## Dissolution of a Valid Bond — the Three Exceptions

A **ratified and consummated** sacramental marriage cannot be dissolved by any power on earth (c. 1141). But three genuine dissolutions exist, and knowing them is essential:

1. **Ratum non consummatum** (c. 1142) — a valid but unconsummated marriage may be dissolved by the Roman Pontiff for a just cause.
2. **The Pauline privilege** (cc. 1143-1147) — a marriage between two *unbaptised* persons may be dissolved in favour of the faith when one is baptised and the other departs. Rooted in 1 Cor 7:12-15.
3. **The Petrine privilege / privilege of the faith** — dissolution by papal act of a marriage between a baptised and an unbaptised person, in favour of the faith of the Catholic party.

Note what all three have in common: none dissolves a *consummated sacramental* marriage.

## Mixed Marriages and Disparity of Cult

- **Mixed marriage** (Catholic with a baptised non-Catholic) requires the **permission** of the ordinary (c. 1124).
- **Disparity of cult** (Catholic with a non-baptised person) is a **diriment impediment** requiring **dispensation** (c. 1086) — without which the marriage is invalid.
- In both cases the Catholic party must declare readiness to remove dangers of defecting from the faith and promise sincerely to do all in their power to have the children baptised and raised Catholic; the other party is **informed** of this promise but no longer required to make it (a significant softening from *Ne Temere*, made in *Matrimonia Mixta*, 1970).
