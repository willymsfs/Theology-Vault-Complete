# The Theological Vocabulary of George M. Soares-Prabhu, SJ
### A Research Glossary of Terms and Concepts, with His Own Definitions
*Compiled from the Collected Writings, Vols 1–4 (NotebookLM primary-source queries, 20 July 2026)*

---

## I. Foundational Concepts

**Abba experience** — Jesus's foundational experience of God as "unconditional love" and a "loving Parent." It is "radically liberating" because it fulfills the basic human need to be loved, freeing a person from finding fulfillment through possessing things or clinging to attachments. Rather than an abstract, all-pervading Absolute, *Abba* is the unconditionally loving Parent who gifts Himself to humankind. This experience destroys religious legalism and purity boundaries. Everything else in his theology derives from it.

**Dharma** — A "way of life"; a "complex blend of worldview and values of belief and prescription" that holds a community together. Traditionally defining caste obligation and social duty, he deliberately redefines it for the **dharma of Jesus**: "the complex of religious insight and ethical concern (of experience, world-view and value) which determines the life-style that Jesus proclaimed and practised" — a "dharma of unconditional and therefore of absolutely universal love."

**Spirituality** — "A way of life based on certain convictions and values (a dharma) which interacts creatively and appropriately with its historical context." Hence any genuine spirituality is a *contextualized* spirituality; mysticism and politics are inseparable.

**Kingdom of God (basileia tou theou)** — "God's definitive liberative intervention in human and cosmic history"; "the decisive salvific action through which God, finally 'liberating' creation, reveals God's self as King" (Mk 1:14–15). Stripped of other-worldly connotations: not heaven-after-death, not the Church. It creates a new society measured by three dimensions — **personal freedom, communitarian fellowship, structural justice** — the "parameters by which the arrival or the delay of God's reign is to be gauged."

**Salvation** — "Integral liberation": "eschatological but not other-worldly"; it comes from God but "is actualized in and through the struggles of the poor," freeing people from social, economic, and societal oppression as well as personal bondage.

**Liberation** — In the Indian context: "liberation from poverty arising out of material want and liberation from oppression and social ostracism" — always paired with inner liberation (see *moksha*). Total liberation = both, never one.

**Contextual theology** — "An intentional and thought-out effort to do theology in and for a given context" by those who belong to it. "To do theology means to contextualize one's faith." "Faith endures; but no theology is perennial!"

## II. The Poor and Social Analysis

**The poor / anawim ('anî / 'anāw) / ptochoi** — From a Hebrew root meaning "bent, bowed down, afflicted." He fiercely rejects spiritualizing the poor: they are the "sociologically poor" — "the economically destitute and the socially outcast," "all those who are in any way, and not just economically, deprived of the means or the dignity they need to lead a fully human existence" (Lk 6:20).

**Class (biblical)** — Against Marxist reduction, the biblical poor are defined by three propositions: a *sociological* group (defined by deprived social situation), a *dialectical* group (oppressed by antagonistic groups), and a *dynamic* group (through whom God shapes history).

**Option for the poor** — The defining element of biblical spirituality: the poor are "those on whose side God stands, and from whom God's salvation comes."

**Culture of silence** — An "alienated society" silenced and treated as the "mere object" of a dominant or colonizing power (adapted from Freire).

**Tinsel facade** — The "glittering but tinsel facade of Westernization" imposed by a privileged elite, hiding the overwhelming poverty of the "real India."

**Idolatry** — "The absolutization of things like money, kingship and temple or the reduction of God to an idol"; turning God into "the legitimizing principle of absolutized values like money power... or of absolutized institutions like the Temple or the state." The prime target of prophetic religious critique.

**Communalism** — The "turning in of religious communities upon themselves to form tightly knit defensive groups maintaining a hostile attitude" toward rival groups, fueled by insecurity; life perceived as competing religious communities. Countered by the "anticommunal Jesus" who abolished purity boundaries (Mk 7:1–23).

## III. Jesus: Life, Teaching, Praxis

**Jesus of faith** — Against both the "Jesus of history" of academic reconstruction and the "Christ of dogma" of Hellenistic formulas: "the Jesus (not the Christ) of faith (not of history)" — "the real Jesus who lived in Palestine... as he was encountered and experienced by his first followers." The starting point of Third World Christology.

**Discipleship** — "To be a disciple of Jesus means to experience God the way that Jesus experienced God." Adopting Jesus's worldview, not rule-following or ritual.

**Love commandment / agapē** — Not affection but "effective love," a "love of concern": "to do good to someone." "To love God means, concretely, to love neighbour." "In an unjust society agapē inevitably becomes a struggle for justice: it strives 'to set free the oppressed'."

**Miracle** — Not "compelling 'proofs'" of divinity but "'signs', indications perceptible to those disposed to receive them"; "a proclamation in action of the 'good news'"; signifying "the subversion of a whole oppressive power structure."

**Table fellowship** — Jesus's habit of eating with outcasts: an "acted parable, through which he brings home to listeners his experience of God as Abba the loving parent" (Mk 2:15ff). Enacts the Kingdom as community of grace against ritual purity — his direct weapon against caste.

**Conflict** — "Not a secondary dispensable element in the ministry of Jesus; it is of its essence." The inevitable result of exercising love in an unjust world.

**The Cross** — "No arbitrary intrusion into the life of Jesus. It is the natural outcome of a life of solidarity with the poor and the outcast and of confrontation with the powerful who oppress them."

**Forgiveness** — Paired with "a refusal to condemn"; a "necessary precondition for effective prayer"; "only the spiral of active forgiveness can break the spiral of violence that is tearing our world apart." Human forgiveness is the result and paradigm of experiencing God's unlimited forgiveness, never a way to earn it.

**Prayer** — Flows from the Abba experience: "Because the Christian experiences God as a loving Parent and relates to God as a trusting child, Christian prayer will be a child's prayer." The Lord's Prayer is "wholly a prayer for the Kingdom" — prayer commits to praxis.

**Metanoia** — Radical dislocation of familiar patterns of perception and behavior; a total turning of the human being toward God and neighbor.

**Liberative pedagogy** — Jesus's teaching style: "pedagogy of the oppressed with a strong commitment to societal liberation"; "nonélitist and dialogical, based on the equality of all before God"; involves **conscientization** — liberating people "from the manipulative myths which legitimized their oppressive and alienating society" and "making them conscious of their worth."

**Sermon on the Mount as dharma** — Read as juridical law it becomes "thoroughly utopian law" with impossible demands. As dharma: "proclaims freedom from the burden of the law, since it is grounded on an experience of God as abba who loves us his children with an unconditional love."

## IV. Mission and Church

**Mission** — "There is no uniform, unchanging, universal understanding of mission." It means "making God's love present in whatever way it can" to build God's Kingdom — never Church growth. "Mission is therefore concerned not with the power, security or growth of the missionizing Churches, but with the welfare of the missionized world."

**Witness** — The most crucial aspect of mission in India: mission is "not so much doing as being; not so much noisy proclamation as infectious witness."

**Proclamation / evangelization** — Proclamation is the verbal "announcing of the good news"; verbal evangelization is a "derivative part" of mission — an offering of love, not "conquest" or "sale."

**Conversion** — Personally: an "active commitment," an ongoing response to a new situation. Also his model for inculturation (below).

**Church-as-mission** — The Church exists not for itself but as "the symbol and the servant of God's Reign"; mission is the Church serving the world, not expanding itself.

**Colonial Christianity** — An "aggressive mission carried out under the patronage of the avaricious and ruthless colonial powers," treating people "as objects to be converted."

**Alienation** — The profound isolation of the Indian Church and its theology from the Indian people, intellectuals, and local religiosity — the direct legacy of colonialism. His signature movement: **from alienation to inculturation**.

**Inculturation** — "The passage of a religious tradition from one cultural situation to another, in a movement that resembles the conversion of a person from a situation of sin to a situation of salvation"; "throwing off an imposed, alien and alienating system of doctrine and worship, in order to allow Indian experiences of Jesus to find their unforced expression." The New Testament itself is the primary model.

**Exclusivism / inclusivism / pluralism** — *Exclusivism*: salvation "mediated exclusively through Jesus" and the visible Church. *Inclusivism*: Christ is the source of salvation for all, reaching people of good will without explicit faith. *Theocentric pluralism* (his position): other religions are "genuine ways of salvation and sources of revelation" revolving around God.

**Dialogue** — "Explicit interreligious dialogue, with all the risks and opportunities that such a dialogue involves": critical collaboration to realize Kingdom values, never paternalistic error-correction.

## V. Spirituality and Religious Life

**Contrast community** — A community showing "it is possible to live fruitful and fulfilled lives based on values which are the opposite of those proposed by the power and possession hungry consumer society in which we live"; where "all alienation is at an end and men and women live together as children of the one Father in heaven in freedom, fellowship and justice."

**Prophetic religious life** — Has a "twofold structure," "mystical" and "political"; religious communities exercise a "prophetic impact on the Church" and serve as "productive models for the Church as a whole."

**The three vows** — Communitarian "acts of self-liberation," not individual sacrifices. *Poverty*: "frees us from the tyranny of possessions"; "not just an individual's renunciation of possessions but the sharing that creates a community of sharing." *Chastity*: frees "from the pull of inordinate affections"; "the unrestricted love which fashions a community capable of loving." *Obedience*: frees "from the addictive need for achievement and the craving for power"; "commitment to God's will that enables a community to make common cause for the Kingdom." Summed: "We are freed from all things, in order that we might make ourselves slaves of all through love."

**Antigreed** — Indigenous tribal value mirroring Jesus's ethic: freedom from consumerism and accumulation (Mk 10:17–27).

**Antipride** — "Not self-abasement but fearless and active service" (Mk 10:35–45).

**Christian purusharthas** — The four classical Hindu goals of life (with the *asramas*, stages of life), used as frameworks to contextualize Christian spirituality and the vows — with the caution that exact equivalence is impossible across worldviews.

## VI. Hermeneutics: How to Read

**Pre-understanding** — The sociological and ideological commitment a reader brings to the text, consciously or not. There is no neutral standpoint: "no such thing as an autonomous, impartial, academic theology floating free above the realm of human options and biases."

**Hermeneutic circle** — An interpretive process that "always presupposes a profound human commitment," proceeding from the interpreter's life-experience to the text (via Segundo).

**Ideological / exegetical suspicion** — The "negative pole of the hermeneutical circle": questions accepted views of reality and drives the interpreter to read the text anew.

**Author meaning / intentionality** — "What the original author intended to say": an objective meaning historically locked in the text, recovered through historical criticism — which he retains as base and "control" against eisegesis.

**Eisegesis** — The "fanciful flights" of an interpreter who reads their own biases or "any interpretation at all" into a text. What sound historical criticism prevents.

**Historical-critical method (his verdict)** — "The pretended neutrality of Western exegesis and its historical critical method is thus... an illusion. The method as practised today is and always has been ideologically loaded" — functioning as "legitimisation of the capitalistic technocracy." Retained only as safeguard, never as end.

**Religious reading** — Applying "traditional methods of Indian exegesis to the Gospel text," transposing Hellenistic symbols into Indian ones. Two steps: assimilate one's own religious past by reading the Indian scriptures; then re-read the Gospel in that light.

**Social reading** — Reading the Bible "in the light of a liberating praxis among the socially oppressed."

**Integral Indian Reading** — The convergence of religious and social readings into one approach disclosing "the liberation announced by Jesus in its totality" — freedom from social oppression *and* from personal bondage (*moksha*).

**Intertextual reading** — Linking "biblical texts with texts from the other religious scriptures" for richer, mutually illuminating understanding.

**Dhvani / vyanjana** — From classical Indian poetics: *vyanjana* is language's capacity to communicate suggested meaning; *dhvani* is "the resonating effect of a word through which it evokes in the hearer deep meanings." Adapted as hermeneutical tools to draw out spiritual depth beyond the reach of the historical-critical method — "elicits deeper meaning in the hearer which otherwise would not have been perceived at all."

**Dalit reading** — Reading from the Dalit perspective: e.g. the Decalogue establishes "equality of all humans, whatever their status, before the law" — against the hierarchical Laws of Manu.

**The Indian mind** — "Context-sensitive rather than context-free"; experiences reality as an interrelated, interdependent whole; "inclusive, cosmocentric, symbolic, and practical"; prefers the "logic of complementarity (both/and) rather than the Aristotelian way of exclusion (either/or)." "Because of its passion for wholeness the Indian mind is prepared to risk the chance of error rather than the loss of any part of truth."

**Double-distancing** — Indian theology must distance itself both from "the academic theology of the West" and from "traditional Indian forms which may blunt its liberative thrust" (elitist ashram spiritualities).

**Two-eyed vision / elliptical truth** — Truth is "elliptical," made of "contradictory facets which cannot be apprehended adequately from any single point of view"; requires complementary perspectives (Asian and Semitic religious experience together).

**Prophet-as-theologian** — Theology modeled on the biblical prophets: rooted in a "concrete historical situation," not abstract exegesis; a two-fold protest — religious critique (idolatry) and social critique (exploitation and oppression); begins with social analysis, ends in social change.

**Praxis** — An authentic "fine blend of action and reflection": "to speak a true word is to transform the world" (via Freire). The terminus of all interpretation.

**Conscientization** — See liberative pedagogy: freeing people from oppressive myths by "making them conscious of their worth."

## VII. Terms He Handles Critically

**Advaita (as hermeneutic)** — Warns against non-dualist readings (e.g. of John) so totalizing that "the distinct, historical, and prophetic voice of the Bible is drowned out" — his critique of Osho Rajneesh's Jesus.

**Ashram spirituality** — "A brahmanical spirituality, the spirituality of an affluent, privileged class... a mockery for the five hundred millions of our people who subsist below, on, or just above the poverty line."

**Western academic exegesis** — "Academic barrenness"; "ruinous heap of hypothetical possibilities"; exegetes "fiddle while Rome burns"; seminary training produces "a trained incapacity to deal with the real problems of actual living persons in their daily lives."

**Latin American liberation theology (limits)** — Right on structures, blind to Asia's religiosity: a theology that "focuses almost exclusively on societal change, will not therefore satisfy Asian aspirations." Must integrate *moksha*.

**Moksha** — The Hindu concept of final liberation, which he affirms and integrates: unconditioned personal freedom through detachment from greed, illusion, and material anxiety — the "freedom from personal bondage" that structural liberation alone cannot give.

## VIII. Additions from the Full Volume 2 (added 20 July 2026)

**Form criticism / "spokenness"** — Studying biblical literary forms "recovers the 'spokenness' of God's Word": the text ceases to be a dead book and becomes a direct, urgent address summoning the reader to personal encounter.

**Prophetic language** — The prophet's speech "breaks down the structures of the dominant society and empowers the dominated to structure an alternative community." Social analysis driven by a God-given "alternative vision."

**Temple (critique)** — Solomon's Temple as political legitimation: it domesticated Yahweh and "effectively reverses the movement of the Mosaic revolution," replacing an economics of equality and a politics of justice with affluence, forced labor, and imperial oppression. Institutionalization as the standing threat to liberative faith.

**Inspiration of non-Christian scriptures** — The OT is inspired "not in itself but inasmuch as it is illumined by the New"; by the same paradigm, since Christ the Word enlightens everyone, "the inspiration of non-Christian scriptures is a distinct possibility."

**Gospels as "history interpreted through faith"** — Not objective biographies but a "mosaic" of traditions revealing the religious significance of Jesus's life.

**Sonship and Spirit (twin poles)** — "Sonship (the experience of God as abba, Father) and Spirit (the experience of the eschatological power of God working in and through him) were in fact the twin poles of Jesus' experience of God." The Spirit marked not by ecstatic enthusiasm but by "the authority of his teaching, and the power of his healings and exorcisms."

**Johannine sutra / sacred in the secular** — Jn 1:14 read as sutra with three implications: christologically it "decisively excludes any sort of epiphanic or avatara type of Christianity"; anthropologically God is met by serving the neighbor in the secular; cosmically, "the sacred interpenetrates the secular (as the soul interpenetrates the body), and is accessible only through it."

**Dhvani reading (worked model, Mk 4:35–41)** — Take the narrative as it stands; read its oppositions (great storm/great calm; sleeping Jesus/panicking disciples; passive passenger/commanding Lord) until they evoke a limit-experience: "the experience of ultimate stability behind the turmoil of our agitated and threatened existence."

**Iconoclasm as the dark side of monotheism** — The anti-idol satire of Isa 44 is a "damaging and therefore sinful misrepresentation of a people's religion and culture": idolaters worship the divine that "dwells" in the image, as "bread and wine in the Christian Eucharist mediate the 'real presence' of Jesus." The Bible's "fanatical iconoclasm" has issued historically in "devastating destruction of peoples and cultures."

**Priesthood (Indian, biblical)** — Against a purely cultic, sacralized priesthood: the priest enters the Kingdom movement that "continually subverts a world structured... by relationships of oppression" toward relationships of freedom and love.

**Mary as eschatological Daughter of Sion** — Luke's Annunciation fuses OT call-narrative and birth oracle; Mary personifies the faithful remnant of Israel.
