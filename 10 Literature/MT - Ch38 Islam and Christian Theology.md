# Ch. 38 — Islam and Christian Theology
*Chapter author: Ataullah Siddiqui*

## Core Idea
Siddiqui traces Christian theology's engagement with Islam from confrontational missiology toward collaborative dialogue, arguing a genuine theology of religions must be grounded in the postcolonial reality of Christians and Muslims as literal neighbours, not an academic abstraction.

## Detailed Explanation
Large-scale postcolonial migration created genuinely multi-religious European neighbourhoods, turning Christian-Muslim dialogue from an academic luxury into an urgent pastoral and political necessity. Karl Rahner's description of Islam as a "concrete monotheism" — a real, valid adoration of the one God, though lacking trinitarian differentiation — helped open Vatican II's own more positive posture in Nostra Aetate and Lumen Gentium toward Islamic monotheism, Mary, and the shared hope of the Day of Judgement. Kenneth Cragg's The Event of the Quran reshaped Christian scholarship by treating the Quran as a literary-historical event and taking Muhammad's character and achievement seriously on its own terms rather than through inherited polemic.

## Example
The 1954 Bhamdoun Consultation in Lebanon, where Muslims and Christians first formally organized to resist the joint expulsion of religion and moral values from modern public life, is the chapter's clearest concrete instance of interfaith cooperation moving from private theological reflection into organized public action.

## Illustration
Keith Ward's proposal that Christians who already accept the Hebrew Bible as divinely inspired (theopneustos, "God-breathed") should logically be open to recognizing a comparable inspired status for the Quran works like extending a courtesy you already extend to one older sibling's writing to a younger sibling's writing on the same grounds — consistency, not compromise, is the argument's own logic.

## Key Terms
- **Concrete Monotheism**: Rahner's term for Islamic monotheism: a real, valid adoration of the one God, though without trinitarian differentiation.
- **Cragg's Corner**: Siddiqui's term for the conceptual meeting-place Kenneth Cragg established by treating the Quran as a literary-historical event worthy of serious Christian engagement.
- **Theopneustos** (Greek) — "God-breathed / divinely inspired": Keith Ward's category, applied by extension to the Quran given Christians already grant it to the Hebrew Bible.
- **Bhamdoun Consultation**: The 1954 Lebanon gathering where Muslims and Christians first organized jointly to resist religion's expulsion from public life.

## Connections
Barth and Emil Brunner (Part I) represent early neo-orthodox resistance, rejecting Islamic monotheism as a merely human "religion." Hans Küng's "global ethics" project (Part II.D) became a major dialogical bridge. Rahner's transcendental inclusivism (Part I) directly underpinned Nostra Aetate and Lumen Gentium's positive posture toward Islam. Keith Ward and John Hick (this Part, previous chapter) represent the pluralist transition Siddiqui situates this dialogue against.
