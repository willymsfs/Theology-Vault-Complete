# Unit 7 — From Trent to the Personalist Turn

## The Legal Consolidation

- **Ne Temere** (Pius X, 1907) — canonical form required universally for Catholics, resolving *Tametsi*'s patchwork. Its stringency in mixed marriages became a significant source of friction with Protestants, especially in Ireland and Germany.
- **The 1917 Code** codified the classical framework: **c. 1013 §1** stated that "the **primary end** of marriage is the procreation and education of offspring; the **secondary end** is mutual help and the remedy for concupiscence," and that the **essential properties** are unity and indissolubility.

## Casti Connubii (Pius XI, 31 December 1930)

Issued in direct response to the **Anglican Lambeth Conference of 1930**, which had cautiously permitted contraception in limited circumstances — the first Christian body to do so.

The encyclical reaffirms the three Augustinian goods; declares contraception "**intrinsically vicious**"; condemns eugenic sterilisation and abortion; and upholds indissolubility. It also, notably, contains genuinely warm language about **conjugal love** as "the chief reason and purpose of matrimony, if matrimony be looked at not in the restricted sense as instituted for the proper conception and education of the child, but more widely as the blending of life as a whole" — a sentence the personalists would seize upon.

## The Personalist Challenge

The most important pre-conciliar development, and one you should be able to narrate.

- **Dietrich von Hildebrand**, *Marriage* (1929), and **Herbert Doms**, *The Meaning of Marriage* (1935), argued that the **hierarchy of ends distorts the reality**: the mutual self-giving union of the spouses is not a *means* to procreation but the very **meaning** of marriage, from which procreation flows. Doms in particular argued that the traditional scheme reduced the spouses to instruments of the species.
- **The reaction was swift.** In **1944 the Holy Office** issued a decree reaffirming the traditional hierarchy and stating that the secondary ends are not "equally principal and independent" of the primary — and in 1951 Pius XII repeated the point.
- **Yet the argument was not defeated**, and its substance would be substantially received at Vatican II, which pointedly declined to repeat the hierarchy (Unit 8).

**Teaching point.** This is a clean case of a position condemned in one decade being substantially incorporated in the next generation — and a useful check on any account of doctrinal development that expects a straight line.
