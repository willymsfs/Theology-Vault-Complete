# Ch. 33 — Eastern Orthodox Theology
*Chapter author: Rowan Williams*

## Core Idea
Twentieth-century Orthodox theology recovers the patristic and Byzantine heritage — apophaticism, relational ontology, eucharistic ecclesiology — presenting God's being as a relational communion of love and true knowledge of God as contemplative participation (theosis) in the divine energies, not rationalistic mastery.

## Detailed Explanation
The 1917 Russian Revolution's expulsion of intellectuals made Paris, and specifically the Institut Saint-Serge (founded 1925), the centre of Orthodox theological revival. Émigré theologians split into two camps: Sergei Bulgakov's "Sophianic" school, pursuing a speculative, romantic-idealist metaphysics of divine wisdom, and Vladimir Lossky's and Georges Florovsky's "Neopatristic synthesis," seeking a rigorous return to the Greek Fathers and Gregory Palamas's essence-energies distinction. Rowan Williams, a leading Western scholar of this tradition (and later Archbishop of Canterbury), traces how it moved Orthodox theology from abstract school-theology toward presenting the Church itself as the living, sacramental image of the Trinity.

## Example
Vladimir Lossky's insistence that theology is completed not in a system of correct propositions but in ekstasis — the contemplative "shock" of encountering the unmasterable living God — concretely shapes Orthodox liturgical practice, where icons and apophatic (negative) theological language deliberately resist offering a fully graspable, controllable picture of God.

## Illustration
Western scholastic theology often works like assembling a complete, correctly-labelled model of God from available parts. Lossky's apophatic Orthodoxy works more like standing before a brilliant light so bright you cannot look directly at it — you know something real and overwhelming is there precisely because ordinary sight fails, not despite it.

## Key Terms
- **Sobornost** (Russian: соборность) — "conciliarity / organic unity-in-diversity": The communal unity by which the Church, through the Spirit's eucharistic gathering, becomes the living image of the Trinity, overcoming individualist isolation.
- **Bogochelovechestvo** (Russian: богочеловечество) — "divine humanity / Godmanhood": Soloviev's and Bulgakov's term for the cosmic process by which the Incarnation reconciles and deifies human personality and the material cosmos.
- **Ekstasis** (Greek: ἔκστασις) — "ecstasy / stepping-out": In Lossky's apophatic theology, the contemplative self-transcendence by which the believer is "shocked" past conceptual control into encounter with the living God.
- **Podvig** (Russian: подвиг) — "ascetic struggle / achievement": Florovsky's category for Christ's history as a concrete, free ascetic triumph over mortality and sin, not a static metaphysical state.

## Connections
Florovsky's 1931 debate with Barth (Part I) sharpened his insistence on revelation's historical priority. Lossky maintained close ties with de Lubac and the nouvelle théologie (Part I). Von Balthasar (Part I) is the closest Western analogue to Bulgakov's dramatic, kenotic trinitarian theology. Moltmann (Part II.A) explicitly incorporated the Cappadocian "social trinitarianism" John Zizioulas synthesized from this tradition.
