# Part 8 — Global Shift — The World Church

Yves Congar described the Council as "a decentralization of the *urbs* [Rome] on the *orbis* [world]"; Karl Rahner named it the Church's first self-realization as a genuine **"World Church" (*Weltkirche*)**.

- **Latin America** — 22% of the fathers (versus negligible presence at Vatican I); **CELAM** (est. 1955) organized continental collegiality; **Medellín (1968)** received *Gaudium et Spes* regionally and coined the **"preferential option for the poor."**
- **Africa** — Cardinal Laurean Rugambwa argued successfully for indigenous liturgical inculturation; laid groundwork for **AMECEA** and **SECAM**.
- **Asia** — led directly to the **Federation of Asian Bishops' Conferences (FABC, 1972)** and its "theology of the local Church" built on a triple dialogue with religions, cultures, and the poor. Key Asian-reception literature: Kroeger, Trisco/Komonchak, Avery Dulles, Denis Hurley, Francisco Claver, Peter C. Phan.

Lasting global effects: liturgical pluralism ending a millennium of Tridentine uniformity; *Dignitatis Humanae* dismantling *Staatskirchentum*; *Nostra Aetate* mandating permanent interreligious dialogue; a theological "incubator" effect producing Latin American liberation theology, Dalit and Minjung theologies, indigenous inculturation, and feminist/*mujerista*/ecofeminist theologies — all drawing on *Lumen Gentium*'s baptismal ecclesiology.

## Connection to Contemporary Synodality

Under Francis, this trajectory continues through **synodality**, retrieving the first-millennium principle *"what concerns all must be examined and approved by all."* Francis has largely bypassed the academic continuity/discontinuity debate, implementing the Council's trajectories (preferential option for the poor, ecumenical openness, outward orientation) through **gesture and pastoral action** — a "generative reception" rather than textual citation.
