# Unit 6 — Models of the Church: Avery Dulles

## Why Models? Why More Than One?
For Dulles, the Church is a mystery: no single concept can fully capture it. Theologians must therefore work with multiple, complementary models simultaneously — "a kind of mental juggling act... no supermodel does full justice to all aspects of the Church." Dulles proposes three criteria any model must satisfy: (1) basis in Scripture, (2) basis in Christian tradition, and (3) the capacity to give members a genuine sense of corporate identity and mission.

## The Five Models
Institution — the Church as visible structure and juridical authority of offices (Bellarmine's societas perfecta, "perfect society"). Strength: strong recent magisterial endorsement and a strong sense of corporate identity. Weakness: weak scriptural and patristic basis; prone to exactly the clericalism, juridicism, and triumphalism named in Unit 5; an obstacle to ecumenism.

Mystical Communion — the Church as interior, Spirit-given koinonia (Pauline Body of Christ, People of God). Strength: strong biblical foundation; ecumenically fruitful. Weakness: can leave unclear why anyone must remain loyal to visible, institutional structures at all.

Sacrament — the Church as a visible sign that truly embodies and transmits the invisible grace it signifies (cf. LG 1). Strength: synthesizes institution and communion, securing loyalty while remaining open to reform ("the Church is continually called to become a better sign of Christ than it has been"). Weakness: can appear complacent, insufficiently prophetic against structural sin.

Herald — the Church as kerygmatic community gathered by the proclaimed Word (the OT prophetic tradition; Barth and Bultmann's dialectical theology). Strength: strong biblical foundation, a clear missionary identity, a rich theology of the Word as living address. Weakness: weak on the incarnational and sacramental dimensions; can reduce to bare verbal proclamation.

Servant — the Church as diaconal community modelled on the Suffering Servant, working for justice and healing in solidarity with the world. Strength: deeply relevant to contemporary struggles, bridging Church and world. Weakness: risks secularizing into a mere social agency, losing its supernatural, Christocentric core.

## The Integrating Model: Community of Disciples
In a later essay, Dulles proposed "community of disciples" as a way to harmonize the other five: the Church as a visible, structured fellowship of intentional followers gathered around Jesus as Lord and Teacher, under the Spirit's ongoing guidance. Strength: broadly inclusive, building bridges to all five other models and preventing "communion" from collapsing into mere mutual gratification. Weakness: it too is only one more perspective, not a supermodel exhausting the Church's mystery.

## Illustration
Dulles's models work like different camera lenses on the same landscape: a wide-angle lens (institution) shows the boundaries and paths clearly but flattens the depth; a macro lens (communion) shows the intimate texture but loses the wider shape. No single photograph does justice to the whole terrain; a competent theologian needs the whole camera bag.

## Indian Context
In the Indian pastoral situation — a small, often vulnerable minority alongside vast religious plurality — the servant and herald models often carry special weight (the Church as a visible sign of justice for Dalits and Adivasis, and as witness amid religious pluralism), while the institutional model's triumphalist overtones sit especially awkwardly in a minority context.
