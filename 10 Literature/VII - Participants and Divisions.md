# Part 5 — Participants and Divisions

## Major Coalitions

- **The European Alliance ("Rhine Group")** — Germany, Austria, Switzerland, France, Netherlands, Belgium — the Council's most organized force, expanding into a **World Alliance** coordinating 65 episcopal conferences via weekly meetings at the *Domus Mariae*.
- **The Conservative Roman Curia Bloc** — controlled the original 75 preparatory schemata; defended Latin liturgy, monarchical papal structure, formal dogmatic definition.
- **Coetus Internationalis Patrum** — formed by Archbishops **Geraldo de Proença Sigaud** (Brazil) and **Marcel Lefebvre**; ran an independent press; drafted substitute schemata.
- **The Bishops' Secretariat** — founded November 1963 by Archbishop Pacifico Perantoni; mobilized 679 signatures to force a separate chapter "On Religious."
- **The St Paul's Conference** — English-language moderate opposition founded by Archbishop **John Heenan** of Westminster.

## The Periti

- **Edward Schillebeeckx, O.P.** — anonymously authored a 1,500-copy critique of the Curial drafts, distributed October 1962.
- **Karl Rahner, S.J.** — *peritus* to Cardinal König; also active member of the drafting commissions for **both** *Christus Dominus* **and** *Gaudium et Spes*. Co-authored, with the young **Joseph Ratzinger**, the alternative revelation schema *De revelatione Dei et hominis in Jesu Christo facta*, circulated in the critical early weeks. His **"World Church" thesis** divides Christian history into three macro-epochs: brief Jewish-Christianity, nearly two millennia of European/Hellenistic Christianity (the Church as an "export firm"), and the genuinely global "World Church" era inaugurated by Vatican II itself.
- **Joseph Ratzinger** — theologian to Frings; translated Rahner's theology into strategy for German bishops.
- **Hans Küng** — declared the rejection of the Curial drafts a "change of atmosphere."
- **John Courtney Murray, S.J.** — chief architect of *Dignitatis Humanae*.
- **Gérard Philips** — drafted the moderate compromise text underlying *Lumen Gentium*.

## Primary-Source Voices: The Council in Its Own Words

The collection *Council Speeches of Vatican II* (ed. Küng, Congar, O'Hanlon, Dec 1963) organizes selections around Paul VI's four-point program. Notable interventions:

- **Cardinal Léon Suenens** — the Spirit gives charisms to *all* Christians; proposed admitting women and religious as lay auditors.
- **Bishop Emile-Joseph De Smedt** — grounded lay participation in baptismal incorporation into Christ's threefold office; delivered the pivotal intervention defining religious liberty positively as freedom to follow conscience, negatively as immunity from coercion.
- **Patriarch Maximos IV Saigh** — *"the only head of the Church... is our Lord Jesus Christ, and he alone"* — primacy as service.
- **Bishop Ernest Primeau** — rejected treating laypeople as reducible to *"believe, pray, obey and pay."*
- **Archbishop Eugene D'Souza** — demanded Curial reform: *"unity" must not be confused with "uniformity."*
- **Archbishop Léon-Arthur Elchinger** — ecumenism requires confessing Reformers sometimes championed neglected truths (justification by faith).
- **Cardinal J. Humberto Quintero** — confessed Catholic clergy's own share of blame for the Reformation-era rupture.
- **Cardinal Augustin Bea** — framed the Jewish-relations decree as strictly religious, demanding the eradication of antisemitism.

## Voting Patterns Across the Sessions

- **20 Nov 1962:** 62% vote to reject *De Fontibus* — short of two-thirds, overridden by papal intervention.
- **29 Oct 1963:** Combining the Marian schema into *De Ecclesia* passes **1,114-1,074** — a 17-vote margin.
- **30 Oct 1963:** Collegiality straw vote passes **1,717-408**.
- **4 Dec 1965:** 483 negative votes force clarification of the nuclear-deterrence passage in *Gaudium et Spes*.
- **7 Dec 1965:** *Gaudium et Spes* finally passes **2,309-75**.

## The Rising Laity — A Fourth Major Theme

For roughly 1,500 years the laity was defined negatively ("not clergy, not monks"). **Pius X's 1906 encyclical *Vehementer Nos*** called the Church an "unequal society" whose multitude's duty is to be led as a "docile flock." **Yves Congar's 1953 *Jalons pour une théologie du laïcat*** (*Lay People in the Church*) provided the first systematic theology of the laity. At **Cardinal Suenens's July 1963 suggestion**, the fathers voted to place "People of God" (LG ch. 2) before hierarchy. *Lumen Gentium* §§10, 31 grounds this in the **common priesthood of all believers**; theologian **Paul Lakeland** later summarized: *"the layperson, not the ordained, is the 'default Christian.'"* *Apostolicam Actuositatem* and *Gaudium et Spes* cash this out practically: the laity as **"leaven"** transforming work, family, politics from within.

**Post-conciliar irony:** the old, tightly hierarchical Catholic Action model *collapsed* in membership after the Council even as its theological premises were vindicated, while wholly new **ecclesial movements** (Communion and Liberation, Sant'Egidio, Focolare, the Neocatechumenal Way) flourished instead.
