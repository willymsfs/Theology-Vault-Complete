# Ch. 24 — Pastoral and Practical Theology
*Chapter author: Stephen Pattison and Gordon Lynch*

## Core Idea
Since WWII, practical theology has moved from an "applicationist" model (passing down ministerial hints and tips) to a rigorous three-way critical conversation between lived contemporary experience, theological tradition, and other academic disciplines — treating lived experience as real data able to challenge and revise received doctrine, not merely receive it.

## Detailed Explanation
Anton Boisen, a mental-health chaplain whose own psychological crises led him to found the Clinical Pastoral Education movement, first urged ministers to read "living human documents" — actual lived experience treated as seriously as historic written texts. Seward Hiltner and Don Browning at Chicago then gave the field its liberal-rational academic structure: Hiltner's distinction between "operation-centered" disciplines (conclusions drawn a posteriori from practice) and "logic-centered" disciplines (systematic theology's textual/theoretical starting point), and Browning's four-stage "revised correlational method" (descriptive, historical, systematic, strategic) seeking a genuine "fusion of horizons" between practice and normative text.

## Example
James Poling's and Edward Wimberly's "radical-liberationist" practical theology — incorporating power, race, and disability directly into pastoral method, rather than assuming a supposedly neutral liberal-rational starting point — shows practical theology's most concrete recent turn: pastoral care itself becomes a site of critical, not just comforting, theological work.

## Illustration
The older "applicationist" model of pastoral theology worked like a cooking show: here is the finished, tested recipe (doctrine), now here is how to plate and serve it in a hospital room or a funeral home. Boisen's "living human documents" approach flips the kitchen around: the actual, specific ingredients in front of you (a real person's suffering, in all its mess) get to change the recipe itself, not just how it is served.

## Key Terms
- **Living Human Documents**: Boisen's category treating actual lived human experience, especially in distress, as a text as theologically rich as historic written Christian sources.
- **Operation-Centered Disciplines**: Hiltner's term for theological fields (like pastoral theology) whose conclusions emerge from reflection on acts and practice, contrasted with "logic-centered" systematic theology.
- **Phronesis** (Greek) — "practical wisdom": The neo-Aristotelian goal (via MacIntyre) Browning retrieves for practical theology: constructive, moral living over abstract technical or dogmatic certainty.
- **Revised Correlational Method**: Browning's four-stage method (descriptive, historical, systematic, strategic) seeking a fusion of horizons between contemporary practice and normative Christian texts.

## Connections
Critiques Schleiermacher's older, deductive "applicationist" model. Hiltner's and Browning's method draws directly on Tillich's correlation and David Tracy's revised correlation (Part I, Part II.C). Henri Nouwen and Thomas Oden represent a "neo-traditional confessional" reaction retrieving patristic pastoral wisdom. Moltmann's and Metz's political theology (Part II.A, II.D) directly shaped later liberationist practical theology.
