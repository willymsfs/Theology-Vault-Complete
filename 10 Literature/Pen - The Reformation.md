# Unit 8 — The Reformation

## The Reformers' Objections

Four objections, which every exam answer should be able to state and which Trent will answer point by point:

1. **Enumeration is impossible and produces despair.** Requiring confession of all mortal sins by species and number binds consciences to an impossible standard. Calvin is scathing: the requirement is "a torture of consciences" (*Institutes* III.4), since no one can number their sins any more than they can number the hairs on their head.
2. **Satisfaction impugns Christ's satisfaction.** If Christ has made full satisfaction, human works of satisfaction detract from its sufficiency.
3. **Attrition is servile.** Sorrow motivated by fear of punishment is not repentance at all but self-interest — Luther calls attrition the sorrow of a gallows-penitent.
4. **Priestly absolution usurps God's prerogative** when construed as a **judicial** act rather than the **declaration** of a forgiveness God grants directly to faith.

## The Positions

| Figure | Position |
|---|---|
| **Luther** | Wavered on whether penance is a sacrament — in *The Babylonian Captivity* (1520) he lists it as one of three, then withdraws it for lacking a divinely instituted visible sign, leaving baptism and the Supper. But he **retained private confession and absolution** enthusiastically as a means of consolation, calling it a precious gift; what he rejected was compulsory enumeration, satisfaction, and the juridical framing. Absolution is the **word of the gospel applied to the individual** |
| **Calvin** | Denies it is a sacrament (*Institutes* III.4, IV.19); attacks enumeration and satisfaction sharply; retains and indeed strengthens **ecclesiastical discipline** through the consistory, and permits voluntary private confession to a minister for the troubled conscience |
| **Anglican** | The *Book of Common Prayer* provides a **General Confession** and absolution in the daily offices and Communion; auricular confession is retained as **voluntary** — the Exhortation invites anyone who "cannot quiet his own conscience" to seek a minister for counsel and absolution. The classic formula: "**All may, none must, some should**" |
| **Anabaptists** | Reject sacramental penance; practise the **ban** and shunning as community discipline |

**Teaching point — note the irony parallel to the Eucharist.** Luther is in some respects *closer* to Catholic practice than Calvin on private confession and absolution, which he wanted retained in Lutheran churches and which survived in Lutheran orders for centuries. The Reformation dispute was never simply "confession versus no confession"; it was about **compulsion, enumeration, satisfaction, and the juridical character of absolution**.
