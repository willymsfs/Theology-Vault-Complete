# The Dharma of Jesus (Anthology)

Nineteen essays edited by **Francis X. D'Sa SJ** (Orbis, 2003), arranged Christologically rather than by volume.

## Central thesis

The historical and prophetic message of Jesus has been **obscured by centuries of ahistorical, Western dogmatic theology**. Read with historical-critical rigour *and* hermeneutical openness to the experience of the poor, the Gospels reveal a Jesus whose central message is the **Kingdom of God** — the historical irruption of God's unconditional love. That foundational experience liberates from internal greed *and* external oppressive structures, setting off "a dynamic spiral of personal and societal liberation characterised by **freedom, fellowship, and justice**."

## Why *dharma* rather than "law"

*Dharma* circles etymologically around **"holding, holding together, bearing, supporting"** — "the complex of relationships that one thing has with every other thing," the "principle of being and acting." His definition: "that complex blend of worldview and values, of beliefs and prescriptions, which **'holds together' the followers of Jesus**, and integrates them into a recognisable community."

He rejects **law** for its extrinsic, legalistic connotations — arbitrary rules binding formally because commanded, producing "a striving after *works*" under guilt and scrupulosity. He rejects **ethic** as detached and individualistic. The positive alternative is ***svadharma*** (*Bhagavadgita* XVIII.47): action flowing spontaneously from one's own being as a child of God.

**The missionary payoff:** *dharma* names the whole relationship between "God, World, and Man," letting the Gospel be presented so that any person of goodwill in a multireligious culture can appropriate it without having to "become spiritually a Semite" or "intellectually a Greek."

## The Beatitudes

Luke's "Blessed are you **poor**" is the authentic prophetic utterance; *ptochos* denotes one lacking even necessities, who must beg. **Matthew spiritualised** it. The *anawim* are "all those who are oppressed or diminished" — in India today "the Harijans [Dalits], daily victims of inhuman atrocities... pavement dwellers... landless labourers."

Crucially: the poor are **not** blessed because poverty is a virtue — poverty is dehumanising and evil. They are blessed "**precisely because it is an evil which [Jesus] is about to bring to an end**."

## The antitheses

**"Focal instances"**, not exhaustive statutes — shocking graphic examples awakening the imagination. Equating anger with murder and the lustful look with adultery moves everything to "the innermost intentions of the heart," so that "no one can claim to be without sin and venture to cast the first stone." They project a society "where violence is eradicated at its roots... where women are no longer treated as sex objects... [where] transparency of inter-human relationships makes oaths unnecessary."
