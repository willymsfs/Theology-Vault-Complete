# Ch. 06 — Hans Urs von Balthasar
*Chapter author: Ben Quash*

## Core Idea
Balthasar re-founded theology on the transcendental of beauty rather than on modern human subjectivity: theology must begin from the "form" (Gestalt) of Christ's self-revelation, perceived in contemplation, and unfolded as a cosmic drama (Theodrama) of divine and human freedom.

## Detailed Explanation
A Swiss Jesuit who studied German literature before ordination, Balthasar unusually left the Jesuit order and never held a university post, working instead as a chaplain, translator, and publisher. His theology was shaped decisively by his relationship with the mystic and physician Adrienne von Speyr, whose visions of Christ's descent into hell on Holy Saturday supplied central themes for his own systematic work; together they founded a secular institute, the Johannesgemeinschaft. His trilogy — The Glory of the Lord (aesthetics), Theo-Drama (dramatics), Theo-Logic (logic) — proceeds in that order deliberately: one must first perceive the beautiful "form" of revelation before entering its dramatic action and finally its logical articulation, reversing the usual order of modern theological method. He died in 1988 days before he was to be made a cardinal.

## Example
Balthasar's Mysterium Paschale develops his Holy Saturday theology — Christ's descent into the God-forsaken "nothingness" of the dead, drawn from von Speyr's visions — into a full account of atonement that has become a distinctive reference point in later debates about divine suffering, directly paralleling and contesting Moltmann's theology of the cross (Part II.A).

## Illustration
Most modern theology approaches faith like reading an instruction manual: work through the argument, arrive at belief. Balthasar treats faith more like standing before a great painting or hearing a symphony: you are first arrested and moved by its sheer beauty and form, and only from that experience of being "struck" does understanding — and then reasoned articulation — properly follow.

## Key Terms
- **Gestalt** (German) — "form / shape": The objective, beautiful "form" in which God is self-revealed in Christ, to be perceived through contemplation before it is analysed.
- **Sendung** (German) — "mission": Christ's person and saving work coincide perfectly in his mission — who he is and what he does for salvation are one and the same.
- **Theodrama** — "the drama of God": God's interaction with human freedom understood as a genuine, dramatic, unscripted-feeling encounter, centred on Christ's passion, death, and resurrection.
- **Triduum Mortis** (Latin) — "the three days of death": Good Friday through Easter Sunday, especially Christ's descent into the utter God-forsakenness of Holy Saturday — a central, distinctive motif drawn from von Speyr's mysticism.

## Connections
Mentored by de Lubac (previous chapter), whose ressourcement method he adopted; engaged for decades with Barth, taking from him the sovereignty of divine initiative while filtering it through a Catholic, analogical, and ecclesial lens; a fierce critic of Rahner's "anonymous Christianity" (in his polemical book Cordula); and his theology of the suffering Son connects directly to Moltmann's theology of the cross.
