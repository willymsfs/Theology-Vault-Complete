# Klima (ed.), *The Metaphysics and Theology of the Eucharist* — Overview

Gyula Klima (ed.), *The Metaphysics and Theology of the Eucharist: A Historical-Analytic Survey of the Problems of the Sacrament* (Studies on Nature, Mind and Action, vol. 10). Twenty chapters, roughly a millennium of argument, from the *Corpus Dionysiacum* through contemporary analytic philosophy of religion. Read via NotebookLM, 16 August 2026.

## The central problem
Not only theological but metaphysical: [[Transubstantiation]] and the [[Real Presence]] appear to require [[Accidens Sine Subjecto|accidents existing without a subject]], which classical Aristotelian metaphysics rules out as a contradiction in terms. The volume traces how successive thinkers repaired, strained, and eventually abandoned the classical framework trying to state the doctrine coherently.

## The through-line (Chapters 6–17, chiefly)
See [[The Scholastic Fracture - Aquinas to Luther on the Fate of the Bread]] for the full progression: [[Aquinas]] (via [[Avicenna]]) → [[Duns Scotus]] → [[Ockham]] → [[John Buridan]] → [[Luther]]. Each successive thinker's fix for the previous account's weak point required bending Aristotle's categories further, until the system was strained enough that Luther felt free to reject the framework rather than repair it.

## Contemporary revival (Chapters 18–19)
The debate did not end with the Reformation. Two live twenty-first-century defences: Ross McCullough's [[Emergence|transformational emergence]] account, and Joshua Hochschild's argument for recovering [[Eucharistic Semantics - Realist Versus Nominalist|realist semantics]] against modern nominalism.

## Full chapter survey
Twenty chapters cover, in addition to the through-line above: the Christology of the *Corpus Dionysiacum* under Origenist pressure (Perczel); Byzantine–Latin disputes over azymes and the epiclesis (Geréby); Armenian scholastic synthesis (Contin); Bonaventure's alternative solution to substance-less accidents (Afonso); Aquinas's semantics of the words of consecration (Crimi); Giles of Rome and Scotus's reification of accidents (Cross); Wyclif and the Hussite reform movements (Levy); the Jesuit Benet Perera against the Thomist essence/existence distinction (Ventimiglia); Descartes's failed attempt to reconcile mechanistic physics with the doctrine (Schmal); and Andrew Pinsent's second-person relational reading of the Mass.
