# Ch. 35 — Evangelical Theology
*Chapter author: David F. Wells*

## Core Idea
Evangelical theology is organized around sola scriptura (Scripture as reality's normative interpretive framework) and solus Christus (atonement as penal substitution), asserting the transcendent God has objectively revealed Godself in history and requires personal conversion and active global evangelization, against both liberal relativism and sacramental clericalism.

## Detailed Explanation
Emerging from the early-twentieth-century fundamentalist-modernist controversy, post-WWII "neo-evangelicalism" built serious scholarly infrastructure (Tyndale House, Cambridge) and multi-volume systematic theologies (Carl F. H. Henry, Millard Erickson). The movement organized itself globally and non-hierarchically through the 1974 Lausanne Congress on World Evangelization, producing the Lausanne Covenant (drafted by John Stott) to coordinate theology, social responsibility, and mission across denominations and nations. The 1978 Chicago Statement on Biblical Inerrancy, co-led by J. I. Packer, gave the movement's commitment to Scripture's total truthfulness its classic formal statement.

## Example
David Wells's own trilogy (No Place for Truth, 1993; God in the Wasteland, 1994; Above All Earthly Pow'rs, 2004) argues that consumerism and therapeutic psychology, not liberal theology alone, have quietly eroded Evangelicalism's confidence in objective truth from within — a sustained cultural diagnosis that shaped Evangelical seminary curricula across North America.

## Illustration
If liberal revisionist theology (Part II.C) treats modern culture as a genuine conversation partner reshaping the tradition, classical Evangelical theology treats Scripture more like a fixed, load-bearing wall in a house: culture can renovate many rooms, but this wall itself is never up for structural renegotiation.

## Key Terms
- **Penal Substitution**: The atonement model in which Christ voluntarily bears the divine wrath and penalty due to human sin, satisfying God's holy justice on the cross.
- **Biblical Inerrancy**: The conviction, formalized in the 1978 Chicago Statement, that Scripture is wholly truthful and without error in all it affirms.
- **Wesleyan Quadrilateral**: A method coordinating Scripture, tradition, reason, and experience, with Scripture retained as the primary norm — associated with Evangelicalism's Wesleyan wing.
- **Evangelization**: The urgent task of proclaiming the Gospel to prompt personal conversion, distinguished by classical Evangelicals from purely secular humanitarian development.

## Connections
Carl Henry and G. C. Berkouwer engaged Barth (Part I) for decades, praising his Christocentrism while rejecting his refusal to simply identify the biblical text with the objective Word of God. John Hick's turn from Evangelical faith to religious pluralism (Part VII) served as Evangelicalism's key negative contrast-case. Thomas Oden's patristic retrieval parallels the neo-traditional reaction in Pastoral and Practical Theology (Part IV).
