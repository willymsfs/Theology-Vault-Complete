# Unit 4 — The Patristic Era II — Mystagogy, Realism, and the Augustinian Question

## The Fourth-Century Mystagogues

**Cyril of Jerusalem** (*Mystagogical Catecheses* 4-5) instructs the newly baptised in vividly realist terms — "do not judge the matter by taste, but by faith be fully assured that you have been deemed worthy of the Body and Blood of Christ" — and gives the earliest detailed description of the **epiclesis** and of how to receive: "make your left hand a throne for the right, as for that which is to receive a King."

**Ambrose of Milan** (*De Sacramentis* IV, *De Mysteriis* IX) supplies the West's decisive causal account: the elements are changed **by the word of Christ**. His argument is a fortiori — if Elijah's word called down fire, and if God's word made the world from nothing, then Christ's word can surely change what already exists into something else. "Before the consecration it was not the body of Christ, but after the consecration, I tell you, it is now the body of Christ."

**John Chrysostom** contributes the language of **awe** — "the fearful mysteries," "the dread table" — and simultaneously the sharpest social application in patristic literature: *"You wish to honour the body of Christ? Do not despise him when he is naked... What use is it to weigh down Christ's table with golden cups when he himself is dying of hunger?"* The altar and the poor are the same body.

## The Augustinian Question

Augustine is the most contested patristic figure in Eucharistic theology, because both Catholics and Reformers claimed him.

**What he actually holds:**
- A robust **sign/reality (*sacramentum*/*res*)** distinction: the sacrament is a visible sign of an invisible grace, and one must not stop at the sign.
- A strongly **ecclesial** reading — the *res* of the Eucharist is the unity of the Body of Christ, the Church. Hence *Sermon* 272 to the newly baptised: *"If you are the body of Christ and his members, it is your own mystery that is placed on the Lord's table... **Be what you see, and receive what you are**."*
- **Totus Christus** — head and members together; to receive the Eucharist is to be assimilated to Christ, not to assimilate him: "You will not change me into yourself as you would food of your flesh; but you will be changed into me."

**The interpretive caution.** Augustine's sign-language is *not* modern symbolism. For Augustine a *sacramentum* genuinely contains and effects what it signifies; the sign/reality distinction is a distinction of levels, not a denial of presence. Ninth-century and Reformation readers who took his *signum* in a merely figurative sense were reading a later problematic back into him. A good exam answer says exactly this — that Augustine's categories predate the question later put to them, and that forcing him onto either side of the Berengarian or Reformation divide is anachronistic.

## East and West: The Epiclesis Question

A structural difference emerges that will matter for a millennium:

- **East** — the ***epiclesis*** (invocation of the Holy Spirit upon the gifts and the assembly) *follows* the institution narrative and is regarded as the consecratory moment.
- **West** — the Roman Canon's language is diffuse; the medieval West came to locate consecration precisely in the **words of institution** (*verba*) spoken by the priest.

This is not a difference of faith but of theological accent — and the 1969 Missal deliberately restored a strong, clearly articulated epiclesis (split into a consecratory epiclesis before the institution narrative and a communion epiclesis after), narrowing the historic gap.
