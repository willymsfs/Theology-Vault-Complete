The arc is thematic, not chronological: **context → method → interiority → structural action**.

**Three constants across all volumes:**
1. The foundational **Abba-experience** of Jesus.
2. Unwavering commitment to the **concrete Indian reality**: massive poverty, the caste system, pluriform religiosity.
3. The **preferential option for the poor**.

## 2. Volume 1 — Biblical Themes for a Contextual Theology Today

Thesis: Indian Christian theology must break free of Western colonial frameworks and address poverty, caste, and religious pluralism.

### Part I: Mission
- **Ch 1 — Expanding the Horizon of Christian Mission:** mission ≠ church growth; building God's Kingdom through solidarity, poverty, powerlessness.
- **Ch 2 — The Church as Mission:** mission is theocentric (glory to God); witness of the whole community over individual proclamation.
- **Ch 3 — Following Jesus in Mission:** the Great Commission = radical detachment, "missionary discipleship" vs colonial conquest.

### Part II: Inculturation, Liberation, Dialogue
- **Ch 4 — Inculturation–Liberation–Dialogue:** Asian theology must stop imitating Western academia; address Asia's poverty + profound religiosity.
- **Ch 5 — From Alienation to Inculturation:** the Indian Church is alienated by colonial roots; must integrate into Indian culture.
- **Ch 6 — The NT as a Model of Inculturation:** the early Church constantly adapted its message to new cultures — the blueprint.
- **Ch 7 — The Liberative Pedagogy of Jesus:** teaching non-elitist, praxis-oriented; framework for Indian liberation theology.
- **Ch 8 — The Indian Church Challenged by Poverty and Caste:** "Christian dalits" = contradiction of Jesus's inclusive Kingdom.
- **Ch 9 — Challenged by Pluralism and Dialogue:** abandon exclusivism; other religions are valid ways of salvation.
- **Ch 10 — Religion and Communalism:** overcome intolerance via Jesus's radical anti-communal love and open boundaries.
- **Ch 11 — Jesus Christ amid the Religions and Ideologies of India Today:** Jesus bridges the spiritual focus of Indian religions and the material concern of secular ideologies → holistic liberation.

### Part III: Contextual Interpretation of the Bible
- **Ch 12 — Towards an Indian Interpretation of the Bible:** dual approach — "religious reading" (Indian methods, dhvani) + "social reading" (liberation).
- **Ch 13 — The Table Fellowship of Jesus:** dining with outcasts dismantled purity lines → direct condemnation of caste and untouchability.
- **Ch 14 — Antigreed and Antipride:** tribal values foundational to Jesus's ethics.
- **Ch 15 — Class in the Bible:** the biblical "poor" = a socially/economically oppressed class; God's salvation is linked to their liberation.

## 3. Volume 2 — A Biblical Theology for India (full volume)

Sixteen chapters in three parts. Part I: Biblical Methods, Old Testament, Non-Christian Scriptures. Part II: Theological Portraits of Jesus. Part III: Reading the Bible from Indian Perspectives.

### Chapter theses
**Part I**
- **Ch 1 — The Historical Critical Method:** HCM inadequate for India; integrated "religious" + "social" reading proposed (detailed below).
- **Ch 2 — The Word of God and the Forms of Literature:** form criticism "recovers the 'spokenness' of God's Word" — the text becomes a direct, urgent address summoning personal encounter, not a dead book.
- **Ch 3 — Socio-Cultural Analysis in Prophetic Theologizing:** the biblical prophet as paradigm: social analysis of contemporary events driven by a God-given "alternative vision," in language that "breaks down the structures of the dominant society and empowers the dominated to structure an alternative community."
- **Ch 4 — The Temple of Jerusalem:** built for political legitimation, the Temple domesticated Yahweh; Solomon's project "effectively reverses the movement of the Mosaic revolution," replacing an economics of equality and a politics of justice with affluence, forced labor, imperial oppression.
- **Ch 5 — The Inspiration of the OT and Non-Christian Scriptures:** the OT is inspired "not in itself but inasmuch as it is illumined by the New"; if Christ is the Word enlightening everyone, salvation history encompasses the universe — "the inspiration of non-Christian scriptures is a distinct possibility."

**Part II**
- **Ch 6 — Are the Gospels Historical?:** the Gospels are "history interpreted through faith," a "mosaic" of traditions revealing religious significance, not chronological biography.
- **Ch 7 — Jesus and the Spirit in the Synoptics:** the Spirit grounds Jesus's sonship (infancy), installs him messianically (baptism), empowers exorcism and evangelization (ministry) — marked not by ecstatic enthusiasm but by "the authority of his teaching, and the power of his healings and exorcisms." "Sonship (the experience of God as abba, Father) and Spirit (the experience of the eschatological power of God working in and through him) were in fact the twin poles of Jesus' experience of God."
- **Ch 8 — Jesus the Teacher:** liberative pedagogy — dialogical methods and parables conscientizing marginalized audiences toward a new fraternal world.
- **Ch 9 — Jesus in Matthew:** a majestic, exalted Jesus who "stands above the stress of events," "almost an avatar"; the "Teacher of Righteousness" and divine Son authoritatively interpreting God's will.
- **Ch 10 — Mary in Luke's Annunciation:** Luke fuses an OT call-narrative with a birth oracle ("a birth oracle replaces the commissioning"); Mary as "eschatological Daughter of Sion" personifying the faithful remnant.
- **Ch 11 — The Man Born Blind:** physical sight vs spiritual insight; for India, a call to "radical openness" to a Christ who "fits no formula, who breaks through the restraints of our comfortably constructed systems."

**Part III**
- **Ch 12 — The Sacred in the Secular (Johannine Sutra, Jn 1:14):** three implications — christological: "decisively excludes any sort of epiphanic or avatara type of Christianity"; anthropological: God met by serving neighbor in the secular world; cosmic: the Incarnation demands respect for matter — "the sacred interpenetrates the secular (as the soul interpenetrates the body), and is accessible only through it."
- **Ch 13 — Christian Priesthood in India Today:** against a purely cultic, sacralized priesthood; the priest enters the Kingdom movement that "continually subverts a world structured... by relationships of oppression."
- **Ch 14 — 'And There Was a Great Calm' (dhvani reading of Mk 4:35–41):** the worked model of the dhvani method (below).
- **Ch 15 — Good News to the Poor:** Jesus's message has a "strongly social thrust" aimed "to set free the oppressed"; Luke deliberately modified Isaiah 61 to block spiritualizing readings of "Blessed are you poor."
- **Ch 16 — Laughing at Idols (Isa 44:9–20):** the biblical anti-idol satire is a "damaging and therefore sinful misrepresentation of a people's religion and culture" — idolaters worship the divine that "dwells" in the image, as "bread and wine in the Christian Eucharist mediate the 'real presence' of Jesus." The "fanatical iconoclasm of the Bible" is the "dark side of its monotheism," historically issuing in "devastating destruction of peoples and cultures."

### The dhvani method worked (Mk 4:35–41)
Takes the narrative as it stands (against source-dissection) and reads three oppositions: great storm vs great calm (precariousness of life on the edge of chaos); sleeping Jesus vs panicking disciples (faith vs fear); passive passenger vs commanding Lord. Yield: "It is this experience of ultimate stability behind the turmoil of our agitated and threatened existence that this story of the Stilling of the Storm evokes."

### Critique of the historical-critical method
- "The pretended neutrality of Western exegesis and its historical critical method is thus... an illusion." The method is "ideologically loaded."
- Functions as "legitimisation of the capitalistic technocracy" — lets interpreters ignore the Gospel's challenge to wealth and power.
- Western scholarship = "production for profit — for academic kudos if not for money."
- "No such thing as an autonomous, impartial, academic theology floating free above the realm of human options and biases."

### What he retains
- "Sound historical criticism... will in fact bring out the objective dimension of its meaning" — reveals the text's original "intentionality," prevents "far-fetched flights of fancy." HCM = safeguard and control.

### The Integral Indian Reading
Two complementary readings that must converge:

1. **Religious reading** — transposes the Gospel into India's religious traditions. Two steps: "first I have to assimilate my own cultural and religious past by a reading of the Indian scriptures; then I will re-read the Gospel in the light of this new experience." Uses Indian hermeneutics: *dhvani* = "the resonating effect of a word through which it evokes in the hearer deep meanings."
2. **Social reading** — interprets the Gospels "in the light of a liberating praxis among the socially oppressed." Targets institutional structures causing mass poverty and illiteracy; also personal liberation from greed and aggression.

**Convergence:** the halves correct and enrich each other, revealing "liberation announced by Jesus in its totality" — "both the liberation from social oppression... as well as the freedom from personal bondage (moksa)."

### The interpreter's double identity
- Must assimilate own religious heritage before re-reading the Gospel; requires "radical self-emptying."
- Indian theologians alienated from their roots by "long years of Western indoctrination" in Westernized seminaries.
- The task: "To a critical consciousness derived from Western modernization, he must bring a profound and sympathetic understanding of ancient Indian tradition; to a pluriform religious experience he must join a deep and active concern for social issues."

## 4. Volume 3 — Biblical Spirituality of Liberative Action

Central claim: genuine spirituality is contextualized; Jesus's Abba-rooted spirituality spills into political action, solidarity with the oppressed, struggle against unjust structures.

### Structure
1. **Jesus's Foundational Experience and its Expression (ch 1–9)** — the "Dharma of Jesus."
2. **Dynamics of Christian Spirituality** — salvation = social + cosmic transformation, not individual rescue.
3. **Spirituality of Jesus's Professional Followers** — religious life, priesthood, vows as prophetic commitment.

### Key chapter theses
- Miracles (ch 2–4): "signs of the Kingdom," not proofs — "announce and actualize (make present) the saving action of God"; subvert oppressive power structures.
- 'As We Forgive' (ch 6): human forgiveness = result and paradigm of experiencing God's unlimited forgiveness, not a way to earn it.
- Love Commandment (ch 7): loving God ≡ loving neighbor; active concern for others = core of spiritual life.
- Jesus and Conflict (ch 11): root of conflict = God's unconditional love prioritizing people over the Law.
- Social Stance (ch 14): Jesus sided with the poor/outcast, confronted the socio-religious establishment.
- Unprejudiced Jesus, Prejudiced Church (ch 16): boundary-breaking love vs Church discrimination.
- Priesthood as Call in the OT (ch 18): Christian priesthood models charismatic prophets, not cultic OT priesthood.
- Christian Purusharthas (ch 22): Hindu life-goals reinterpreted; individual liberation inseparable from community liberation.

### The Abba experience and the Dharma of Jesus
- Dharma of Jesus = "dharma of unconditional and therefore of absolutely universal love."
- Abba = "loving Parent"; experience of "unconditional love," "radically liberating."
- God as parent → all people brothers/sisters → "empowers him to identify with the poor and the outcast and confront the powerful and the rich."
- Mysticism and politics intertwined (via Metz): following Christ "has a mystical element and one that is situational, one that is practical and political." Genuine mysticism "pushes" one into history.
- Prayer → praxis: the Lord's Prayer is "wholly a prayer for the Kingdom"; praying for forgiveness commits us to practicing it.
- The Cross: "no arbitrary intrusion into the life of Jesus. It is the natural outcome of a life of solidarity with the poor and the outcast and of confrontation with the powerful who oppress them."

### Vows
- "We are freed from all things, in order that we might make ourselves slaves of all through love." Communitarian practices freeing for radical action, not private asceticism.

## 5. Volume 4 — Theology of Liberation: An Indian Biblical Perspective

Central claim: a biblical theology of liberation through Indian eyes — holistic liberation from poverty, caste, cultural alienation.

### Chapter theses
- **Ch 1 — Interpreting the Bible in India Today:** beyond Western HCM via the "Indian mind."
- **Ch 2 — The Prophet as Theologian:** historically rooted critique against religious idolatry and social injustice.
- **Ch 3 — Commitment and Conversion:** radical commitment to the poor + adoption of the Indian worldview.
- **Ch 4 — Two Mission Commands:** Mt 28 vs Buddhist Mahāvagga; mission = liberation + compassion, not domination.
- **Ch 5 — The Bible as Magna Carta:** human rights grounded in the sacredness of the person + responsibility for the marginalized.
- **Ch 6 — Class in the Bible:** the "poor" = oppressed sociological group championed by God.
- **Ch 7 — The Synoptic Love-Commandment:** agapē requires concrete action and struggle for societal justice.
- **Ch 8 — The NT Church:** radical sharing and social protest = principles for economic liberation.
- **Ch 9 — Radical Beginnings:** the Jesus movement = radically free, egalitarian, serving community; archetype for the Church.
- **Ch 10 — The Dharma of Jesus:** the Sermon on the Mount as dharma, not legal code.
- **Ch 11 — Jesus and the Poor:** poor = destitute + marginalized + illiterate + afflicted; end social poverty, call to spiritual poverty (freedom from mammon).
- **Ch 12 — The Christian Response to the Indian Situation:** historical, incarnational, "integral."
- **Ch 13 — A Dalit Reading of the Decalogue:** the Ten Commandments = absolute equality before the law; contrast with the Laws of Manu.
- **Ch 14 — John 1:1–18, an Asian Perspective:** inculturated Prologue reading; Īśāvāsya Upanishad vs consumerism.
- **Ch 15 — The Kingdom of God: Jesus's Vision of a New Society:** freedom + fellowship + justice.
- **Ch 16 — Jesus the Teacher:** "liberative pedagogy" — dialogical, parabolic, consciousness-raising.
- **Ch 17 — The Jesus of Faith:** Third World Christology from encounter with the historical Jesus, not Hellenistic formulas.

### The Indian mind hermeneutic
- "Context-sensitive rather than context-free"; reality as interconnected whole.
- "Holistic and inclusive"; "logic of complementarity" (both/and) vs Aristotelian "logic of exclusion" (either/or).
- Worldview: "inclusive, cosmocentric, symbolic, and practical."
- "Because of its passion for wholeness the Indian mind is prepared to risk the chance of error rather than the loss of any part of truth."
- Interpreter's two-fold conversion: (1) "radical commitment to the poor"; (2) conversion to the Indian worldview.

### The prophet-as-theologian
- Theologize like the prophets: rooted in concrete history, not abstract exegesis.
- Two-fold protest: **religious critique** (condemns idolatry — absolutization of money, state, institutions) + **social critique** ("concrete expressions of exploitation and oppression").
- Theology begins with social analysis and leads to social change.

### Dharma of Jesus / Sermon on the Mount
- Dharma (Indian sense): "all comprehensive" — religious observance, righteousness, justice, conformity to law and custom, duty.
- Dharma of Jesus: "the complex of religious insight and ethical concern (of experience, world-view and value) which determines the life-style that Jesus proclaimed and practised."
- As juridical law the Sermon = "thoroughly utopian law." As dharma: "The dharma of the Sermon on the Mount then proclaims freedom from the burden of the law, since it is grounded on an experience of God as abba who loves us his children with an unconditional love."

### The Kingdom as New Society
- "A new society is realized whenever humans become free internally and externally, when there is fellowship of concern among humans and when they strive for a just society."
- Three dimensions: **personal freedom** (internal + external), **communitarian fellowship** (mutual concern, radical equality), **structural justice**.
- These are "parameters by which the arrival or the delay of God's reign is to be gauged."

### Human rights
- The Bible grounds rights via: (1) sacredness of every person (imago Dei); (2) responsibility for the powerless (widow, orphan, refugee). The neighbor = locus of encountering God.
- "In an unjust society agapē inevitably becomes a struggle for justice: it strives 'to set free the oppressed'."

### Two mission commands
- Mt 28 vs Mahāvagga: similar tripartite structure. The Buddhist command stresses the missionary's own enlightenment and unconditional compassion; Matthew's is heavily Christological. An Asian reading recovers the missionary's inner liberation: "The Christian mission, for all its christological grounding, also presupposes the 'enlightenment' of those who are sent."

### Jesus of Faith christology
- "To be a disciple of Jesus... means to experience God the way that Jesus experienced God."
- Rooted in the abba experience → radical freedom, love, solidarity with the poor → conflict → cross.

## 6. The Unified Interpretive Pipeline

1. **Commitment & pre-understanding** — conscious commitment to the Indian context (poverty, pluriform religiosity, caste). No neutral standpoint.
2. **Historical-critical exegesis** — recover the original "author meaning"; historical base and control against eisegesis.
3. **Social analysis** — structures of the biblical world and contemporary India; all theology is socially conditioned.
4. **Indian hermeneutic (dual reading)** — religious reading (dhvani, symbolic depth) + social reading (liberating praxis).
5. **Praxis** — total liberation: from societal oppression and personal bondage (moksha).

### Worked example: Table Fellowship
- Pre-understanding: caste/untouchability.
- HCM: Jesus habitually dined with "tax collectors and sinners."
- Social analysis: a shared meal = religious affirmation of belonging; subverted purity laws and elitism.
- Dual reading: socially — confronts caste; religiously — God as loving parent rejecting ritual purity as barrier.
- Praxis: eradicate caste discrimination in the Church; build an egalitarian community.

## 7. Key Concept Definitions (his own words)

- **Kingdom of God:** "the decisive salvific action through which God, finally 'liberating' creation, reveals God's self as King" (Mk 1:14–15). Three dimensions: personal freedom, communitarian fellowship, societal justice.
- **The Poor (anawim):** from a root meaning "bent, bowed down, afflicted" — "all those who are in any way, and not just economically, deprived of the means or the dignity they need to lead a fully human existence" (Lk 6:20). Defined by sociological powerlessness; privileged beneficiaries of God's action.
- **Inculturation:** "the passage of a religious tradition from one cultural situation to another, in a movement that resembles the conversion of a person from a situation of sin to a situation of salvation"; "throwing off an imposed alien and alienating system of doctrine and worship, in order to allow Indian experiences of Jesus to find their unforced expression."
- **Dhvani:** "the resonating effect of a word through which it evokes in the hearer deep meanings"; applied intertextually it "elicits deeper meaning in the hearer which otherwise would not have been perceived at all."
- **Liberative pedagogy:** "pedagogy of the oppressed with a strong commitment to societal liberation"; "nonélitist and dialogical, based on the equality of all before God."
- **Communalism:** life perceived "in terms of religious communities, each with a distinct identity of its own, competing with other religious groups for survival and growth"; countered by the "anticommunal Jesus" (Mk 7:1–23).
- **Table fellowship:** "acted parable, through which he brings home to listeners his experience of God as Abba the loving parent" (Mk 2:15ff).
- **Antigreed:** tribal value; rejection of consumerism (Mk 10:17–27).
- **Antipride:** "not self-abasement but fearless and active service" (Mk 10:35–45).
- **Dharma of Jesus:** "the complex of religious insight and ethical concern... which determines the life-style that Jesus proclaimed and practised"; a "dharma of unconditional and therefore of absolutely universal love."

## 8. Polemical Positions

| Target | Critique | Sharpest quotes |
|--------|----------|-----------------|
| Western academic exegesis | Ideologically loaded, capitalist-conditioned, socially irrelevant | "ruinous heap of hypothetical possibilities"; exegetes "fiddle while Rome burns"; seminary training = "a trained incapacity to deal with the real problems of actual living persons in their daily lives" |

## 9. Voice and Style

- **Architecture:** thematic headings; deep biblical foundation first, then Indian application; essays open with broad statement of context/purpose, close with appeals for paradigm shift.
- **Rhythm:** balanced, flowing sentences that build to passionate, rhetorically charged statements; exclamation points at climaxes.
- **Citations:** extensive footnotes; synthesizes and critiques Western scholars (Bultmann, Jeremias) alongside Indian thinkers (Gandhi, Samuel Rayan, Ramanujan); draws on Segundo, Pieris, Metz.
- **Scripture:** heavy inline citation used to subvert traditional Western readings, not proof-text.
- **Devices:** sharp contrasts (mission not "conquest" but "light"; "context-sensitive" vs "context-free"; "culture of silence" vs "tinsel facade"), rhetorical questions, vivid metaphors ("two-eyed vision," "elliptical" truth).
- **Register:** described by his editors as the "careful and precise activity of a committed and gifted scribe" — scholarly, analytical, prophetic.

### Signature quotes
1. "Faith endures; but no theology is perennial!"
2. "To do theology means to contextualize one's faith."
3. "An Indian exegesis will be greatly concerned about relevance and so will strenuously avoid the academic barrenness which afflicts 'scientific' exegesis today."
4. "Mission is therefore concerned not with the power, security or growth of the missionizing Churches, but with the welfare of the missionized world."
5. "Any genuine spirituality... is a contextualized spirituality, a way of life based on certain convictions and values (a dharma) which interacts creatively and appropriately with its historical context."
6. "To experience God as Abba is therefore to experience the 'wholly other' in the give and take of human history."
7. "In an unjust society agapē inevitably becomes a struggle for justice: it strives 'to set free the oppressed'."
8. "To be a disciple of Jesus... means to experience God the way that Jesus experienced God."
