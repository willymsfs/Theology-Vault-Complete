# Ch. 15 — Systematic Theology after Barth: Jüngel, Jenson, and Gunton
*Chapter author: John Webster*

## Core Idea
Writing in the shadow of Barth's death (1968), Eberhard Jüngel, Robert Jenson, and Colin Gunton each construct a distinctively Christian, trinitarian ontology in which God's being is not a static substance but is dynamically realized in God's historical, relational self-gift in Christ.

## Detailed Explanation
Jüngel, raised in the Stalinist East Germany and formed by Bultmann's existential-hermeneutical school, coined the axiom Gottes Sein ist im Werden ("God's being is in becoming") — God's identity is not fixed in advance but is realized in God's coming-to-speech and suffering on the cross. Jenson, an American Lutheran shaped by direct debate with Barth, radicalizes Barth's "actualism" (Part I) by identifying God's very triune being with the concrete temporal history of Jesus, embodied sacramentally in the Church. Gunton, an English Reformed theologian at King's College London who died suddenly during this volume's compilation, builds a relational, "perichoretic" metaphysics of the "one and the many" to protect creaturely particularity against both individualism and totalizing system.

## Example
Jenson's insistence that God's triune identity is inseparable from the concrete history of Israel and Jesus, rather than a timeless metaphysical fact behind history, shapes his controversial claim that the Church's own sacramental life (baptism, Eucharist) is where that triune identity continues to be enacted in time — a claim with direct, practical consequences for how his Lutheran tradition understands sacramental real presence.

## Illustration
Classical philosophical theology often pictures God like a perfect, unchanging marble statue — complete, static, admired from outside. Jüngel's "God's being is in becoming" pictures God more like a river: genuinely, identifiably the same river at every point, yet only actually existing as a living, moving, historical happening, never as a frozen block.

## Key Terms
- **Entsprechungen** (German) — "correspondences": Jüngel's term for the analogical correspondence between God's active, justifying grace and the human being's responsive moral action.
- **Gottes Sein ist im Werden** (German) — "God's being is in becoming": Jüngel's axiom: God's being is not static substance but is dynamically realized in God's historical self-revelation and suffering.
- **Triune Identity**: Jenson's radicalization of Barth's actualism: God's very being is identified with the concrete, temporal history of Jesus and its sacramental continuation in the Church.
- **Perichoretic Unity** (Greek root: perichoresis, mutual indwelling): Gunton's relational, communion-based metaphysic holding together "the one and the many" without collapsing creaturely particularity into a totalizing system.

## Connections
All three remain, in Webster's own framing, "theologians of revelation" continuing Barth's family of Protestant theological realism (Part I). Jenson and Gunton both reject what they see as Barth's residual Augustinianism on election. Gunton's relational ontology allies systematically with T. F. Torrance's dogmatic frame (Part II.B).
