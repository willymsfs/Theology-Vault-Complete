# Part 3 — Major Theological Themes

| Theme | Key Document(s) | Core Development |
|---|---|---|
| Liturgy | *Sacrosanctum Concilium* | Silent rubrics -> active, conscious, communal participation; vernacular introduced |
| Revelation | *Dei Verbum* | "Two-source theory" abandoned for a single deposit communicated through Scripture and Tradition together |
| Nature of the Church | *Lumen Gentium* | *Societas perfecta* -> communion/People of God; baptismal equality placed before hierarchy |
| Collegiality | *LG* ch.3, *Christus Dominus* | Recovers what Vatican I left unfinished — the Council's fiercest internal battleground |
| Ecumenism | *Unitatis Redintegratio*, *Orientalium Ecclesiarum* | "Unionist" return-to-Rome model -> recognition of elements of truth in other communities |
| Religious Liberty | *Dignitatis Humanae* | "Error has no rights" -> freedom grounded in human dignity and civil immunity from coercion |
| Other Religions | *Nostra Aetate* | Began as a post-Shoah antisemitism statement; expanded to Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism |
| Church and Modern World | *Gaudium et Spes* | Inductive "see-judge-act" method; resolved via Christological anthropology (GS 22) |

## Liturgical Spirituality (Gregory Collins's Four Principles)

Authentic liturgical spirituality is: **Christological** (rooted in Cross and Resurrection), **universal** (holiness for all, not an elite), **pneumatological** (dependent on the Spirit given in Baptism), and **community-centred** (genuine only if it issues in love of neighbour). Liturgical prayer requires an intuitive, embodied, symbolic understanding — physical gestures (procession, kissing the cross, the thurible) are themselves acts of prayer, not merely illustrations of doctrine. The Eucharist is the **"Source and Summit"** of Christian life, forming a single act of worship with two halves: the Liturgy of the Word and the Eucharistic Liturgy (SC 56).

## Dei Verbum's Continuity/Novelty (Witherup's Analysis)

- **Continuity:** DV explicitly reaffirms Trent's and Vatican I's teaching on inspiration, canon, and inerrancy.
- **Novelty:** replaces cold propositional formulas with **personalist language** — revelation as an invitation to friendship, God's voice compared to a mother speaking to her children — and uses an **Incarnation analogy** (fully divine, fully human authorship) to explain Scripture's dual nature. Officially adopts the Pontifical Biblical Commission's finding of **three historical layers of tradition** within the Gospels, legitimizing historical-critical exegesis.

## Nostra Aetate and Judaism (Ruokanen's Close Reading)

Careful, deliberate use of *patrimonium spirituale* ("spiritual patrimony") to name the unique bond with Judaism — the Council acknowledges Judaism shares a true biblical *revelatio* with Christianity, while Christological *gratia* is invoked only once in the same passage: real theological generosity, held together with an undiminished claim about Christ.

## O'Collins's "Nine Invitations" — A Study Framework

A synthesis of the Council's entire spirituality: **be liturgical - be committed to the needy - be biblical - be priestly/prophetic/kingly - be Jesus-centred - be in dialogue - be reformers (*ecclesia semper reformanda*) - be collegial - be in touch with Mary and the saints.**
