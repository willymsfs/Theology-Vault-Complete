# Unit 10 — Amoris Laetitia and the Contemporary Debate

## The Two Synods

**The Extraordinary Synod of 2014** and the **Ordinary Synod of 2015** on the family were the most contested since the Council. The flashpoint was the pastoral care of the **divorced and civilly remarried**.

**Cardinal Walter Kasper's** February 2014 address to the consistory proposed a limited *via poenitentialis*: after a period of penance and discernment, and where the first marriage could not be shown null but reconciliation was impossible, admission to communion might be possible in some cases — appealing to the Orthodox practice of *oikonomia* and to patristic evidence for a penitential path.

Opposition was substantial and public, including from five cardinals in a jointly authored book, arguing that the proposal was incompatible with the indissolubility of marriage and with the Eucharistic discipline flowing from it.

## Amoris Laetitia (19 March 2016)

Most of the exhortation is a warm, practical, and widely welcomed treatment of love and family life — chapter 4's meditation on 1 Corinthians 13 is its best-known section. The controversy concerns **chapter 8**.

**Its method** is threefold: **accompanying, discerning, and integrating** weakness. Its key moves:

- **AL 301**: "it can no longer simply be said that all those in any 'irregular' situation are living in a state of mortal sin" — because subjective culpability may be diminished by ignorance, conditioning, or circumstances.
- **AL 300**: discernment must be personal and pastoral, with a properly formed conscience, and "what is possible is simply a renewed encouragement to undertake a responsible personal and pastoral discernment of particular cases."
- **Footnote 351**, attached to a discussion of those in irregular situations: "In certain cases, this can include the help of the sacraments... I would also point out that the Eucharist 'is not a prize for the perfect, but a powerful medicine and nourishment for the weak.'"

**The dubia.** In September 2016 four cardinals (Brandmüller, Burke, Caffarra, Meisner) submitted five *dubia* asking for a yes-or-no clarification, principally whether AL permits communion in such cases and whether the teaching on intrinsically evil acts still stands. No formal reply was given.

**The authoritative interpretation.** Francis wrote to the bishops of the Buenos Aires region approving their guidelines — which allow that in some cases access to the sacraments may be possible — and stated "there are no other interpretations." Both the guidelines and the letter were subsequently published in the *Acta Apostolicae Sedis* with a note designating them **authentic magisterium**.

**How to handle this in an essay.** State the interpretive dispute fairly: one reading holds AL 8 develops *Familiaris Consortio* 84 by applying the traditional principles of imputability and the internal forum to a situation FC treated only in the objective order; another holds it introduces a discontinuity in Eucharistic discipline that the earlier teaching excluded. The *AAS* publication settles the document's authority without ending the theological argument about its content.

## Mitis Iudex Dominus Iesus (2015)

Often overlooked but arguably of greater practical consequence: Francis's reform of the **annulment process**. It abolished the requirement of a **second concordant sentence** before a declaration of nullity takes effect; permitted the **bishop himself** to judge certain cases in a brief process (*processus brevior*) where nullity is manifest and both parties agree; and directed that the process be free of charge where possible. Annulment timelines fell dramatically in many dioceses.
