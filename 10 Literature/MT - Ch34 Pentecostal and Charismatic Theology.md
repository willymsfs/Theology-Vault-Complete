# Ch. 34 — Pentecostal and Charismatic Theology
*Chapter author: Allan Anderson*

## Core Idea
Pentecostal/Charismatic theology centres on a direct, personal encounter with the Holy Spirit, expressed as "narrative and oral" theology of praise and healing rather than scholastic dogma, expecting the New Testament's apostolic gifts (tongues, prophecy, healing) to be present realities today ("restorationism").

## Detailed Explanation
Pentecostalism began with the Azusa Street Revival (1906-08) in Los Angeles, led by African American preacher William J. Seymour, emerging from nineteenth-century Holiness/Sanctification movements. Three historical phases followed: Classical Pentecostalism (1901-1920s, e.g. Assemblies of God), the Charismatic Movement entering mainline Protestant and Catholic churches from 1960, and the "Third Wave" of independent, non-denominational megachurches since 1975 — now Christianity's fastest-growing global sector. Theologically, salvation is organized around the "fivefold gospel": Jesus as Saviour, Sanctifier, Baptizer in the Spirit, Healer, and soon-Coming King.

## Example
David Yonggi Cho's Yoido Full Gospel Church in Seoul — the world's single largest congregation, built on a "theology of prosperity" and small home-cell groups — is the clearest concrete instance of Pentecostalism's explosive, indigenized global growth outside its North American origins.

## Illustration
If much academic systematic theology reads like a carefully argued written brief, Pentecostal theology is closer to a live musical performance: the "text" is as much sung, prayed, and enacted in the moment as it is argued on a page, and its truth is confirmed by what happens (healing, tongues, transformed lives) as much as by what is logically demonstrated.

## Key Terms
- **Baptism in the Holy Spirit**: The distinct, post-conversion experience of spiritual empowerment, linked by classical Pentecostals to speaking in tongues as initial evidence.
- **Fivefold Gospel**: Pentecostalism's core Christology: Jesus as Saviour, Sanctifier, Baptizer in the Spirit, Healer, and Coming King.
- **Restorationism**: The conviction that the contemporary Church should "restore" the apostolic purity and charismatic power of the first-century Church.
- **Moya** (Shona/Zulu) — "spirit / breath": African contextual pneumatology integrating the Holy Spirit with traditional concepts of vital force and ancestral healing.

## Connections
Pentecostal scholars like Gordon Fee engage Barth's neo-orthodoxy (Part I) directly through Luke-Acts exegesis to defend Spirit-baptism as distinct from conversion. Walter Hollenweger's sociological research mapped Pentecostalism's non-Western, oral roots against Eurocentric academic norms. Chung Hyun Kyung's Canberra pneumatology (Part V, Part VI's Ecumenical chapter) intersected with, and was debated within, global Charismatic circles.
