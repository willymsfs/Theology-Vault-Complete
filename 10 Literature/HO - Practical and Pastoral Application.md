# Unit 13 — Practical and Pastoral Application

## Formation and Ministry

| Area | Current framework |
|---|---|
| **Formation** | *Pastores Dabo Vobis*'s four dimensions — human, spiritual, intellectual, pastoral — plus the 2016 *Ratio*'s propaedeutic stage and insistence on gradual integration |
| **Incardination** | Every cleric must be incardinated into a diocese, personal prelature, or institute (CIC c. 265) — the surviving form of Chalcedon c. 6 |
| **Requirements for ordination** | Baptised male (c. 1024); required age, formation, and interstices; freedom from irregularities and impediments (cc. 1040-1049) |
| **Loss of the clerical state** | By judicial sentence, administrative decree, or rescript — which removes the *exercise* and obligations but **not the character**; a laicised priest still validly absolves in danger of death (c. 976) |

## The Indian Context

- **Vocations.** India is one of the world's principal *sending* churches, supplying clergy and religious to Europe, North America, and Africa. This is a genuine strength and also raises real questions about the distribution of personnel, the formation of men for cross-cultural ministry, and the effect on the sending dioceses.
- **Caste and the clergy.** The most serious pastoral issue, and it connects directly to what the vault already holds. **Dalit Christians** form a large majority of the Catholic population in several regions while being persistently under-represented in the episcopate and in seminary leadership; caste-based grouping within seminaries and in appointments is documented and has been the subject of Dalit Christian protest for decades. **George Soares-Prabhu's** judgement that caste in the Church makes the Eucharist "a sacrilege" and "parody" applies with particular force to *orders*, since the ordained exist to serve the unity that caste division denies — and since the priest is the one who presides at the very Eucharist Soares-Prabhu says caste parodies.
- **Formation and inculturation.** **Michael Amaladoss's** rule that inculturation must begin from actual living symbols applies to formation as much as to liturgy: a seminary curriculum transplanted from Europe forms men for a Church that does not exist.
- **Clericalism in a hierarchical society.** Francis's critique of clericalism lands differently in a society already organised by hierarchy and deference. The pastoral risk is that clerical status is absorbed into existing structures of social precedence rather than standing against them.

## Common Questions

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Can a laicised priest ever function? | He may absolve in danger of death (c. 976); otherwise no. The character remains |
| Is celibacy required for validity? | No. It is Latin discipline; Eastern Catholic and Orthodox married clergy are validly ordained |
| Can a deacon anoint the sick or absolve? | No — both require priestly ordination (cc. 965, 1003) |
| Why three bishops at an episcopal consecration? | Nicaea c. 4; the principal consecrator alone suffices for validity, but the norm expresses collegiality |
| Are Anglican orders valid? | *Apostolicae Curae* (1896) says no; the picture is complicated by Old Catholic co-consecrations, and further complicated by women's ordination |
