# Unit 9 — Lumen Gentium I: Mystery, Sacrament, People of God

## Lumen Gentium as Magna Charta
The Dogmatic Constitution on the Church (promulgated 21 November 1964) is rightly called Vatican II's "Magna Charta": nearly every other conciliar document presupposes its ecclesiology.

## Church as Mystery
LG 1-8 traces the Church's origin through the Trinity itself: theocentric (the Father's eternal plan, LG 2), Christocentric (the Church instituted through Christ's mission and paschal mystery, LG 3), pneumatological (sanctified and guided by the Spirit poured out at Pentecost, LG 4). The Church is thus not first a human organization that later acquired a religious purpose, but a mystery rooted in the Trinity's own inner life, unfolding in history.

## Church as Sacrament
LG 1 describes the Church as "sign and instrument both of a very closely knit union with God and of the unity of the whole human race." The Church does not merely point beyond itself to salvation (a bare sign); it actively mediates it (an instrument) — while remaining subordinate to, and provisional in relation to, the Kingdom of God it serves and anticipates but does not exhaust.

## Church as People of God
The Council deliberately placed this chapter (LG, ch. 2, esp. 9-17) before the chapter on the hierarchy (ch. 3) — a structural decision asserting that all the baptized share a common, prior dignity and equal call to holiness before any distinction of office is introduced. The People of God is a pilgrim people journeying through history toward the eschaton, always reformable (ecclesia semper reformanda), and marked by equal discipleship by baptism.

LG 14-16 describes three concentric circles of belonging. LG 14: the Catholic faithful, fully incorporated through the threefold bond of professed faith, sacraments, and communion with the pope and bishops. LG 15: other baptized Christians, joined to the Church though not entirely, in an "imperfect communion," yet possessing real elements of sanctification and truth. LG 16: non-Christians related to the People of God in different ways — Jews as the people of the first Covenant, Muslims who profess the faith of Abraham, and others who without their own fault do not know the Gospel yet sincerely seek God, including atheists of upright life.

## Illustration
LG's decision to place "People of God" (ch. 2) before "Hierarchy" (ch. 3) is itself a small illustration of a large claim — as if a family portrait were re-taken so that every family member appears in the same photograph before the parents are pictured separately giving instructions. Everyone belongs to the family first; distinctions of role come second.

## Indian Context
The three concentric circles matter pastorally in India, where Catholics live daily alongside Hindus, Muslims, Sikhs, and other believers who are, per LG 16, "related" to the People of God — a theological basis for respectful relationship rather than simple exclusion.
