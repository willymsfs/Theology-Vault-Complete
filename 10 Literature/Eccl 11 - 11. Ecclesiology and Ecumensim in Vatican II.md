# 11. Ecclesiology and Ecumensim in Vatican II.pdf

**TITLE**: "ECCLESIOLOGY AND ECUMENISM OF THE SECOND SESSION OF THE VATICAN COUNCIL II." By Nikos A. Nissiotis (Associate Director of the Ecumenical Institute, Chateau de Bossey, Switzerland, and official observer of the World Council of Churches) [1]. Published in *The Greek Orthodox Theological Review*, Vol. IX, No. 1, pages 15–36 [2, 3].

**MAIN SUBJECT**:
A critical evaluation of the theological developments, documents, and ecumenical implications of the second session of Vatican II from an Eastern Orthodox perspective [2, 4], focusing on the limitations of Roman Catholic ecclesiology regarding non-Catholic traditions [5, 6].

**KEY TOPICS**:
*   The transition in spirit from the spontaneous first session to the systematic second session [7].
*   The liturgical reforms of the Constitution on the Divine Liturgy as ecumenical progress [8].
*   Critical structural revisions to the Schema on the Church (*De Ecclesia*) [9].
*   The absence of Pneumatology and its effect on Catholic institutionalism [10, 11].
*   The limits of episcopal collegiality in relation to absolute Papal Primacy [12-14].
*   The discriminatory classification of Protestant and Orthodox traditions in the Schema on Ecumenism [6, 15, 16].
*   The structural critique of Roman "pyramid" ecclesiology [11].

**CONCEPTS EXPLAINED**:
1.  **Pyramid Ecclesiology**: A hierarchical model of the Church described from the top (the Pope) downward, wherein juridical authority and "de jure divino" qualities are channeled to the rest of the body, rather than building up from the common cornerstone of Christ [11].
2.  **Ex Sese Non Sine Consensu**: A progressive conciliar reinterpretation of the Vatican I definition of papal infallibility (*ex sese et non ex consensu ecclesiae*), asserting that the Pope does not pronounce infallible doctrines in isolation, but "not without the consent of the episcopate" [14].
3.  **Vestigia Ecclesiae (Elements of the Church)**: A quantitative ecumenical pattern implied in the Schema on Ecumenism, measuring separated traditions by the "quantity" of Catholic elements they retained after breaking communion with Rome [17].
4.  **Eucharistic/Pneumatic Discipline**: An Eastern Orthodox alternative to Roman centralized jurisdiction, maintaining spiritual order in the Body of Christ through mutual obedience of members under the guidance of the Holy Spirit [18, 19].

**ISSUES ADDRESSED**:
1.  **The Denial of the Name "Church" to Non-Catholics**: The Schema on the Church’s rigorous identification of the Church exclusively with the Roman communion, which treats non-Catholics purely as "individual Christians" and denies the ecclesial status of their communities [5, 6].
2.  **The Subordination of the Episcopacy to the Pope**: The ambiguity of "collegiality," where no act of the college of bishops can occur without the free approval, invitation, and consent of the Bishop of Rome [13, 14].
3.  **The Marginalization of the Priesthood and the Faithful**: The danger of isolating the episcopate as a sacrament in a way that separates bishops from the rest of the priesthood and the fullness of the laity [20, 21].
4.  **The Historical Omission of the Great Schism**: The Schema on Ecumenism's failure to historically account for the Schism of 1054 while simultaneously presenting the Council of Florence as a benchmark for Eastern reunion [15, 22].

**IMPORTANT QUOTES**:
*   "The center of the liturgical life is slightly shifted from the Cross of Jesus... to the 'paschal joy of the resurrection'..." (Page 17 / Section A.1) - Context: Celebrating the theological shifts in the newly promulgated Constitution on the Divine Liturgy [23].
*   "The section on non-Catholic Christians is the cause of the greatest disappointment... because it clearly identifies the limits of the Church with those of the Roman Communion..." (Page 20 / Section A.2.c) - Context: Criticizing the Council's exclusive ecclesial terminology [5].
*   "...there is no act of collegiality of the body of bishops in the true sense of the word if this does not occur after the invitation and at least with the free approval and agreement of the Bishop of Rome." (Page 22 / Section A.3.d) - Context: Detailing the strict parameters of Roman collegiality [13].
*   "...to consider the episcopate alone as a sacrament in isolation from the rest of the priesthood and the fulness of the faithful can create another type of problem in the future." (Page 28 / Section C.2) - Context: Expressing a key Orthodox warning regarding the clericalization of the episcopacy [21].
*   "The whole concept of the Church is one of a Church which is structured like a pyramid and ecclesiology describes this pyramid starting from the top, from its head..." (Page 29 / Section C.3) - Context: Pointing out the central structural divergence between Eastern and Western ecclesiologies [11].

**SUMMARY**:
Nikos Nissiotis presents a rigorous, critically balanced evaluation of the second session of Vatican II, highlighting a deep theological tension between hopeful liturgical developments and the persistence of rigid Western structures [8, 11]. From his perspective as an Orthodox theologian, the Council's steps forward are constantly undermined by a "pyramid" ecclesiology that derives grace and churchly validity downward from the Papacy rather than outward from the Holy Spirit [10, 11]. Nissiotis warns that the complete absence of Pneumatology in the Schema on the Church leads to a disappointing and ecumenically damaging exclusion of non-Catholic communities, reducing them to individual believers and hindering the development of a true, cooperative dialogue among equals [5, 10, 22].
