# Unit 11 — Theological Significance

**1. The spouses are the ministers.** Unique among the seven: in the Latin tradition the **couple confect the sacrament** by their consent, and the priest or deacon is the Church's qualified witness. From this follow the centrality of consent, the whole law of nullity, and the possibility (with dispensation) of a valid Catholic marriage without a priest present.

**2. The Eastern difference is structural, not merely ceremonial.** In the Byzantine tradition the **priest is the minister** and the **crowning** is the form of the sacrament, the marriage being seen as effected by the Church's blessing rather than by the exchange of consent alone. This is a genuine divergence in sacramental theology, not a difference of custom, and it partly explains why the East can accommodate *oikonomia* for second marriages within a penitential rite while the West cannot easily do so.

**3. Covenant.** *GS* 48's replacement of contract with covenant language re-roots marriage in the biblical figure (Hosea, Ephesians 5) rather than in Roman contract law — with the consequence that fidelity is modelled on God's fidelity, which does not withdraw when the other party fails.

**4. Sacramentality and baptism.** For the baptised, **every valid marriage is a sacrament** (CIC c. 1055 §2) — there is no such thing as a merely natural marriage between two baptised persons. This has real consequences: it means two baptised non-practising Christians contract a sacramental marriage, and it complicates pastoral discussion of "who is really married."

**5. Indissolubility.** Grounded in Christ's appeal behind Moses to creation (Mk 10:6-9) and in the Ephesians analogy: an indissoluble bond signifies an irrevocable covenant. The doctrine's cost is real — it is precisely what generates the pastoral problem of the divorced and remarried — and any adequate treatment says so rather than pretending the difficulty away.

**6. Unitive and procreative.** *HV* 12's "inseparable connection" is the axis of Catholic sexual ethics. *GS* 50's refusal to rank the ends and c. 1055's *bonum coniugum* alongside procreation give the unitive dimension a standing the manual tradition did not accord it.

**7. The domestic church.** *LG* 11 and *FC* 21 make the family a genuinely ecclesial reality with its own mission, not merely a unit the Church ministers to.
