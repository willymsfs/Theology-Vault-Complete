# Synodality — Etymology, Biblical, Patristic and Magisterial Foundations (BTh)

Source: *Synodality* (Indian Edition, 2025), consulted via NotebookLM. Notebook: "The Tribal Church in
India on the Path to Synodality."

## Etymology
*Syn* ("together") + *hodos* ("path/way") = walking together on the same road. Hebrew equivalent
*Qahal Yahweh* — the convoked assembly summoned to gather and walk before God. Early Christians were
first called "followers of the Way" (Acts 9:2, 19:9) — Jesus had already named himself "the Way"
(Jn 14:6). The word "synodality" itself does not appear in the Vatican II documents; the ecclesiology
predates the vocabulary.

## Biblical roots — Old Testament
The Sinai covenantal assembly (Ex 19:4-8) constitutes Israel as a people, not a collection of
individuals. *Moshab Zeqanim* — tribal elders judging and discerning at the city gates — a
participatory structure. The classical prophets (Amos, Micah, Isaiah) defended a decentralised society
against monarchical centralisation, championing the *anawim*. Post-exilic inclusive voices (Jonah,
Ruth, Trito-Isaiah 56:1-8) challenge the narrower exclusions of Ezra and Nehemiah.

## Biblical roots — New Testament
Jesus's ministry is itinerant, lived on the road; authority is redefined from domination (*exousia*,
*dynamis* as "ruler of the Gentiles") to *kenosis* and service, modelled in the washing of feet. The
Jerusalem community (Acts 2:42-47, 4:32-37) manifests *koinonia*. The Council of Jerusalem (Acts
15:1-35) is the archetype — see [[The Council of Jerusalem as Synodal Archetype]].

## Patristic witness
John Chrysostom: "Church and Synod are synonymous." Cyprian of Carthage: "I have made it a rule... to
make no decision according to my own private opinion, without the advice of the presbyters and the
consent of the people." Augustine: *concordissima fidei conspiratio* — the most harmonious consensus of
the faithful as the active voice of the Spirit. The *Didache* and *Didascalia Apostolorum* record
bishops chosen with the active consent of the local community.
