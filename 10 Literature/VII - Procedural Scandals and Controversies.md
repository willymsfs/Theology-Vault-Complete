# Part 6 — Procedural Scandals and Controversies

1. **The Silenced Microphone (30 October 1962):** Ottaviani, exceeding his ten-minute limit, was cut off mid-speech by Cardinal Alfrink; progressive fathers applauded — an early, humiliating psychological turning point.
2. **Frings vs the Holy Office (8 November 1963):** Frings publicly condemned the Holy Office's secretive procedures; Ottaviani privately lamented, *"the French and the Germans have united everyone against us."*
3. **The Nota Praevia and the Pope's Tears (November 1964):** Thirty-five cardinals warned Paul VI that ambiguous collegiality language would be exploited post-Council; a leaked memo from an "extreme progressive theologian" confirmed the suspicion ("*we shall draw the conclusions*" after the Council); Paul VI reportedly wept before ordering the Preliminary Explanatory Note appended to *Lumen Gentium* ch. 3 by "Superior Authority," without a floor vote.
4. **The Religious Liberty Postponement and "Instanter" Revolt (19 November 1964):** Cardinal Tisserant announced *Dignitatis Humanae*'s vote would be delayed to Session IV; bishops led by Cardinal Albert Meyer gathered 441 signatures pleading *"instanter, instantius, instantissime"* — to no avail.
5. **"Mary, Mother of the Church" (21 November 1964):** Paul VI unilaterally proclaimed the title after the Theological Commission had deliberately excluded it from *Lumen Gentium* for ecumenical reasons.
6. **The St Peter's Steps Scuffle (25 November 1963):** Fr Jorge Mejía and Bishop Joseph Reuss distributed a petition against the media schema; Felici physically confronted Reuss and seized the papers, prompting new papal restrictions on periti organizing public opinion.
7. **The Sidetracked Communism Petition (1965):** 450 signatures demanding formal condemnation of Communism were quietly withheld by commission secretary Msgr Achille Glorieux; the leak to the Italian press caused a public scandal, forcing an explanatory footnote and a humiliating floor admission from Archbishop Garrone.
8. **The Nuns' Midnight Mailing Campaign (3 December 1965):** Archbishop Philip Hannan, opposing the draft condemnation of nuclear deterrence, had a dozen nuns print and hand-deliver over 2,000 letters overnight to bishops' residences — successfully forcing a clarifying amendment.
9. **Nineteen Unvoted Amendments (20 November 1964):** Paul VI inserted nineteen last-minute changes into *Unitatis Redintegratio* after the fathers had already voted on the final text.
10. **Reserved Questions:** Priestly celibacy and artificial contraception were withdrawn from conciliar debate entirely and reserved to the Pope personally — the latter exploding three years later in the *Humanae Vitae* crisis.

## The Fight over the Nota Praevia — Three Competing Interpretations of Collegiality

1. **Conservative:** the episcopal college holds supreme power only by human/positive right, granted by the Pope.
2. **Extreme liberal:** the college is the sole ultimate subject of supreme power; the Pope is bound in conscience to consult it.
3. **Moderate liberal (Paul VI's own position):** both the Pope personally *and* the college in union with the Pope hold supreme power — the position ultimately locked in by the *Nota Praevia*.
