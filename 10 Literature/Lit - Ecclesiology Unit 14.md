# Unit 14 — Pope Francis's Ecclesiological Vision and Synodality

## Evangelii Gaudium: A Program-Setting Document
Francis's first major apostolic exhortation (2013) sets the tone for the whole pontificate: "I dream of a 'missionary option'... capable of transforming everything, so that the Church's customs, ways of doing things, times and schedules, language and structures can be suitably channeled for the evangelization of today's world rather than for her self-preservation" (EG 27).

## The Poor, Listening, and Merciful Church
The poor Church for the poor recovers and extends the "preferential option for the poor" tradition (Medellín 1968, Puebla 1979) as a structural, not merely charitable, priority — continuous with Unit 6's servant model. The listening Church appeals repeatedly to the sensus fidei/sensus fidelium (Unit 9, LG 12) as a genuine locus of discernment, echoing Unit 8's shift from ecclesia docens to ecclesia discens. The merciful Church takes mercy as the interpretive key for pastoral practice, especially toward those on the margins of full ecclesial participation.

## Structural Reforms and the Synod on Synodality
Francis's pontificate has pursued a reorganized Roman Curia (culminating in the 2022 constitution Praedicate Evangelium, oriented explicitly to evangelization rather than internal governance); changed criteria for episcopal appointments, favouring pastoral closeness to the poor; a sustained effort to restore collegiality in practice, not merely in text (recalling Unit 8's tension between proclaimed and received paradigm shifts); and a series of Synods of Bishops on pressing pastoral questions — on the Family (2014-15), Young People (2018), the Pan-Amazon Region (2019), and finally the multi-year Synod on Synodality (2021-2024), which Francis framed not as one more synod but as a description of the very way the whole Church, at every level, should proceed — walking together (syn-hodos), all the baptized included, not the bishops alone.

## Course Synthesis
Francis's ecclesiology can be read as a renewed, urgent attempt to make good on Vatican II's nine paradigm shifts (Unit 8) in concrete institutional practice: People of God over hierarchy, collegiality over papal monarchy, local/world Church over Roman centralization, listening Church over merely teaching Church, kingdom-centred mission over Church-centred mission — not a departure from the Council but its unfinished renovation resumed. For the Church in India and in Bengal specifically, this vision resonates directly with the lived experience of a minority Church already practiced in listening to a religiously plural society, already engaged (through the Dalit and Adivasi apostolates) in being a poor Church for the poor, and already structured, through the CBCI and its regional bodies (Unit 11), for exactly the kind of collegial, synodal practice Francis is calling the universal Church to recover.

## Illustration
If Vatican II wrote the architectural blueprint for a renovated house (Units 8-10), and the intervening decades sometimes moved slowly or only partially on the renovation (Unit 8's "reception" problem), Francis's pontificate can be pictured as a determined return to the same blueprint with a fresh set of tools — the Synod on Synodality, curial reform, a changed profile for choosing bishops — aimed at finally finishing rooms the Council had already designed but left unbuilt.

## Indian Context
See the synthesis above: the Indian/Bengal Church's minority experience of listening, poverty, and collegial structure (CBCI/FABC) anticipates much of what Francis asks of the universal Church.
