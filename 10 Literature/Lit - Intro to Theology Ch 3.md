# Chapter III — Development and Pluralism in Theology

## Master thesis
Theology keeps a "delicate balance between maintaining the single focus — Christ, and allowing a legitimate pluralism of expression." Uniqueness of Christ + multiplicity of expression = the two facets of Christian faith. Pluralism is present **from the very beginning — inside the Bible itself**.

## A. Biblical theologies
The Bible is "a library, a collection of books and consequently of theologies."
- **OT:** Yahwist, Elohist, Priestly, Deuteronomic, Wisdom theologies → [[Old Testament Theologies]]
- **NT:** Matthean, Marcan, Lucan, Johannine; Paul's ascending/descending/cosmic Christologies → [[New Testament Theologies and Paul's Three Christologies]]
- Gospels are "anecdotal rather than biographical"; "history interpreted through faith."

## B. Patristic period (→ mid-8th c.)
Deaths marking the close: John Damascene (749), Gregory the Great (604), Isidore of Seville (639).
- Apostolic Fathers (Clement of Rome, Ignatius, Polycarp) — "pastoral, didactic and exhortatory."
- Apologists (Justin, Irenaeus, Clement of Alexandria).
- **Alexandrian vs Antiochene** schools → [[Alexandrian and Antiochene Schools]]
- Latin Fathers (Tertullian, Ambrose, Augustine).

## C. The Aristotelian turn
Theology shifts "from the plane of action to the plane of being"; need for "second order" reflection.

## D. Scholasticism (c. 800–1500)
Pre / Early / High / Late. University "lectures and disputations."
[[Anselm]] (1033–1109), [[Aquinas]] (1225–1274), [[Bonaventure]] (1221–1274), [[Duns Scotus]] (1266–1308), [[Ockham]] (1285–1347).

## E. Post-Scholastic
Reformation ([[Luther]], [[Calvin]], *Sola Scriptura*); Council of Trent; Neo-Scholasticism ([[Aeterni Patris]], 1879).

## F. Modern & contextual
Vatican II (1962–65) → "World Church." Contextual theologies → [[Contextual Theologies (Modern)]].
