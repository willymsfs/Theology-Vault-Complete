# Unit 10 — Lumen Gentium II: Body of Christ, Temple of the Spirit, Communion

## Church as Body of Christ
The image has Pauline roots (1 Cor 12; Rom 12; Eph; Col). Pius XII's 1943 encyclical Mystici Corporis had already made "Mystical Body of Christ" the Church's central self-description, but in a way that tended to identify the Body strictly with the visible, juridically-defined Roman Catholic Church (paradigm shift 5, Unit 8). LG 7-8 retains the image but insists on a "human and divine" analogy: as Christ's humanity was the living instrument of the Word, so the Church's visible, social structure serves the Spirit who builds it up, without simple identity between the visible structure and the mystery it carries.

## Church as Temple of the Holy Spirit
LG 4 and 12 describe the Spirit dwelling in the Church "as in a temple" (echoing 1 Cor 3:16), distributing "hierarchic and charismatic gifts" among the faithful — a key text grounding lay charisms alongside ordained ministry.

## Church as Communion
This is the single most consequential shift LG initiates: from a Roman-centred, juridically "universalist" model, in which the local church is a subordinate administrative unit of one global structure, toward a communio ecclesiology in which the universal Church exists as, and is realized in, the communion of local churches, each a full realization of the Church of Christ in its own place under its own bishop (LG 23, 26). The 1985 Extraordinary Synod of Bishops, called by John Paul II to mark the Council's twentieth anniversary, solemnly named "communion" as the central, organizing category for interpreting the whole of Vatican II.

## Other Biblical Images (LG 6)
LG 6 gathers further images: sheepfold (Jn 10), a cultivated field (1 Cor 3:9, "God's field"), a building with Christ as cornerstone (1 Cor 3:9-11; Eph 2:20), and, more unusually, a "stranger in a foreign land" — the Church as pilgrim, not yet home, journeying toward its true homeland (1 Pet 2:11; Heb 13:14).

## Illustration
The contrast between "universal Church commanding local branches" and "communion of local churches" is like the difference between a multinational franchise, where every branch is a standardized copy authorized from head office, and a family of full-blooded siblings, each fully and completely a member of the family, not a scaled-down copy of one "original" sibling. LG 23 and 26 insist each local church, gathered around its bishop celebrating the Eucharist, is a full sibling in the one family of churches, not a franchise outlet of Rome. (Advanced note: precisely whether the universal or the local church is theologically prior becomes a major contested debate after the Council — the Ratzinger-Kasper exchange, reserved for MTh-level study.)
