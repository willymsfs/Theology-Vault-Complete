# Unit 9 — Vatican II — The Decisive Reform

## Lumen Gentium 21 — The Sacramentality of the Episcopate

The Council's single most important teaching on this sacrament, settling the question left open since the scholastics:

> "**Episcopal consecration confers the fullness of the sacrament of Orders**... episcopal consecration confers, together with the office of sanctifying, the offices also of teaching and of governing."

Three consequences:
1. **The episcopate is the fullness of the sacrament** — priesthood is a participation in it, not its summit.
2. The **tria munera** — teaching (*docendi*), sanctifying (*sanctificandi*), governing (*regendi*) — are conferred **by consecration itself**, not by subsequent papal appointment. This is the sacramental foundation of collegiality.
3. The medieval framing of episcopacy as mere jurisdiction added to priesthood is set aside.

**The qualification** — added by the *Nota Praevia* — is that the *exercise* of the offices of teaching and governing requires hierarchical communion with the head and members of the college. Consecration confers the offices; communion regulates their exercise.

## Lumen Gentium 10 — The Two Priesthoods

> "Though they differ from one another in essence and not only in degree (***essentia et non gradu tantum***), the common priesthood of the faithful and the ministerial or hierarchical priesthood are nonetheless **ordered one to another**; each in its own proper way shares in the one priesthood of Christ."

This is a carefully balanced formula and must be quoted precisely. It affirms:
- Both are **real participations** in Christ's one priesthood.
- They differ **in essence**, not merely in degree — against the Protestant reduction of the ministry to a function of the common priesthood.
- They are **ordered to one another** — the ministerial priesthood exists *for* the common priesthood, not above it. *LG* 10 continues that the ministerial priest "forms and rules the priestly people."

## The Other Conciliar Texts

| Document | Content |
|---|---|
| ***LG* 28** | Presbyters as "prudent cooperators of the episcopal order," sharing in the bishop's priesthood, forming with him one **presbyterium** |
| ***LG* 29** | The **diaconate** as a distinct grade of the hierarchy, ordained "not unto the priesthood but unto the ministry" (*non ad sacerdotium sed ad ministerium*), and the restoration of the **permanent diaconate**, open to married men of mature age |
| ***Christus Dominus*** | The bishop's pastoral office; episcopal conferences; the Synod of Bishops |
| ***Presbyterorum Ordinis*** | Shift from an isolated cultic model to a relational **servant-leader** model embedded in relationships with bishop, brother priests, and laity |
| ***Optatam Totius*** | Seminary reform: holistic formation integrating philosophy and theology with Scripture, pastoral experience, and psychological evaluation |

## The Reformed Rites

- ***Sacrum Diaconatus Ordinem*** (Paul VI, 18 June 1967) — restores the **permanent diaconate** in the Latin Church, including for married men over 35.
- ***Pontificalis Romani*** (Paul VI, 18 June 1968) — the revised **ordination rites**, with new consecratory prayers; the prayer for episcopal ordination is taken substantially from the ***Apostolic Tradition***, another instance of *ressourcement* reaching behind the medieval West to the third century.
- ***Ministeria Quaedam*** (1972) — **suppresses the minor orders and the subdiaconate** in the Latin Church, replacing them with the lay **ministries of lector and acolyte**. The medieval seven orders are thus formally dismantled.
