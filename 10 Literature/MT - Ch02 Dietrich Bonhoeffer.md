# Ch. 02 — Dietrich Bonhoeffer
*Chapter author: Wayne Whitson Floyd*

## Core Idea
Bonhoeffer centred theology on the radical sociality of human existence — the church as "Christ existing as community" — and on costly, "this-worldly" discipleship against "cheap grace."

## Detailed Explanation
Born in Breslau into an elite academic and musical family, Bonhoeffer completed his doctorate in Berlin at 21. A fellowship year at New York's Union Theological Seminary exposed him to social praxis and, in his own words, turned him "from phraseology to reality." Back in Germany he co-founded the Pastors' Emergency League, directed the underground Finkenwalde seminary training pastors for the Confessing Church, and eventually joined the Abwehr military-intelligence conspiracy against Hitler. Arrested in 1943, he was hanged at Flossenbürg concentration camp on 9 April 1945, days before liberation. His prison writings pushed toward a "religionless Christianity" for a world "come of age," and, provocatively, criticised even Barth's own theology as a "positivism of revelation" that simply asserted doctrine without engaging the concrete, secular world.

## Example
Bonhoeffer's decision to return to Germany from the relative safety of New York in 1939 — and his subsequent participation in the assassination plot against Hitler — embodies Stellvertretung: he chose to bear, in his own body, the burden and moral risk of his neighbours' suffering rather than remain a theologian only in "phraseology."

## Illustration
"Cheap grace" is like a store selling forgiveness on permanent clearance — take it off the shelf, no questions asked, no change required. Bonhoeffer's "costly grace" is like a gift so valuable that receiving it genuinely changes how you live, because the giver paid for it with his own life and asks you to follow the same path.

## Key Terms
- **Stellvertretung** (German) — "vicarious representative action": The willingness to take on the burden and claim of another person as one's own, following Christ's own pattern of standing in for others.
- **Analogia Relationis** (Latin) — "analogy of relationship": Human likeness to God (imago Dei) understood not as shared static qualities (analogia entis) but as being-in-relationship, mirroring the Trinity's own relational life.
- **Cor Curvum in Se** (Latin) — "the heart curved in on itself": Bonhoeffer's definition of sin: a self-enclosed, defensive posture that remains closed to God and neighbour.
- **Billige Gnade** (German) — "cheap grace": Grace preached and received as a bargain — forgiveness without repentance, discipleship, or the cross.

## Connections
Shaped early by Barth's Christocentrism, Bonhoeffer later criticised Barth's "positivism of revelation" from prison. His "worldly holiness" and "religionless Christianity" directly inspired John A. T. Robinson's Honest to God (Part II.B), and he borrowed Tillich's "dimension of depth" image even while, as Eberhard Bethge put it, "Tillich discovered the world; Bonhoeffer discovered the church."
