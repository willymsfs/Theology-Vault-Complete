# Unit 6 — Reformation and Trent

## The Reformers

**Luther** called marriage "a worldly thing" (*ein weltlich Ding*) — **not a sacrament**, since it lacks a divine promise of grace attached to a sign and existed before Christ among all peoples. But he held it in the highest esteem as a **divine estate** and an order of creation, married Katharina von Bora, and wrote warmly on family life. Crucially, he assigned marriage jurisdiction to the **civil authority**, and allowed **divorce with remarriage** in cases of adultery and desertion, appealing to the Matthean exception and to 1 Cor 7:15.

**Calvin** likewise denied its sacramentality (*Institutes* IV.19.34-37), calling its inclusion among the sacraments a late invention resting on the Vulgate's rendering of *mystērion* as *sacramentum* — a genuinely sharp philological point. Geneva's marriage ordinances placed marriage under the consistory and permitted divorce for adultery and desertion.

**The Anglican settlement** retained a solemn liturgical rite in the Book of Common Prayer with its enduring vows ("to have and to hold... till death us do part"), while placing marriage law under the Crown — as the events of the 1530s had made unavoidable.

## Trent, Session XXIV (11 November 1563)

**The doctrine** (twelve canons), affirming against the Reformers:

| Canon | Teaching |
|---|---|
| 1 | Matrimony is **truly and properly one of the seven sacraments** of the New Law, instituted by Christ |
| 2 | Polygamy is unlawful |
| 4 | The Church may establish **impediments** and grant dispensations |
| 5 | The bond is **not dissolved by heresy, difficulty of cohabitation, or wilful desertion** |
| 7 | The Church does **not err** in teaching that the bond cannot be dissolved on account of adultery |
| 10 | The state of **virginity or celibacy is better and more blessed** than being bound in marriage |

**Note the careful wording of canon 7.** It anathematises the claim that *the Church errs* in teaching indissoluble marriage after adultery — rather than directly anathematising the practice itself. This formulation was deliberately chosen so as not to condemn the **Greek Orthodox** practice of *oikonomia*, with Venetian bishops representing Greek communities pressing the point. It is a fine example of conciliar precision serving an ecumenical purpose four centuries before the word existed.

## Tametsi — The Decree That Changed Everything

The reform decree of the same session finally solved the clandestinity problem by requiring, **for validity**, that marriage be contracted **before the parish priest (or a delegate) and two or three witnesses**.

**This is the single most consequential practical change in the history of the sacrament.** For fifteen centuries a marriage was valid by consent however expressed; from 1563 the Church made **canonical form** a condition of validity for Catholics.

**Its flaw:** *Tametsi* bound only in parishes where it had been **promulgated**, producing centuries of legal chaos in mission territories and mixed regions where its force was uncertain. This was finally resolved by **Ne Temere** (1907), which imposed canonical form universally.
