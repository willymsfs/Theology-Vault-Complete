# Unit 6 — Biblical Foundations of Confirmation

Confirmation has no single dominical institution text. Its scriptural warrant is a **converging pattern**: a distinct gift of the Spirit, associated with the laying on of hands and with anointing, following upon baptism.

## The Two Foundational Narratives

- **Acts 8:14-17 (Samaria)** — Philip has baptised, "but the Spirit had not yet fallen on any of them." Peter and John come down, lay hands, and they receive the Spirit. **This is the single most important text for Confirmation as a distinct rite** — and the Fathers read it as establishing that the *apostolic* (that is, episcopal) hand is required.
- **Acts 19:1-7 (Ephesus)** — Paul baptises and lays on hands; the Spirit comes with tongues and prophecy.

## Supporting Texts

- **Heb 6:2** — "laying on of hands" listed among the *foundational* teachings, alongside baptisms and resurrection.
- **2 Cor 1:21-22** — "he has **anointed** us (*chrisas*), and has **sealed** us (*sphragisamenos*), and given us the Spirit as a **pledge** (*arrabōn*) in our hearts." All three key words in one verse.
- **Eph 1:13-14** — "sealed with the promised Holy Spirit, who is the guarantee of our inheritance"; cf. **Eph 4:30**, "the Holy Spirit in whom you were sealed for the day of redemption."
- **1 Jn 2:20, 27** — "you have been anointed (*chrisma*) by the Holy One... his anointing teaches you about everything."
- **Isaiah 11:2** — the source of the **sevenfold gifts** (wisdom, understanding, counsel, fortitude, knowledge, piety, fear of the Lord — the Septuagint/Vulgate reading of a doubled term gives seven rather than six).

## Pentecost as Archetype

Confirmation is to the individual Christian what Pentecost was to the Church: the Spirit given for **mission and witness** (Acts 1:8). The Baptism-Confirmation relationship mirrors the Easter-Pentecost relationship.
