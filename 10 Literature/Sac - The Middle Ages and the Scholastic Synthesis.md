# Unit 3 — The Middle Ages and the Scholastic Synthesis

## Fixing the Number: Seven

For a thousand years the Church celebrated sacraments without agreeing how many there were. *Sacramentum* (Latin for the Greek *mystērion*) was used loosely — Hugh of St Victor could count around thirty. The **septenary** was fixed by **Peter Lombard, *Sentences* IV** (c. 1150s), which became the standard theology textbook of the medieval university, and was made magisterial at the **Second Council of Lyons (1274)** and **Florence (1439)**.

## Aquinas on Baptism (ST III qq. 66-71)

| Category | Baptism |
|---|---|
| **Matter** (*materia*) | Natural water — remote matter; the washing itself — proximate matter |
| **Form** (*forma*) | "I baptise you in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" |
| **Minister** | Ordinarily a bishop, priest, or deacon; **in necessity, anyone** — even a non-Christian — with right intention |
| **Effects** | Remission of original and all actual sin *and all punishment due*; infusion of grace and virtues; incorporation into Christ and the Church; indelible character |
| **Necessity** | Necessary for salvation — but see the three baptisms below |

**The three baptisms** (*ST* III q.66 a.11) — the doctrine that saves Catholic theology from a cruel exclusivism:

- ***Baptismus fluminis*** — of water (the sacrament proper).
- ***Baptismus sanguinis*** — of blood (martyrdom for Christ).
- ***Baptismus flaminis*** — of desire (*in voto*), explicit or implicit.

**The scholastic triple distinction** — memorise this; it unlocks everything:

| Level | Baptism |
|---|---|
| ***sacramentum tantum*** (the sign alone) | the washing with water plus the words |
| ***res et sacramentum*** (reality-and-sign) | the **character** — indelible, hence unrepeatable |
| ***res tantum*** (the reality alone) | sanctifying grace — this alone can be blocked by the recipient's disposition |

This is precisely how the tradition explains a *valid but unfruitful* baptism: the middle level is present even when the third is obstructed. Remove the obstacle (repentance), and the grace "revives" — the doctrine of ***reviviscentia***.

***Ex opere operato***. Literally "from the work worked." Often mistranslated in exam scripts as "automatically" or "magically." It means: **the efficacy of the sacrament derives from Christ's action, not from the moral worth of the minister or the recipient.** Its complement, ***ex opere operantis***, names the fruitfulness that *does* depend on the recipient's dispositions. Both are needed. Sacraments are not magic (which would drop *ex opere operantis*) and not mere psychology (which would drop *ex opere operato*).

**Illustration.** A cheque written by a wealthy benefactor is valid whether the postman who delivers it is honest or a scoundrel — *ex opere operato*. But it does you no good until you endorse and deposit it — *ex opere operantis*. And once written in your name, it is *your* cheque even if it lies uncashed in a drawer for twenty years — *character*.

## The Conciliar Documents

- **Lateran IV (1215), c. 1 *Firmiter*** — "there is one universal Church of the faithful outside which no one at all is saved"; baptism rightly conferred "by anyone in the form of the Church" is profitable to salvation.
- **Lyons II (1274)** — Michael Palaeologus's profession of faith lists the seven sacraments.
- **Council of Florence, *Exsultate Deo* / Decree for the Armenians (22 Nov 1439)** — the finest medieval summary: for each sacrament it specifies **matter, form, minister, and effect**. For baptism: matter = true and natural water; form = the Trinitarian formula; minister = a priest ordinarily, anyone in necessity; effect = remission of original and actual sin and all punishment.

## Medieval Practice

- **Infant baptism universal**, normally *quam primum* (as soon as possible) for fear of limbo.
- **Immersion gives way to affusion** (pouring) across the West by the late Middle Ages.
- **Emergency baptism by midwives** — manuals instruct laywomen in the formula; some dioceses supplied syringes for intrauterine baptism in obstructed labour. A grim but revealing index of how seriously the necessity of baptism was taken.
- **The catechumenate survives only as a compressed ritual fossil** inside the infant rite — the exorcisms, the *ephphetha*, the salt, the renunciations answered by godparents.
