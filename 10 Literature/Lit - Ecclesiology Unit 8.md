# Unit 8 — Reception of Vatican II: Paradigm Shifts and Critiques

## The Nine Paradigm Shifts
Vatican II re-categorized the Church's self-understanding across nine axes, each a move from a pre-conciliar to a post-conciliar category: (1) Institutional Church → Mystery dimension (LG 1-8). (2) Hierarchy (a pyramid of pope-cardinals-bishops-priests-deacons-passive flock) → People of God (LG 9-17), a baptismal, pilgrim people. (3) Papal Monarchy → Episcopal Collegiality (LG 18-24), a synodal, collaborative exercise of authority. (4) Universal/Roman Church dominating local jurisdictions → Local Church/World Church (Rahner's "communion of local churches"). (5) Roman Catholicism, exclusively identified with Christ's Church → Christian Church (Unitatis Redintegratio's subsistit in, real if imperfect presence in other communities). (6) Christianity as the one true religion, holding an exclusive monopoly on truth → Many religions (Nostra Aetate: "rays of that Truth which enlightens all"). (7) Church-centred/teaching Church (ecclesia docens) → Listening/learning Church (ecclesia discens), engaging the human and empirical sciences and lived experience. (8) Static, "perfect society" isolated from history → Dynamic, evolving pilgrim Church, embedded in history. (9) Church-centred mission (recruiting members; salvation confined to the Roman Catholic Church) → Kingdom-centred mission (the Church as visible, provisional sacrament of the Kingdom of God).

## Reception Critiques
"Sedevacantism" — the fringe position that the papal see has been effectively vacant since Vatican II, judging the Council a rupture with tradition — and the Coetus Internationalis Patrum, a minority bloc of conservative council fathers who resisted many reforms from within, are the precursors of later traditionalist resistance (e.g. Archbishop Lefebvre's Society of St Pius X).

From the opposite direction, Hans Küng and Peter Phan argue the Council's own paradigm shifts have been only partially and unevenly implemented in practice — a "restorationist" tendency in the decades since has pulled some dials back toward pre-conciliar categories, especially regarding the real authority of episcopal conferences, and on questions the Council itself left officially unresolved: the precise scope of infallibility, mandatory priestly celibacy, and the ordination of women (flagged by the 1976 CDF declaration Inter Insigniores and reaffirmed by John Paul II's 1994 Ordinatio Sacerdotalis).

## Illustration
The nine shifts can be pictured as nine dial settings on a control panel, each moved from one position to another at the Council — but a dial that has been turned does not stay turned by itself. Sedevacantists want to turn all nine back; critics like Küng and Phan argue that, in practice, several dials have quietly drifted back toward their old settings even though official teaching says they were moved.
