# 12. ecclesiology of Yves congar.pdf

**TITLE**: "THE ECCLESIOLOGY OF YVES CONGAR: AN ORTHODOX EVALUATION." By Iakovos, Metropolitan of Germany [24]. Published in *The Greek Orthodox Theological Review*, Vol. XV, No. 1, Spring 1970, pages 85–106 [25, 26] (A summary of a doctoral dissertation submitted to Boston University School of Theology in 1968) [26].

**MAIN SUBJECT**:
A systematic exposition and Eastern Orthodox theological critique of the ecclesiology of Yves Congar, evaluating his contributions to the theology of the laity, reform, and Tradition while highlighting his continued reliance on Western scholastic and legalistic structures [25-27].

**KEY TOPICS**:
*   Congar’s Thomistic training and his ecumenical return to patristic and biblical sources [25, 26].
*   The trinitarian, christological, and pneumatological foundations of the mystery of the Church [28-30].
*   The structure of the Church as both an "organism" and an "organization" [31].
*   The dualism between the "teaching" and "taught" aspects of the Church [32].
*   The theology of the laity, *sensus fidei*, and the dynamic role of lay charisms [32, 33].
*   Scripture, Tradition, and the theological mechanics of "dogmatic development" [34-36].
*   Orthodox and Protestant ecumenical dialogues and pathways to concrete reconciliation [37-39].

**CONCEPTS EXPLAINED**:
1.  **Organism vs. Organization**: Congar's dual structural paradigm [31]. The "organism" (derived from Möhler) represents the invisible, spiritual communion of grace, whereas the "organization" is the visible, socio-juridical framework governed by the hierarchy [31, 32].
2.  **Sensus Fidei (Sense of Faith)**: The layman's intuitive capacity for preserving the faith [32]. In Congar's view, this is primarily a passive "power of adhesion and a sense of oneness" rather than an active authority in the formulation of church doctrine [32, 40].
3.  **Dogmatic Development**: The Tübingen School-inspired theory asserting that while the objective revelation of Christ is closed, its subjective understanding can grow, unfold, and change dynamically within the Church over time [35, 36].
4.  **Ex Opere Operato Holiness**: The view that the Church's means of grace (sacraments and teaching) are holy and infallible in themselves because of God's objective involvement, even though the administering clergy remain fallible, sinful men [41].

**ISSUES ADDRESSED**:
1.  **The Objective-Noetic Distortion of Revelation**: The Western scholastic tendency (traced to Augustine and Thomas Aquinas) that treats revelation as a package of conceptual knowledge (*gnosis*) or moral law to be acquired, rather than a realistic, existential new being in Christ [42-44].
2.  **The Subordination of the Holy Spirit (*Filioque*)**: How Congar’s defense of the *filioque* clause uses the temporal economic dependence of the Spirit on the Word to deduce an ontological, eternal dependence, which the Orthodox argue over-rationalizes the Trinity and restricts the Spirit’s free action [45, 46].
3.  **The Clericalization of Grace**: How Congar’s heavy structural framework ties the flow of grace and salvation exclusively to the mediation of the hierarchy, leaving little room for the unmediated communion of the believer with the Savior [47, 48].
4.  **The Relativization of the Christ-Event**: The risk that Congar's theory of dogmatic development treats the historic incarnation as a mere "beginning" rather than the absolute, complete fullness of truth, making the contemporary Church seem more important than the apostolic era [36, 49].

**IMPORTANT QUOTES**:
*   "Christ is the principle of the objective truth of revelation while the Church and the Holy Spirit are the means that impart it to men. Thus we have in Congar also the western moralistic view." (Page 89 / Section II.B) - Context: Critiquing Congar's scholastic framing of faith and grace [43].
*   "Congar is called the architect of the structural form of the Church. His ecclesiology consists of elaborating a sui generis structure..." (Page 90 / Section III.A) - Context: Outlining Congar's synthesis of organism and organization [31].
*   "...he minimizes the existential meaning of salvation as deliverance from sin and a safeguard in the present, through a hope for the hereafter..." (Page 92 / Section III.B) - Context: Warning against the heavy hierarchical mediation that dominates Congar's model [48].
*   "Catholicity also seems in him a function... its authority leaves very little room for the sanctifying and unifying power of the Holy Spirit." (Page 95 / Section IV.B) - Context: Evaluating the functional limits of Catholicity when divorced from dynamic Pneumatology [40].
*   "By making the magisterium the subject of Tradition he turns the whole theory all the way around and makes the present time of the Church more important than the time of Christ..." (Page 98 / Section V.B) - Context: Analyzing the systematic dangers of Congar's post-Tridentine view of Tradition [49].

**SUMMARY**:
Iakovos, Metropolitan of Germany, provides an appreciative but firm Orthodox critique of Yves Congar's massive ecclesiological framework, celebrating his irenic ecumenical openness while identifying major structural shortcomings [24, 25, 50]. He argues that despite Congar’s return to patristic sources, his theology remains captive to a Western moralistic and Thomistic perspective that treats faith as an objective system of knowledge and subordinates the Holy Spirit to the institutional magisterium [40, 42, 43]. Ultimately, Iakovos asserts that Congar's defense of papal primacy and his theory of dogmatic development place too heavy an emphasis on visible, juridical mediation, which risks eclipsing the unmediated spiritual presence of Christ and obscuring the simple, existential communion of the believer [36, 51, 52].
