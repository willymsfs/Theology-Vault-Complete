# Ch. 08 — Jürgen Moltmann
*Chapter author: Richard Bauckham*

## Core Idea
Moltmann's "theology of hope" holds cross and resurrection together dialectically, replaces monarchical monotheism with a "social Trinity" of co-suffering, perichoretic persons, and translates directly into political solidarity with victims and ecological care for creation.

## Detailed Explanation
Conscripted into the German army in WWII, Moltmann's life was shattered in the 1943 firebombing of Hamburg, when the friend standing beside him was killed — a "negative contrast experience" that turned him into a seeker after God. As a prisoner of war in Scotland and England he encountered the crucified Christ's cry of dereliction ("My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?") and it became the seed of his entire theology of a God who enters, rather than stands above, human suffering. He studied at Göttingen and became Professor of Systematic Theology at Tübingen (1967–1994). Where classical theism pictures an impassible, all-powerful monarch-God, Moltmann's crucified God freely suffers with creation, and the Trinity itself becomes the pattern for non-hierarchical human and ecological community.

## Example
Moltmann's theology of the "crucified God" and his ecological doctrine of creation as the Spirit's home directly informed the 1980s-90s World Council of Churches emphasis on "Justice, Peace, and the Integrity of Creation" — showing how his academic Trinitarian theology translated into concrete ecumenical social and environmental advocacy.

## Illustration
Most classical pictures of God are like a king watching a battle safely from a hilltop, directing but untouched. Moltmann's crucified God is more like a parent who runs into a burning building after their child — not remaining safely outside the disaster, but taking the full cost of it into their own body.

## Key Terms
- **Social Trinity**: God as a non-hierarchical community of equal divine persons in mutual indwelling (perichoresis), offered as the pattern for human relationships of freedom and equality.
- **Perichoresis** (Greek) — "mutual indwelling": The dynamic, reciprocal interpenetration of the three trinitarian persons — for Moltmann, a social and political, not just metaphysical, model.
- **Trinitarian Panentheism**: The eschatological hope that God will ultimately mutually indwell the whole of creation, drawing it into the trinitarian life.
- **Christologia Viae** (Latin) — "Christology of the way": A dynamic, unfinished Christology of Jesus still "on the way" toward a promised messianic future, rather than a completed, static dogma.

## Connections
Initially followed Barth, later broke with him to stress lived experience of the Spirit over Barth's tightly bounded revelation. Colleague of Pannenberg at Wuppertal; retrieved Bonhoeffer's social ethics; drew on the Marxist philosopher Ernst Bloch's "philosophy of hope." Criticised by von Balthasar for risking the collapse of eternal, inner-trinitarian suffering into mere temporal world-process. Strongly influenced the Church of England's social theology in the 1970s-80s, though Rowan Williams later worried it risked "sacralizing" the present social order.
