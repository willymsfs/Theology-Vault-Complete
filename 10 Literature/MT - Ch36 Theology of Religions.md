# Ch. 36 — Theology of Religions
*Chapter author: Gavin D'Costa*

## Core Idea
D'Costa maps the classic pluralism/inclusivism/exclusivism typology for Christian responses to religious diversity, argues all three are ultimately variants of a single underlying exclusivism, and proposes a return to explicitly trinitarian theology of religions that holds difference and commonality together without dissolving either into an abstract, secularized pluralism.

## Detailed Explanation
Pluralism (all religions are equally valid paths to "the Real"; Christ is one relative revelation among many), inclusivism (Christ is the definitive revelation, but grace works causally within other religions too), and exclusivism (salvation requires explicit faith in Christ, mediated through gospel and baptism) organized most twentieth-century Christian thinking about other faiths. D'Costa, an Indian Roman Catholic theologian and consultant to the Pontifical Commission for Interreligious Dialogue, controversially argues pluralism is not neutral at all but its own kind of "exclusivist secular agnosticism" — an intolerant ideology that quietly controls the public sphere by reducing every religion to equivalent private opinion.

## Example
D'Costa's critique that John Hick's pluralism functions as a hidden new orthodoxy — requiring every religion to accept, in advance, that its own most particular and exclusive truth-claims are merely mythical — plays out concretely whenever interfaith dialogue events implicitly require participants to bracket exactly the convictions (like the unique divinity of Christ) that matter most to them.

## Illustration
Pluralism presents itself like a referee who claims to be entirely neutral between all the teams on the field — but D'Costa's point is that a referee who insists no team is allowed to actually win, or that every goal must count exactly the same regardless of the game each team thinks it's playing, has already imposed a very particular (and disputable) rule of its own.

## Key Terms
- **Pluralism**: The view that all religions are equally valid paths to one divine Reality, with Christ as one relative revelation among many.
- **Inclusivism**: Christ as the normative, definitive revelation, while grace is nonetheless causally active within other religions.
- **Exclusivism**: Salvation tied strictly to explicit faith in Christ, mediated through hearing the Gospel and baptism.
- **Exclusivist Secular Agnosticism**: D'Costa's critical label for pluralism itself, which he argues functions as its own intolerant orthodoxy despite claiming neutrality.

## Connections
Rahner's inclusivist "anonymous Christianity" (Part I) is D'Costa's primary dialogue partner, though he seeks a more explicitly trinitarian alternative. John Hick is his chief target of critique. Aloysius Pieris (Part V) represents a liberationist, socially engaged approach prioritizing justice over doctrine. George Lindbeck's postliberalism (Part II.D) informs D'Costa's stress on Christianity's irreducible particularity.
