# Unit 7 — The Reformation — Presence and Sacrifice

The Reformation divided over the Eucharist more bitterly than over any other doctrine — and, crucially, over **two separable questions**. Keep them apart in every essay.

## Question One: The Real Presence

| Position | Figure | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Transubstantiation** | Catholic teaching | Substance converted; accidents remain |
| **Sacramental union** (popularly "consubstantiation" — a term Luther never used and disliked) | **Luther** | Christ's body and blood truly present "**in, with, and under**" the bread and wine, which remain bread and wine; supported by his doctrine of the **ubiquity** of Christ's glorified body |
| **Memorialism** | **Zwingli** | *"Est"* means *significat*; the Supper is a memorial and a public pledge of allegiance; Christ's body is locally in heaven and cannot be on earth |
| **Spiritual real presence / instrumental** | **Calvin** | Christ is truly given and truly fed upon, **by the power of the Holy Spirit**; but rather than Christ descending into the elements, the communicant is **lifted up** to Christ — hence the liturgical ***Sursum corda*** taken with full seriousness (*Institutes* IV.17) |
| **Receptionism / virtualism** | **Cranmer** and the English tradition | Christ is received by the faithful communicant in the act of reception; the elements are not changed. Compare the 1549 words of administration ("The body of our Lord Jesus Christ...") with the 1552 revision ("Take and eat this **in remembrance** that Christ died for thee") — the doctrinal shift is visible in the liturgy itself |

**The Marburg Colloquy (1529)** is the set-piece. Philip of Hesse convened Luther and Zwingli to unite the Protestant cause; they agreed on fourteen of fifteen articles and split irreparably on the fifteenth. Luther chalked ***"HOC EST CORPUS MEUM"*** on the table and refused to move from it, reportedly telling Zwingli, "You have a different spirit from ours." Protestantism has never had a single Eucharistic doctrine since.

**Teaching point.** Note the irony worth an essay paragraph: Luther is closer to Rome on *presence* than to Zwingli, while being further from Rome than Zwingli on *sacrifice*. The Reformation is not a single spectrum.

## Question Two: The Sacrifice of the Mass

This was, for Luther, the graver error — an "abomination" — and the objections were three:

1. **Hebrews** insists Christ's sacrifice was **once for all** (*ephapax*, Heb 7:27, 9:12, 10:10); to offer again implies its insufficiency.
2. It makes the Mass a **human work** offered to God, undermining justification by faith alone.
3. It underwrote the economy of **private and votive Masses, stipends, and chantries** — the practical abuses everyone could see.

The Catholic reply, formulated at Trent, is that the Mass does not *repeat* Calvary but **re-presents** it: the same victim and the same priest, "the manner of offering alone being different" — bloody on the Cross, unbloody on the altar. The theological load-bearing concept is *anamnesis* in its Hebraic sense (Unit 2).
