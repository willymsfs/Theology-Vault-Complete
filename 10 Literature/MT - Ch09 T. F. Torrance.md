# Ch. 09 — T. F. Torrance
*Chapter author: Daniel W. Hardy*

## Core Idea
Torrance built a "scientific theology" treating the self-revealed, triune God as the objective reality theology investigates, arguing theology must conform its concepts to that reality exactly as physics conforms its theories to the actual, contingent order of the physical universe.

## Detailed Explanation
Born in West China to missionary parents, Torrance studied classics and philosophy at Edinburgh before completing a doctoral thesis on the Apostolic Fathers under Karl Barth in Basel. After a spell as a parish minister in Aberdeen, he held the chairs of Church History and then Christian Dogmatics at Edinburgh (1952-1979). His central move is an analogy with the philosophy of science: just as a physicist must let the nature of light or gravity determine the right method for studying it (rather than imposing a method in advance), so theology must let the self-revealing, triune God determine theological method — an approach that made him an unusually confident conversation partner with working scientists.

## Example
Torrance's sustained dialogue with Einsteinian relativity and with the physicist-philosopher Michael Polanyi's account of scientific discovery (the "heuristic leap" of insight) gave him real standing in science-and-religion conferences where most theologians of his generation could not follow the technical physics — a concrete instance of theology disciplining itself to a rigor borrowed from the natural sciences.

## Illustration
You would not study a whale using the methods of geology, or a mountain range using the methods of marine biology — each object of study dictates its own appropriate method. Torrance's "scientific theology" insists God likewise dictates God's own appropriate method of study; theology goes wrong whenever it borrows a method built for studying something else (human religious feeling, say, or abstract philosophical categories) instead of letting the living God set the terms.

## Key Terms
- **Scientific Theology**: Theology conducted in rigorous conformity to the nature of its actual object — the self-revealing, triune God — rather than to a borrowed, external method.
- **Cataphatic / Apophatic Spiral** (Greek roots) — "positive / negative": A spiralling method in which positive claims about God (cataphatic) and acknowledgment of God's transcendent mystery (apophatic) continually correct and enrich each other.
- **Heuristic Leap**: Borrowed from Michael Polanyi: the mental leap by which the mind fuses scattered clues of experience into one unified insight.
- **Contingent Order**: The universe's real, rational order, which is not self-explanatory but depends entirely on the free, loving will of God.

## Connections
A devoted student of Barth and the principal English translator of the Church Dogmatics, Torrance stayed within Barth's basic dogmatic frame but extended it, as Barth himself never did, into sustained dialogue with the natural sciences. Close colleague of Colin Gunton (Part II.D), though Gunton eventually departed from Torrance's residual Augustinianism.
