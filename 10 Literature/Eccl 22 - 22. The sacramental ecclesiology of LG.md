# 22. The sacramental ecclesiology of LG.pdf

**TITLE**: "The Sacramental Ecclesiology of Lumen Gentium." By Avery Cardinal Dulles, S.J. Published in *Gregorianum*, Vol. 86, No. 3, 2005, pages 550–562 [33].

**MAIN SUBJECT**:
An analysis of the recovery, development, and systematic implications of the concept of the Church as "sacrament" in the Second Vatican Council's Dogmatic Constitution *Lumen Gentium*, demonstrating how this model resolves preconciliar juridical limitations and ecumenical blockages [34, 35].

**KEY TOPICS**:
*   The pre-conciliar preparation and the agendas proposed by Cardinals Suenens and Montini [33, 36].
*   The transition from the juridical framework of *Mystici Corporis* to the "mystery" of the Church [35].
*   The patristic and medieval recovery of sacramental vocabulary (de Lubac, Rahner, Semmelroth) [37-39].
*   The ontological and symbolic properties of a sacrament applied to the Church (*sacramentum et res*) [40, 41].
*   The use of sacrament to balance and control biblical images like the "Body of Christ" and "People of God" [42-44].
*   The ecumenical breakthrough of the *subsistit in* formula [45-47].
*   Episcopal collegiality, Papal Primacy, and the lay state viewed through a sacramental lens [48-52].
*   The eschatological finality and holiness of the pilgrim Church, with Mary as its archetype [53, 54].

**CONCEPTS EXPLAINED**:
1.  **Veluti Sacramentum (Like a Sacrament)**: The core theological analogy of *Lumen Gentium* 1, defining the Church as a visible sign and historical instrument of intimate union with God and of the unity of all humankind [35].
2.  **Sacramentum et Res**: A medieval scholastic category applied to the Church: the visible, hierarchical social structure is the *sacramentum tantum* (the bare sign), while the inner heavenly grace and Trinitarian communion is the *res sacramenti* (the spiritual fruit) [41, 55].
3.  **Subsistit In**: The historic linguistic shift in LG 8 (replacing *est*) which asserts that while the Church of Christ exists in its substantial completeness in the Roman Catholic communion, authentic "elements of sanctification and truth" are present in non-Catholic communities [45-47].
4.  **Hierarchical Communion**: The sacramental bond among the ordained, indicating that episcopal ordination confers a permanent participation in Christ's own office of shepherd, which must be exercised in communion with the whole college of bishops and the Pope [49].
5.  **Universal Sacrament of Salvation**: The missionary designation of the Church as the global, visible sign through which Christ permanently communicates his saving grace to all nations [56, 57].

**ISSUES ADDRESSED**:
1.  **The Scholastic Restriction of the Term "Sacrament"**: Overcoming preconciliar manualist objections that using the term analogously for the Church would dilute the definition of the seven formal sacraments or favor Modernist errors [37, 58].
2.  **The Monophysite Danger of the "Mystical Body" Model**: Countering the risk of an unhealthy, romanticized mysticism that depicts the Church as a single physical person (a "continued Incarnation") rather than a mystical union of distinct, free human persons [42, 43].
3.  **The Political Distortion of the "People of God"**: Correcting post-conciliar attempts by liberation and political theologians to use "People of God" to foster class conflict or pure democratization, by re-anchoring the term in the liturgical, worshiping framework of sacrament [44].
4.  **The Exclusive Membership Doctrine of *Mystici Corporis***: Resolving the preconciliar legalism that placed baptized non-Catholics on the same footing as unbaptized pagans, allowing instead for degrees of real but imperfect communion [48, 59-61].

**IMPORTANT QUOTES**:
*   "The light of the world that the Church is commissioned to bring to all the nations... is the light of Christ, not the Church’s own light..." (Page 550 / Passage 67) - Context: Explaining the title alteration from Pope John XXIII’s radio address in Lumen Gentium's opening lines.
*   "If Christ is the sacrament of God, the Church is for us the sacrament of Christ; she represents him, in the full and ancient meaning of the term, she really makes him present." (Page 551 / Passage 71) - Context: Quoting Henri de Lubac's seminal 1938 definition of sacramental ecclesiology.
*   "The visible structure is the sacramentum tantum (the bare sign); the heavenly grace is the res sacramenti (the fruit of the sacrament). In her totality the Church is both sacramentum and res." (Page 554-555 / Passage 88-89) - Context: Delineating the unity between the institutional and spiritual dimensions of the Church.
*   "Lumen Gentium was confronted with the question whether the identity between the Catholic Church and the Mystical Body was exclusive... Relying on the sacramental model, the Council was able to declare that while the Church of Christ... 'subsisted' in the Roman Catholic communion, this did not exclude the presence of ecclesial elements elsewhere..." (Page 556 / Passage 96-97) - Context: Explaining the dogmatic and ecumenical implications of *subsistit in*.
*   "The college without the Pope as its sacramental head would be a lifeless torso." (Page 558 / Passage 110) - Context: Defending the Pope's active, corrective role in conciliar collegiality from a non-political, sacramental perspective.
*   "The Church, embracing sinners in her bosom, is at the same time holy and always in need of being purified, and incessantly pursues the path of penance and renewal..." (Page 561 / Passage 120) - Context: Showing how Lumen Gentium balances formal holiness with historical human sinfulness.

**SUMMARY**:
Avery Cardinal Dulles analyzes "sacramental ecclesiology" as the primary organizing framework of Vatican II's *Lumen Gentium* [34, 62]. By defining the Church as a visible sign and instrument of God's union with humanity, the Council bypassed dry, preconciliar legalism without sliding into a disembodied, invisible mysticism [35, 42, 55]. Sacramental ecclesiology allowed the Council to recognize the "elements of sanctification and truth" existing in non-Catholic communities through the *subsistit in* formula, establishing a theological basis for real but imperfect ecumenical communion [45, 46, 63]. Dulles concludes that this sacramental vision successfully integrates the Church's visible hierarchy, lay ministries, and calling to holiness with its ultimate, eschatological perfection in Christ [49, 51, 52, 64].
