# Unit 10 — Casuistry, Jansenism, and Alphonsus Liguori

The post-Tridentine period produced a distinctive and often-caricatured body of work: the **moral manuals**, written to train confessors in judging concrete cases (*casus*). The genuine question underlying the whole enterprise was: **when a moral obligation is doubtful, how free is the penitent?**

## The Systems

| System | Principle | Tendency |
|---|---|---|
| **Rigorism / Tutiorism** | One must always follow the safer opinion favouring the law | Condemned as excessive (Alexander VIII, 1690) |
| **Probabiliorism** | One may follow the opinion favouring liberty only if it is **more probable** than the opinion favouring the law | Dominican tendency |
| **Equiprobabilism** | Where the opinions are **equally probable**, liberty may be followed | **Alphonsus Liguori** |
| **Probabilism** | Any **solidly probable** opinion favouring liberty may be followed, even if the contrary is more probable | Jesuit tendency |
| **Laxism** | A slenderly probable opinion suffices | Condemned (Innocent XI, 1679) |

**The caricature and the reality.** Pascal's *Provincial Letters* (1656-57) made "casuistry" a byword for moral evasion, and the laxist excesses were real enough to be condemned by Rome itself. But the underlying project — the disciplined application of principle to concrete circumstance, with attention to the penitent's actual freedom, knowledge, and constraints — is indispensable to any real pastoral practice. The modern equivalent is clinical ethics.

## Jansenism

**Antoine Arnauld's *De la fréquente communion* (1643)** argued for a return to patristic severity: penitents should be tested by long delays before absolution, and communion should be approached only rarely and after prolonged preparation.

The pastoral effect was chilling in the exact sense: **absolution deferred**, communion avoided, a generation formed in scrupulosity and fear. Jansenist rigorism is the single largest factor behind the sombre reputation of Catholic penitential practice in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, and its influence long outlasted its formal condemnation.

## Alphonsus Liguori (1696-1787)

The decisive corrective figure, and **patron of confessors and moral theologians**. His *Theologia Moralis* charted the **equiprobabilist** middle course between laxism and rigorism, and — more importantly for practice — his pastoral counsel insisted that the confessor is **father and physician before he is judge**, that absolution should not be needlessly deferred, and that scrupulous penitents must be treated with gentleness rather than severity.

**Teaching point.** Liguori is the pivot between the Tridentine juridical emphasis and the modern pastoral one. Pius X's encouragement of frequent communion (1905) and the 1973 Rite's language of healing both stand in his line. A good essay on the modern reform traces the continuity back through him rather than presenting Vatican II as a rupture.
