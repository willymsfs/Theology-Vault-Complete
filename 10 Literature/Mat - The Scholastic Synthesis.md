# Unit 5 — The Scholastic Synthesis

## Marriage Among the Seven

Marriage's inclusion among the seven sacraments met genuine resistance, on two grounds worth knowing:

1. **It existed before Christ** and among non-Christians — so how can it be a sacrament of the New Law instituted by Christ?
2. **It involves sexual intercourse**, which the tradition associated with concupiscence — so how can it confer grace?

The scholastic answer to the first: marriage exists as a *natural institution* from creation and was **raised** (*elevatum*) by Christ to sacramental dignity for the baptised. To the second: the marital act, rightly ordered, is not sinful, and the sacrament confers grace to live the state faithfully.

## Aquinas (ST Suppl. qq. 41-68)

| Category | Content |
|---|---|
| **Matter and form** | The **words or signs expressing consent** — the mutual exchange itself |
| **Minister** | **The spouses themselves.** The priest is the Church's official witness, not the minister. This is unique among the seven sacraments |
| **Effect** | The **bond** (*vinculum*) and the grace to fulfil the duties of the state |
| **Character** | **None** — hence marriage is not repeatable during the life of both spouses, but a widow or widower may remarry |
| **Ends** | **Primary: the procreation and education of offspring.** Secondary: mutual help, and the remedy for concupiscence (*remedium concupiscentiae*) |
| **The three goods** | Augustine's *proles, fides, sacramentum* retained as the framework |

**The hierarchy of ends is the point to remember**, because it is precisely what the twentieth century would contest (Units 8-9). In the scholastic framework, marriage is *defined* by its ordination to procreation; mutual love and companionship are real but **secondary**.

**That the spouses are the ministers** is the distinctive doctrinal claim of this sacrament and has large consequences: it means the sacrament is confected by the couple's free act; it grounds the Church's insistence that **defective consent invalidates** (the entire modern law of nullity, Unit 12); and it is why the Eastern tradition, which locates the form in the **priest's crowning** rather than in the exchange of consent, differs at a genuinely structural level (Unit 11).

## Late Medieval Practice

The betrothal (*sponsalia de futuro*) and the marriage proper (*sponsalia de praesenti*) were carefully distinguished, since only the latter created the bond — but popular practice conflated them constantly, generating litigation. Marriage moved gradually from the home to the **church porch** (*ad ostium ecclesiae*), where consent was exchanged before the priest, with the nuptial Mass following inside.
