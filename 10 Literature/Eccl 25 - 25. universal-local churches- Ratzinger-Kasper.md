# 25. universal-local churches- Ratzinger-Kasper.pdf

**TITLE**: "The Ratzinger/Kasper Debate: The Universal Church and Local Churches." By Kilian McDonnell, O.S.B. (President of the Institute for Ecumenical and Cultural Research, Collegeville, Minnesota) [Page 227]. Published in *Theological Studies*, Vol. 63, 2002, pages 227–250 [Page 227].

**MAIN SUBJECT**:
An exploration of the high-level theological and public debate between Cardinals Joseph Ratzinger and Walter Kasper over the ontological and temporal relationship between the universal Church and the local (particular) churches, exploring its structural and pastoral implications for papal primacy and episcopal authority [Pages 227, 228].

**KEY TOPICS**:
*   The 1992 CDF letter "Some Aspects of the Church Understood as Communion" [Page 227].
*   The theological claim of the ontological and temporal priority of the universal Church [Pages 228, 229, 234].
*   Kasper's critique of "Roman centralism" and the perceived dismissal of Vatican II's collegiality [Pages 231, 236].
*   Ratzinger's defense of pre-existence based on patristic, rabbinic, and biblical sources [Pages 229, 234, 235].
*   The limits of episcopal conferences and the critique of *Apostolos suos* [Pages 231, 232].
*   The rejection of Leonardo Boff's "ecclesiological relativism" [Pages 238, 239].
*   The theological meaning of baptism (infant baptism) as entry into the universal Church [Pages 237, 246].
*   The "perichoretic" concept of simultaneity and mutual interiority between the local and the universal [Pages 228, 230, 244, 247].

**CONCEPTS EXPLAINED**:
1.  **Ontological and Temporal Priority of the Universal Church**: Ratzinger's central thesis asserting that the one, unique Church of Christ precedes creation in God's eternal, pre-existent design and precedes any local church historically, being manifested at Pentecost as a universal entity [Pages 228, 229, 234].
2.  **Simultaneity (Simultaneität)**: Kasper's counter-thesis asserting that the universal Church and the local churches are co-original, incorporated into and interpenetrating one another perichoretically, meaning they exist simultaneously without chronological sequence [Pages 240, 244, 247].
3.  **Mutual Interiority (Mutua Interioridad)**: The conciliar principle (*Lumen Gentium* 23) that the universal Church exists *in and from* (*in et ex*) the local churches, establishing a relationship of reciprocity and mutual inclusion rather than a top-down hierarchy [Pages 228, 230].
4.  **Collegialitas Affectiva (Affective Collegiality)**: A restrictive theological category in *Apostolos suos* depicting episcopal conferences as purely pastoral, administrative, and affective gatherings rather than bodies exercising genuine "effective" collegial or teaching authority [Pages 231, 232].
5.  **Ecclesiological Relativism**: A sociological reduction of ecclesiology (associated by Ratzinger with Leonardo Boff) which views the early Church merely as a human, historical collection of diverse local assemblies with no prior universal reality [Page 238].

**ISSUES ADDRESSED**:
1.  **The Danger of Roman Centralization**: Kasper's concern that asserting the ontological priority of the universal Church de facto identifies the universal Church with the Pope and the Roman Curia, effectively reducing local dioceses to mere administrative districts of Rome [Pages 231, 236, 237].
2.  **The Reduction of "Communion" to a Political Slogan**: Ratzinger's frustration that the rich theological category of *communio* had been flattened and falsified into a dispute over administrative competencies and power sharing between dioceses and Rome [Pages 234, 237].
3.  **The Chronological and Exegetical Reading of Pentecost**: The debate over whether the Pentecost community of the Twelve in Acts 2 represents a local congregation that expands (Kasper) or the immediate, Spirit-born manifestation of the universal Church sent to all nations (Ratzinger) [Pages 231, 236, 239].
4.  **The Status of Episcopal Conferences**: Resolving whether regional bodies of bishops have true theological and teaching authority or are merely functional, practical arrangements for regional planning [Pages 231, 232].

**IMPORTANT QUOTES**:
*   "The universal Church is not the result of the communion of the churches, but in its essential mystery it is a reality ontologically and temporally prior to every individual particular church." (Page 228 / Section: CDF 1992 Letter) - Context: Outlining the core, controversial thesis of the 1992 CDF Document.
*   "If this is the case, then the 1992 Letter of the CDF cannot be considered an aid in the clarification of communion ecclesiology... but must be seen as its dismissal (Verabschiedung)." (Page 231 / Section: Reaction of Walter Kasper) - Context: Kasper's sharp warning on the centralizing consequences of the CDF's ontology.
*   "The Church is not there for itself, but to serve God's presence in the world." (Page 234 / Section: Joseph Ratzinger's Response) - Context: Ratzinger re-centering the purpose of the Church away from administrative squabbles.
*   "Jerusalem above is our mother... If one accepts this biblical and rabbinic teaching on the temporal precedence of Israel and the Church, then one cannot honestly deny the ontological priority of the one Church..." (Page 235 / Section: Joseph Ratzinger's Response) - Context: Proving the pre-existence of the Church from scripture and patristic traditions.
*   "The local church and universal Church are simultaneous in all concrete historical manifestations... I no longer care to attribute too much importance to the really rather speculative question of whether the situation is precisely the same..." (Page 244-245 / Section: Kasper's Final Response) - Context: Reaching a peaceful, practical consensus on "simultaneity" while relativizing Ratzinger's abstract ontology.
*   "To have two curial cardinals publicly discussing what may be the most important theological issue facing the coming conclave is a sign of great hope." (Page 250 / Section: Reflections and Conclusion) - Context: Closing the essay with admiration for the theological candor of the disputants.

**SUMMARY**:
Kilian McDonnell reviews the high-profile public debate between Cardinals Joseph Ratzinger and Walter Kasper over the relationship between the universal Church and local churches [Page 227]. Ratzinger (representing the CDF) defends the "ontological and temporal priority of the universal Church" as an eternal mystery pre-existing creation, warning that a one-sided emphasis on the local church collapses ecclesiology into a sociological, political power struggle (such as Leonardo Boff's relativism) [Pages 228, 234, 238]. Kasper counters that this priority risks restoring "Roman centralism" and treating local dioceses as mere administrative districts, proposing instead that the universal and local churches exist in a "perichoretic" relationship of "simultaneity" and mutual interiority [Pages 231, 240, 247]. McDonnell concludes that while the debate exposes deep differences in philosophical systems (Platonic priority vs. Aristotelian historical concrete reality), both theologians eventually converged on the practical co-existence of the universal and local in every concrete ecclesial event, such as baptism and the Eucharist [Pages 245, 247, 248].
