# Unit 10 — Theological Significance

**1. Initiation is one reality in three moments.** *CCC* 1275: "Christian initiation is accomplished by three sacraments together: Baptism which is the beginning of new life; Confirmation which is its strengthening; and the Eucharist which nourishes the disciple with Christ's Body and Blood for his transformation in Christ." Any theology that treats Confirmation in isolation has already gone wrong.

**2. Trinitarian.** Baptism is conferred in the name of the Trinity, at the Jordan the Trinity is manifested, and Confirmation seals with the Spirit whom the Father sends through the Son. Matthew 28:19's *singular* "name" is the same monotheistic-yet-triadic grammar the Cappadocians would later formalise as *mia ousia, treis hypostaseis*.

**3. Christological and Paschal.** Romans 6 makes baptism a real participation in the death and resurrection — which is why the Easter Vigil, not a private Sunday afternoon, is its native home.

**4. Pneumatological.** From Ezekiel 36 through Acts 8 to *Accipe signaculum*, the Spirit is the sacraments' common term. Confirmation exists to make explicit what baptism already contains.

**5. Ecclesial.** 1 Cor 12:13 — one is never baptised into a private relationship with Jesus but into a body. Hence godparents, hence a public rite, hence the 2020 ruling that the community cannot say "we baptise": the Church is where you are baptised, not what baptises you.

**6. Dignity and mission.** *LG* 10-11: baptism confers the **common priesthood**, confirmation confers the **obligation to witness**. Together they are the theological basis for the entire lay apostolate — which is exactly the argument *Apostolicam Actuositatem* makes.

**7. Eschatological.** *Sphragis* and *arrabōn* (2 Cor 1:22): the seal marks ownership *now*; the pledge is a down-payment on a future not yet delivered.
