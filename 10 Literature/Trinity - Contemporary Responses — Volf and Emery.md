# Part 7 — Contemporary Responses — Volf and Emery

## Miroslav Volf: After Our Likeness — The Church as the Image of the Trinity

Volf constructs a systematic Free Church ecclesiology, rescuing the Protestant "gathered community" from individualism without falling into the "ecclesiological holism" of classical Catholic and Orthodox models, which rely on trinitarian models where "earthly hierarchies tend to mirror the heavenly one," underwriting authoritarian practice.

**Critique of Zizioulas — Monarchy and Asymmetry:** Zizioulas's "monarchy of the Father" is mirrored in his eucharistic ecclesiology by the bishop acting as the "one" who constitutes the "many." Because a freely affirmed, ideally reciprocal integration is unattainable this side of history, an asymmetrical trinitarian hierarchy inevitably produces "partially coerced subordination of the many to the dominant one." Volf also suspects Zizioulas of **projection**: the monarchy of the Father is not actually necessary to secure divine unity (constituted, for Volf, by love, not causal hierarchy) — so Zizioulas may be "projecting the hierarchical grounding of unity into the doctrine of the Trinity from the perspective of a particular ecclesiology."

**Critique of Ratzinger — The Precedence of the Whole:** in Ratzinger's Augustine-influenced model, the divine persons are wholly identical with "pure relations" of being-from-the-other and being-toward-the-other; applied to human persons, this ideal calls for no independent identity. Ratzinger gives priority to the one divine substance over the three persons, translating ecclesiologically into the universal Church's precedence over local churches, the Pope's precedence over bishops, bishops over congregations. Volf: this produces a "dissolution of the respective individual identities of the various local churches."

**Volf's Symmetrical Alternative and "Trinitarian Modesty":** Volf differentiates the *relations of origin* (the Father genuinely is the source) from the *eternal relations of love* (entirely reciprocal, equal, symmetrical). Applied to church structure: rejects monarchical "one-man-rule" as "monistic and thus also un-trinitarian," advocating a collegial, polycentric understanding of office in which even a congregation of "two or three" gathered in Christ's name stands as a full image (*eikon*) of the Trinity. He guards this with **"Trinitarian modesty":** (1) the *ontological limit* — humans are not divine, so "person" and "perichoresis" apply only analogously, never univocally; (2) the *noetic/moral limit* — because of sin, humans can never perfectly image the Trinity, so it shapes a flexible "social vision," never a literal blueprint.

## Gilles Emery's Thomistic Ressourcement

The Swiss Dominican Emery offers a deliberate alternative to social trinitarianism by retrieving Aquinas's classical metaphysical precision.

**Against collapsing the immanent and economic Trinity:** targets Rahner's *Grundaxiom* directly — "there is no pure and simple identity between the 'immanent Trinity' and the 'economic' Trinity." Because humans are finite creatures, they receive knowledge of God only in a manner proportionate to their own limited condition. Posing the Trinity strictly in Rahnerian terms risks a "dialectical and even wooden" presentation; on a proper Thomistic framework, the temporal missions of Son and Spirit already bear the eternal mystery of the divine persons — so there is "no need to reunite" economic and immanent Trinity, since they were never separated.

**Divine simplicity and subsisting relations:** rather than three separate psychological subjects (risking tritheism), Emery recovers Aquinas's *relatio subsistens* — because the divine nature is simple, there are no accidental compositions or separate wills in God; the three persons share a single essence and act in absolute unity.

**Undivided operations and Trinitarian appropriations:** against the "tritheist myth" of social trinitarianism, Emery insists the Trinity's actions *ad extra* are undivided (***opera trinitatis ad extra indivisa sunt***). To still express the persons' distinctness without dividing God's action, he deploys **trinitarian appropriations** (seen already in the Victorines, Part 4): Power to the Father, Wisdom to the Son, Goodness/Love to the Spirit — each person acting according to a "proper mode of action" that is intrinsically relational, even though the underlying act is always the single, common act of the whole Trinity.

**Speculative theology as spiritual exercise:** for Emery, this classical precision is not an arid rationalistic exercise but a contemplative discipline — a **"spiritual exercise"** (*exercitatio*) of purification, stretching human language to its limits while respecting the divine mystery's incomprehensibility, guarding against anthropomorphic projection and fostering the soul's liturgical union with the triune God.
