# 13. faithful dissenters fem.pdf

**TITLE**: "FAITHFUL DISSENTERS? FEMINIST ECCLESIOLOGIES AND DISSENT." By Natalie Watson [53] (Ripon College Cuddesdon, Oxford) [54]. Published in *Scottish Journal of Theology*, Vol. 55, No. 4, 2002, pages 464–484 [54, 55].

**MAIN SUBJECT**:
An exploration of feminist ecclesiology—specifically the "women-church" movement—as a form of "faithful dissent" within a patriarchal church, comparing its methodology and models of authority with traditional Protestant non-conformist groups [53, 56, 57].

**KEY TOPICS**:
*   A working definition of religious dissent as a search for "authenticity" [56].
*   Comparative analysis of the Brethren and Pentecostal house churches versus feminist "women-church" [57-59].
*   The historical marginalization of women in patriarchal church systems [60, 61].
*   Elisabeth Schüssler Fiorenza's concept of the "discipleship of equals" and the "ekklesia of women" [55, 62, 63].
*   Rosemary Radford Ruether's definition of "women-church" as an "exodus community" [64-66].
*   The methodological distinction between objective historical "archetypes" and critical-political "prototypes" [67-69].
*   Letty Russell's *Church in the Round* and the concept of "hospitality as mission" [70, 71].
*   Models of clerical power, hierarchy, and functional authority [72-74].
*   Mary Daly's post-Christian "sisterhood as cosmic antichurch" [75-77].
*   Mary McClintock Fulkerson's poststructuralist critique of women's academic discourses of faith [78, 79].
*   The Church as a storytelling community performing the narrative of Christ [80, 81].

**CONCEPTS EXPLAINED**:
1.  **Dissent (Religious)**: Not simple disagreement, but a deliberate, active search for "authenticity" to live out a pattern of Christian life that is truer to the New Testament than mainstream or state churches [56].
2.  **Ekklesia of Women**: Elisabeth Schüssler Fiorenza's oxymoron describing the democratic assembly of women as full citizens of the *polis*, reclaiming the ecclesial center to assert that "women are church and have always been church" [63, 82].
3.  **Exodus Community from Patriarchy**: Rosemary Radford Ruether's concept of the Church as a group on a journey away from patriarchal alienation, creating "liberated zones" and temporary base communities to nurture their own spirituality [65, 66].
4.  **Archetype vs. Prototype**: An archetype is a static, perfect historical model that must be copied literally (non-conformist view). A prototype is a dynamic, liberating sign or vision that must be creatively re-visioned and embodied anew in each historical context [67-69, 83].
5.  **Storytelling/Performing Community**: An incarnational, poststructuralist understanding of the Church where the narrative of Christ is physically performed and inscribed into the concrete, sexuate bodies of human beings [80, 84, 85].

**ISSUES ADDRESSED**:
1.  **The Exclusion of Women from representing Christ**: The traditional theological restriction of ordained ministry to males, which marginalizes women and denies their baptismal calling [86, 87].
2.  **The Danger of Token Presence**: The risk that campaigning for women's ordination in an unchanged patriarchal system leads to co-optation, clericalism, and divisions between ordained and lay women [88].
3.  **The Male-Centeredness of Canonical Reconstruction**: Traditional non-conformist groups using New Testament "household codes" as an archetype to build rigid, male-dominated elder systems [68, 89, 90].
4.  **Academic Liberal Bias**: Mary McClintock Fulkerson's critique that feminist theologians have historically ignored the actual "discourses of faith" of real, non-academic women (such as Pentecostal or housewife groups) who submit to male leadership [78, 79, 91].

**IMPORTANT QUOTES**:
*   "Dissent or 'religious dissent' can be understood to be not so much as disagreement for the sake of it... but also as the search for 'authenticity'..." (Page 464 / Section 1) - Context: Defining the core conceptual lens of the essay [56].
*   "Women have a calling in the churches today: a calling to represent that liberating sign of new humanity..." (Page 465 / Section 1) - Context: Quoting Rosemary Radford Ruether on the prophetic vocation of women [61, 92].
*   "...to speak of the church of women does not mean to advocate a separatist strategy but to underline the visibility of women..." (Page 467 / Section 1) - Context: Quoting Elisabeth Schüssler Fiorenza on reclaiming the ecclesial center [93].
*   "The church, according to Ruether, is an 'exodus community from patriarchy'." (Page 470 / Section 2) - Context: Conceptualizing the transition of feminist base communities [65].
*   "What is possible is the construction of ecclesiology, of new and creative ways of being church, which are in fact 'authentic'... authentic to those who claim to be church: women." (Page 475 / Section 3) - Context: Presenting the postmodern, relational shift in constructing ecclesiology [94].
*   "...gender, being male or female... is an important aspect of being human, being an actor in the performance of Christ’s story." (Page 482 / Section 4) - Context: Highlighting the bodily, sexuate necessity of incarnational ecclesiology [85].

**SUMMARY**:
Natalie Watson explores feminist theology as a unique form of "faithful dissent" that seeks the radical transformation of the Church from within rather than simple separation [53, 56, 95]. She contrasts the "women-church" movement with traditional non-conformist groups, demonstrating that while the latter rely on a literal, static "archetype" to construct new patriarchal hierarchies, feminist theologians utilize critical prototypes to recover the "discipleship of equals" [68, 69]. By integrating Letty Russell's *Church in the Round* and poststructuralist critiques, Watson redefines the Church as an open, storytelling community where Christ's narrative is physically performed by sexuate, bodily human beings, offering a multiplicity of creative ways of being Church that are truly "authentic for women" [70, 80, 85, 94, 96].
