# Unit 10 — Post-Conciliar Developments and Contemporary Debates

## The Documentary Sequence

| Document | Date | Content |
|---|---|---|
| ***Inter Insigniores*** (CDF) | 1976 | Declaration on the question of the admission of women to the ministerial priesthood; argues from constant tradition and from the priest's acting *in persona Christi* |
| ***Pastores Dabo Vobis*** | 1992 | John Paul II's post-synodal exhortation on priestly formation; the **four dimensions** — human, spiritual, intellectual, pastoral — now standard in every seminary programme |
| ***Ordinatio Sacerdotalis*** | 1994 | "The Church has **no authority whatsoever** to confer priestly ordination on women, and this judgement is to be **definitively held** by all the Church's faithful" |
| ***Omnium in Mentem*** | 2009 | Benedict XVI amends **cc. 1008-1009**: deacons are ordained to serve, and the phrase *in persona Christi Capitis* is reserved to **bishops and priests** — a canonical clarification with real theological weight for the diaconate debate |
| ***Ratio Fundamentalis*** (new) | 2016 | "The Gift of the Priestly Vocation"; introduces a propaedeutic stage and emphasises gradual, integrated formation |
| ***Querida Amazonia*** | 2020 | Francis's post-synodal exhortation which, against expectation, **did not** adopt the Amazon Synod's proposal for ordaining *viri probati* |
| ***Spiritus Domini*** | 2021 | Opens the instituted ministries of **lector and acolyte to women** (a lay ministry change, not an ordination change) |

## The Live Debates

**1. Women's ordination.** *Ordinatio Sacerdotalis* declares the matter closed for the priesthood, and the CDF's 1995 *responsum* stated the teaching belongs to the deposit of faith. The continuing questions are (a) the precise **note** of the teaching — definitive but not defined *ex cathedra* — and (b) the **diaconate**, which *Ordinatio Sacerdotalis* does not address. Francis established study commissions in **2016** and **2020**; neither produced a unanimous historical judgement, largely because the evidence for ancient women deacons (Phoebe; the Byzantine ordination rites for deaconesses, which closely parallel those for male deacons) is real but its interpretation contested — were they sacramentally ordained to the same order, or a parallel non-sacramental office?

**2. Celibacy and *viri probati*.** The Amazon Synod (2019) voted for ordaining married men of proven virtue in regions of acute priest shortage; *Querida Amazonia* declined. The debate is genuinely open because, as Unit 4 establishes, celibacy is **discipline, not doctrine** — so the question is prudential rather than dogmatic, which is precisely why it is so contested.

**3. Clericalism.** Francis has made this the central critique, naming it in the 2018 *Letter to the People of God* as a root of the abuse crisis: clericalism "nullifies the baptismal grace" of the laity and generates "a rupture within the Church's body." The theological question is whether clericalism is a *distortion* of the ministerial priesthood or is somehow fostered by the *essentia et non gradu tantum* framing itself — a question sharpened by every abuse inquiry.

**4. The diaconate's identity.** Is the permanent deacon primarily a liturgical minister, a minister of charity (the *diakonia* of Acts 6), or a bridge to the secular world? *Omnium in Mentem*'s clarification sharpens the question by explicitly distinguishing the deacon's configuration to Christ from the priest's.

**5. The theology of the presbyterate.** Vatican II's shift to a relational, servant-leader model left an unresolved tension: is the priest defined primarily by **cultic capacity** (confecting the Eucharist, absolving) or by **mission and proclamation**? *Presbyterorum Ordinis* 4 gives primacy to preaching; centuries of practice give it to the altar.
