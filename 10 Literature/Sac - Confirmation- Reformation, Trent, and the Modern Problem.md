# Unit 9 — Confirmation: Reformation, Trent, and the Modern Problem

## The Reformers' Rejection

All the magisterial Reformers **denied Confirmation is a sacrament**, on the grounds that it lacks a dominical institution and a promise attached to a sign.

- **Luther** dismissed it caustically in *The Babylonian Captivity* — the bishops "have invented" it; he called such rites *Affenspiel*, "monkey business." But he approved retaining a **catechetical rite**: instruct the young, examine them, lay hands on them, pray. The content survives; the sacramental claim does not.
- **Calvin (*Institutes* IV.19)** is more interesting: he *praises* the ancient practice of catechesis followed by laying on of hands as a "laudable" custom and actively wishes it restored — while attacking the medieval version as an insult to baptism (as though baptism were incomplete) and denouncing the anointing as human invention.
- **Anglicanism** retained Confirmation in the *Book of Common Prayer* as the rite by which the baptised **ratify their baptismal vows in person** and are admitted to Holy Communion — the "confirmation-as-mature-commitment" model that has since influenced Catholic parish practice more than most Catholics realise.

## Trent, Session VII — the Three Canons on Confirmation

1. Anathema on holding Confirmation is "an idle ceremony and not a true and proper sacrament."
2. Anathema on holding that those who ascribe power to the sacred chrism "offer insult to the Holy Spirit."
3. Anathema on holding that the **ordinary minister is not the bishop alone** but any simple priest.

## The Great Modern Anomaly: Quam Singulari (1910)

**This is the single most important thing to understand about Confirmation today.**

**Pius X, *Quam Singulari* (8 Aug 1910)** lowered the age of **First Communion** to the age of discretion (about 7) — a magnificent pastoral reform, aimed at ending the practice of withholding the Eucharist from children until 12 or 14.

**But it had an unintended structural consequence.** Confirmation, tied to the bishop's availability, stayed late. So the ancient order was **inverted**:

| Patristic and Eastern order | Modern Western order (post-1910) |
|---|---|
| Baptism, **Confirmation**, Eucharist | Baptism, **Eucharist**, Confirmation |

The Eucharist — which the whole tradition calls the ***consummation* of initiation** (CCC 1322) — now comes *before* the sacrament that was supposed to complete initiation. The result is the pastoral confusion every catechist knows: Confirmation gets re-interpreted as a "**sacrament of Christian maturity**" or a personal ratification of baptismal promises — essentially the Anglican model — and is sometimes even deployed as a retention device to keep adolescents in catechesis.

**Note the tension carefully, because it is the sharpest examinable issue in this whole course:** **CCC 1308** explicitly warns — *"Although Confirmation is sometimes called the 'sacrament of Christian maturity,' we must not confuse adult faith with the adult age of natural growth."* And it points out that Aquinas himself said the age of the body does not determine the age of the soul.

**The "Restored Order" movement.** A growing number of dioceses have returned Confirmation to *before* First Communion, restoring the ancient sequence. **CIC c. 891** permits this: confirmation is to be conferred "at about the age of discretion" **unless the episcopal conference determines another age**.

## The Reformed Rite

**Paul VI, Apostolic Constitution *Divinae Consortium Naturae* (15 Aug 1971)** — the definitive modern document. It locates Confirmation firmly within **Christian initiation**, not as a free-standing coming-of-age rite, and defines the **essential rite**: *anointing with chrism on the forehead, done by the laying on of the hand*, with the words:

> **"*Accipe signaculum Doni Spiritus Sancti*" — "Be sealed with the Gift of the Holy Spirit."**

This form was **deliberately borrowed from the Byzantine rite**, replacing the medieval Latin formula — a genuine act of ecumenical *ressourcement*, and a fine example for essay use of Vatican II's method: reaching *backward* past the medieval West to a more ancient and more shared tradition.

| Document | Reference |
|---|---|
| *Divinae Consortium Naturae* | 15 Aug 1971 |
| *Ordo Confirmationis* (Rite of Confirmation) | 22 Aug 1971 |
| *CCC* | **1285-1321** |
| *CIC* | **cc. 879-896** (c. 882 minister; c. 883 those confirming by law; c. 891 age; c. 893 sponsor) |
| Vatican II | **SC 71**; **LG 11**; **AG 11, 36** |

**LG 11** is the key conciliar sentence: by Confirmation the faithful "are **more perfectly bound to the Church** and are **enriched with a special strength** of the Holy Spirit. Hence they are, as true witnesses of Christ, **more strictly obliged to spread and defend the faith** by word and deed."
