# Unit 8 — Trent and the Catholic Reformation

Trent treated the Eucharist across **three separate sessions**, and knowing which is which is a standard exam discriminator.

| Session | Date | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Session XIII** | 11 Oct 1551 | **Decree on the Most Holy Eucharist** — real presence, transubstantiation, worship and reservation, communion |
| **Session XXI** | 16 July 1562 | **Communion under both kinds and communion of children** |
| **Session XXII** | 17 Sept 1562 | **Doctrine and canons on the Sacrifice of the Mass** |

## Session XIII — Presence

Defines that "after the consecration of bread and wine, our Lord Jesus Christ, true God and true man, is **truly, really, and substantially** (*vere, realiter, substantialiter*) contained under the appearance of those sensible things." It affirms the **conversion of the whole substance**, states that the Church "most fittingly calls this change transubstantiation," and defends **concomitance**, **reservation** of the sacrament, **adoration** (*latria*) of Christ present, and the requirement of sacramental confession before communion for those in grave sin.

**Note the precision of the phrasing**: Trent teaches transubstantiation as the *most fitting* term for a change it defines in terms of substance. This wording leaves genuine room, exploited by later theologians, between the dogma of the conversion and any particular Aristotelian physics.

## Session XXII — Sacrifice

The core sentence: in the Mass "is contained and immolated in an unbloody manner the same Christ who once offered himself in a bloody manner on the altar of the cross... **the victim is one and the same, the same now offering by the ministry of priests who then offered himself on the cross, the manner of offering alone being different**."

It affirms the Mass as **propitiatory** for the living and the dead, defends Masses in honour of the saints, private Masses, the Latin language, and the Roman Canon — while also, in its own decree on abuses, ordering bishops to suppress avarice, irreverence, and superstition surrounding stipends.

## The Missal of Pius V (1570)

The bull ***Quo Primum*** promulgated a standardised Roman Missal for the whole Latin Church (excepting rites of over two hundred years' standing, such as the Ambrosian and Dominican). This produced four centuries of near-total liturgical uniformity in the West — the very uniformity *Sacrosanctum Concilium* would relativise.

## Post-Tridentine Eucharistic Devotion

The period's characteristic developments all flow from Session XIII's affirmation of the presence outside the celebration: **Forty Hours' Devotion**, **Exposition and Benediction**, the placing of the **tabernacle on the high altar**, Eucharistic processions and congresses, and later the Sacred Heart devotion. Whatever one's judgement about the balance struck, this is a coherent working-out of *reservation plus adoration*.
