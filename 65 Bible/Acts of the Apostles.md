# Acts of the Apostles

Luke's second volume, addressed like the first to Theophilus, narrating what Jesus "began to do and teach" (1:1) as continuing through the Spirit-filled Church over roughly thirty years.

**Author.** Luke, physician and sometime companion of Paul; the "we"-passages mark where he travels with him.
**Date.** Likely before or in the early AD 60s — the book mentions neither Nero's persecution (AD 65), nor Paul's martyrdom, nor the fall of the Temple (AD 70).
**Audience.** Gentile Christians and enquirers; Hebrew-Aramaic presuppositions are explained or omitted.
**Structure.** Acts 1:8 — Jerusalem (1:1–8:3), Judea and Samaria (8:4–12:25), the ends of the earth (13:1–28:31); Peter dominates 1–12, Paul 13–28.
**Alternate title.** "The Acts of the Holy Spirit" — the Spirit, not the apostles, is the continuous agent.
