# An Illustration Is the Argument, Not Decoration on It

From building a retreat series in the idiom of Sr Inigo SSA's *The Ever-Evolving Consecrated Life*
(16 August 2026): the governing rule extracted from her corpus was **never name a category when you
can name a person, a place, a price, or an object.** Not "the poor" but the Murshidabad bonded
labourer; not "we live comfortably" but "a beautiful convent with marble floors and a generator that
never fails."

The reason this works homiletically, not just rhetorically: an illustration is the place where a claim
about God becomes a thing a person can see — and therefore a thing a person can refuse or obey. A
homily that stays in categories can be agreed with and changed nothing. A homily anchored in one named
object cannot be nodded past.

Twenty reusable illustration types (diagnostic, narrative, conceptual, evidential), each with a
generative recipe and a named failure mode, are catalogued in the `illustration-craft` skill — built
the same day from this corpus, alongside two full retreat series (21 talks on consecrated life; 18
talks on synodality for Carmelite sisters, grounded in Soares-Prabhu). Documents held outside this
vault, in *Talks and Notes* (Retreat folder).
