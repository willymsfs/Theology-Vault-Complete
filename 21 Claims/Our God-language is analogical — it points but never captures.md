# Our God-language is analogical — it points but never captures

When we call God "good" or "father," the word is neither univocal (same as human goodness) nor equivocal (unrelated) but **analogical** — truly pointing to God while always falling short. Hence the permanent theological duty to "continually purify our language" so we never confuse our human pictures with the incomprehensible God.

**Evidence:** "Our human words always fall short of the mystery of God" (ch. 1).
**Consequence:** every doctrinal formula is provisional in expression though not in truth — connects to Polarity 1, [[Theology must hold mystery and intelligibility together]].
