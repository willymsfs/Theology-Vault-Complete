# Buridan's argument that separated accidents are genuine beings forces being to become a univocal term across substance and accident

[[John Buridan]] observes that the separated Eucharistic accidents can be seen, touched and weighed — which means they are clearly beings in their own right, not merely properties-of-something. But Aristotle's system had assumed "being" primarily meant "being *something*," i.e. a substance or a property inhering in one. If accidents can be beings entirely on their own, "being" must apply the same basic way to a horse and to a free-standing accident. This dismantles the absolute status of Aristotle's category system and is one of the pressures historians credit with helping open the door from medieval to modern philosophy.
