# Luther's sacramental union is metaphysically cheaper than transubstantiation because it removes the need to explain accidents without a subject

[[Sacramental Union]] holds that the substance of bread and wine remains unchanged, with Christ's body and blood present "in, with, and under" it — Luther's own analogy is a red-hot iron in a forge, where iron and fire are fully united without either ceasing to be itself. Because nothing is converted, the whole [[Accidens Sine Subjecto|accidents-without-a-subject]] puzzle simply does not arise. The cost: Luther has no account of why the words of institution ("this is my body") should require any metaphysical change at all, if the bread's substance never left.
