# Confirmation became a separate sacrament in the West by administrative accident rather than theological decision

The East preserved the unity of initiation by letting the presbyter chrismate with bishop-consecrated myron, while the West insisted on the bishop's physical presence; as dioceses outgrew episcopal visitation the second anointing was simply delayed. The theology of confirmation as a distinct sacrament conferring robur was then constructed retrospectively, chiefly by Faustus of Riez, to explain a practice that already existed.
