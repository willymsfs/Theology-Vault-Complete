# Doctrine is a rule of communal grammar, not a proposition or a feeling

Lindbeck's thesis in *The Nature of Doctrine*. Against the **cognitive-propositional** model (doctrine as objective truth-claim) and the **experiential-expressive** model (doctrine as expression of prior inner experience), the **cultural-linguistic** model treats doctrines as *communally authoritative rules of discourse, attitude and action*.

Its explanatory payoff: it is the only one of the three that accounts for **doctrinal constancy and historical change at once** — the rule holds while its formulations shift. Its ecumenical payoff: churches can agree on the rule while differing in formulation.

Its cost: theology becomes intratextual — "the text absorbs the world" — and it becomes hard to say how the Church is corrected by anything outside itself, or what "true" means beyond modest cognitivism.
