# Demel's diagnosis that consultative structures make lay co-responsibility a clerical concession
rather than a baptismal right names the trap synodal reform keeps falling into

Any reform that adds consultation without changing canon 129's restriction of governance to sacred
orders leaves lay participation as something granted, and therefore something that can be withdrawn.
Ambrose's proposed correction — forming laity as co-believers rather than collaborators — targets the
standing, not merely the function: a collaborator assists someone else's mission; a co-believer shares
ownership of it by baptismal right.
