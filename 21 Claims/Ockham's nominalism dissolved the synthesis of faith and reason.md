# Ockham's nominalism dissolved the synthesis of faith and reason

By rejecting universals (only individuals are real) and wielding Ockham's Razor, William of Ockham argued theology is "not a science, because its principles are not evident but accepted from revelation." This broke the neat Scholastic marriage of faith and reason that Aquinas had built — separating them rather than harmonizing them.

**Contrast:** [[Aquinas]] (sacred science, faith+reason) vs Ockham (faith and reason apart).
