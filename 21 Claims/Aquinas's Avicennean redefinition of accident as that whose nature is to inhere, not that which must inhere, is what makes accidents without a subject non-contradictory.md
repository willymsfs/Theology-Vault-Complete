# Aquinas's Avicennean redefinition of accident as that whose nature is to inhere, not that which must inhere, is what makes accidents without a subject non-contradictory

If an accident is defined as "that which actually exists in a subject," a free-floating accident is a flat contradiction. Aquinas, following [[Avicenna]], redefines it as "that to whose nature it is due or fitting to exist in a subject" — a thing can retain that nature while God, as the ultimate cause behind even the ordinary case, directly sustains it in existence without the usual intermediary. The [[Eucharist MOC|helicopter analogy]]: a roof naturally due to fall does not stop being "a heavy thing" when a helicopter holds it up; it retains the nature while something more powerful suspends the usual outcome.
