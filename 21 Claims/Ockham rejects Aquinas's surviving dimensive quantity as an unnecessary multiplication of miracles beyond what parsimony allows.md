# Ockham rejects Aquinas's surviving dimensive quantity as an unnecessary multiplication of miracles beyond what parsimony allows

Aquinas holds that the bread's dimensive quantity (its size and shape) survives the consecration and acts as a kind of sub-subject, giving the other accidents (colour, taste) something to attach to. [[Ockham]], applying the razor that bears his name, denies quantity is a detachable thing at all — there is only ever a round wafer, not a free-standing "roundness" that could be reused as scaffolding. His account is more austere than Aquinas's: no explanatory bridge, only the admission that God is holding the accidents up by brute, unexplained power.
