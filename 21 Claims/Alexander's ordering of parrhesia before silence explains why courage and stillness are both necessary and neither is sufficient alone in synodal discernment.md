# Alexander's ordering of parrhesia before silence explains why courage and stillness are both
necessary and neither is sufficient alone in synodal discernment

Silence without prior parrhesia is merely absence of conflict — nothing true was risked, so there is
nothing to sit with. Parrhesia without subsequent silence collapses into debate, where the loudest or
most confident voice wins by attrition rather than by the group actually listening. The sequence is the
argument: courageous speech first, to put something real on the table; silence after, to let it be
received rather than rebutted.
