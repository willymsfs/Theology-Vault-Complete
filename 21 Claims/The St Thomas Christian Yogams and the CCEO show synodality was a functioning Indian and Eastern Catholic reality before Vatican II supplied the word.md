# The St Thomas Christian Yogams and the CCEO show synodality was a functioning Indian and Eastern
Catholic reality before Vatican II supplied the word

Before the Synod of Diamper (1599) imposed Latin forms, the St Thomas Christians were governed by a
genuinely deliberative three-tier lay-clerical assembly (the Yogams), with a native lay Archdeacon
holding real executive power. The CCEO preserves a parallel reality today: Patriarchal and Major
Archiepiscopal Synods, including the Syro-Malabar and Syro-Malankara, hold actual legislative and
judicial power the Latin Synod of Bishops does not. Both answer the charge that synodality is a Western
theological import — India possessed, and in the Eastern Catholic case still possesses, a functioning
synodal polity independent of the word's recent currency.
