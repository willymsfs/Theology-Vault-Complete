# Modern theology is defined by its relation to modernity, not by its conclusions

Ford's minimal definition: theology is modern when it **does not simply repeat past theology but takes account of the changed modern context in content and method** — whether in order to adapt to, criticize, resist, or try to reverse it.

The corollary that surprises students: a fiercely anti-modern theology is still *modern theology*, because its anti-modernity is a considered response to the modern situation. What falls outside is only theology that has not noticed anything happened.

This is what makes the **five types** a spectrum of relationships rather than a scale of orthodoxy, and why most serious theologians move across types depending on the topic.
