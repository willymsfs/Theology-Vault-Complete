# The collapse of canonical penance was caused not by doctrinal objection but by pastoral unviability, and the Irish monks solved a problem the hierarchy had not solved

Because canonical penance was once-only, public, severe, and carried lifelong disabilities, deferral to the deathbed became near-universal and the system fell into disuse by the sixth century; the repeatable private form that replaced it was actually condemned at Toledo III (589) before being accepted at Chalon (813) — meaning the Western Church's normative practice today descends from an innovation the magisterium initially rejected.
