# Scotus's objection exposes that dependent existence cannot spontaneously become independent existence without redefining what an accident is

In Aquinas's account an accident does not have its own *esse*; it merely shares in the substance's existence, as a sticker has no standing power of its own but is glued to a box. [[Duns Scotus]] asks: if the box (substance) is removed, where does the sticker suddenly acquire the power to float on its own? A dependent property cannot become independent without ceasing to be the kind of thing a dependent property is. Scotus's repair — treating accidents as quasi-independent — begins the process of blurring Aristotle's substance/accident line that later thinkers push further.
