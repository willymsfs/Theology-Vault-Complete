# Imposing the oath was the point at which the French Revolution took a wrong turn

The class notes' own judgement: the oath of allegiance to the Civil Constitution of the Clergy (November 1790 – January 1791) **marked the end of national unity and the beginning of civil war**.

**The mechanism**: the clergy split roughly evenly into **jurors and non-jurors**; only 7 bishops in all France swore. The split ran through every parish, made loyalty legible and therefore punishable, and produced the Vendée, the deportations and the September massacres. Before the oath the Revolution had a Catholic constituency — the *curés* had helped make the National Assembly.

**Marked medium confidence** because it is a counterfactual: it assumes a Revolution that reorganized the Church without demanding a loyalty test could have kept that constituency. Two facts support it — the Assembly **three times refused** to make Catholicism the national religion, and the Declaration of the Rights of Man allowed toleration. One fact tells against it: the Assembly had already seized Church property in November 1789, so the conflict may have been unavoidable in substance whatever the procedure.
