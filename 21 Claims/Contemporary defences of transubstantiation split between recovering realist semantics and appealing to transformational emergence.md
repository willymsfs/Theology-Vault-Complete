# Contemporary defences of transubstantiation split between recovering realist semantics and appealing to transformational emergence

Two live twenty-first-century strategies, both conceding the real difficulty was never physics but the theory of reference and the nature of composition. Ross McCullough's [[Emergence|transformational emergence]] account compares the consecration to chemical bonding: matter is reorganised under Christ's presence and gains genuinely new properties, as hydrogen and oxygen gain wetness on bonding into water. Joshua Hochschild's [[Eucharistic Semantics - Realist Versus Nominalist|realist semantics]] argues modern discomfort with the doctrine is really discomfort with modern, nominalist habits of language — recover a realist sense of what a noun like "bread" names, and transubstantiation stops sounding like a magic trick.
