# The loss of the temporal power increased papal moral authority

Every argument for the [[Temporal Power]] — Cappellari's *Triumph of the Holy See* (1799), [[Joseph de Maistre]]'s *Du Pape* (1819) — held that a pope who is some prince's subject cannot teach freely. The nineteenth century tested the claim by removing the territory, and the prediction failed.

**The evidence**: the [[Syllabus of Errors]], written from political weakness in 1864 and read at the time as the last cry of an expiring papacy, **became a symbol of papal moral authority after 1870**. Once Rome fell and the pope became "the prisoner of the Vatican," papal influence did not collapse; it globalized. [[Rerum Novarum]] (1891) gave the papacy a voice in the industrial world it had never had as a landholder, and every pope since has had to speak on the social question.

**The qualification**: the papacy did not choose this. It lost the argument on the ground and discovered afterwards that the loss was a liberation — which is not the same as a theology of powerlessness.
