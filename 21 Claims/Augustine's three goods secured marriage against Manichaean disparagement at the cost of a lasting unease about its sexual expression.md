# Augustine's three goods secured marriage against Manichaean disparagement at the cost of a lasting unease about its sexual expression

De bono coniugali establishes proles, fides, and sacramentum as genuine goods against those who held procreation evil, but De nuptiis et concupiscentia's account of original sin transmitted through generation left the tradition affirming marriage while treating its consummation with suspicion — the ambivalence the personalist turn set out to correct.
