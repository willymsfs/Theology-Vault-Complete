# The Church cannot be a department of state

The Civil Constitution of the Clergy (1790) is the experiment: a national legislature reorganized dioceses, made bishops elected officials, salaried the clergy, and required an oath of loyalty. The reforms **made the Church a department of state and cut it off from the universal Church** — and produced not a reformed national church but a schism, a civil war and, within three years, de-Christianization.

**The structural reason**: a church whose orders, appointments and boundaries are settled by one nation's legislature loses precisely the catholicity that makes it a church rather than a ministry of religion. This is [[Gallicanism]] pushed past its own limit.

**The counter-case worth weighing**: Austria under Joseph II restructured the Church at least as thoroughly and did *not* schism — because **Joseph never asked papal approval**, so the question of communion was never forced. The French bishops did ask, on 30 October 1790. The claim may therefore be narrower than it looks: **not every state restructuring breaks communion, but every state restructuring that demands the Church's own endorsement of it does.**
