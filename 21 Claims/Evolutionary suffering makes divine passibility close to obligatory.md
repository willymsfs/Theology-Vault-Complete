# Evolutionary suffering makes divine passibility close to obligatory

Predation, extinction and pain are not accidents inside evolution but the mechanism of it. A God who creates by that process and remains classically impassible is either indifferent to it or external to it. Hence Peacocke's and Moltmann's turn to a **"pathetic, self-emptying God"** who shares intimately in the suffering of every creature — the same conclusion the Holocaust forced on European theology and that Kitamori and Sobrino reached from Japanese and Salvadoran suffering.

**Where the claim is contestable:** if God suffers by nature, is God still the ground of hope rather than the largest fellow-sufferer? Process theology accepts that price; Jüngel and Moltmann try to derive passibility from God's *freedom* to identify with the crucified Jesus rather than from metaphysical necessity. Marked medium confidence because the derivation, not the conclusion, is disputed.
