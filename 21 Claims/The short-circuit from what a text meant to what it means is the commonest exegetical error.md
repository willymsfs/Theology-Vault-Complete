# The short-circuit from what a text meant to what it means is the commonest exegetical error

Jumping from the ancient sense of a text straight to its contemporary application skips interpretation, history, dialectics and doctrine. The results are anachronistic application and a false quarrel in which history is set against faith. The remedy is the ladder: research and interpretation (what it meant), history and dialectics (how it was received, whose horizons clash), doctrines and systematics (what is affirmed and how it coheres), only then communications (what it means now).
