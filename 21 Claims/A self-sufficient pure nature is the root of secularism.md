# A self-sufficient pure nature is the root of secularism

De Lubac's thesis in *Surnaturel*. Neoscholasticism defended grace's gratuity by positing ***natura pura*** — a human nature complete in itself, with its own proportionate end, to which grace is then added. The unintended result: a world that by construction does not need God. Secularism did not have to be argued for; the manuals had already conceded the ground.

His counter-claim: human nature carries a **natural desire for the supernatural**, and deification by grace is its true destiny — gratuitous precisely because it exceeds a nature that nonetheless cannot rest without it.

Consequences: Rahner's *supernatural existential* is the same move in transcendental key; Stolz traces the divorce of theology from spirituality to the same extrinsicism; Milbank makes the recovery of this thesis the condition for Catholic theology escaping both secular liberalism and closed conservatism.
