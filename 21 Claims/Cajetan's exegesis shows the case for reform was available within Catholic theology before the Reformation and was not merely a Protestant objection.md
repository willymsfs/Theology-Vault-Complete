# Cajetan's exegesis shows the case for reform was available within Catholic theology before the Reformation and was not merely a Protestant objection

Thomas de Vio, a cardinal and Luther's own opponent at Augsburg, argued on purely exegetical grounds that James 5 concerns anointing for recovery rather than extreme unction, and was not condemned for it — which means the twentieth-century recovery restored a reading that had always been legitimate rather than importing a Protestant one.
