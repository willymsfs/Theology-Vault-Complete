# The Anglican three-house system shows a deliberative lay vote is compatible with apostolic
ministry, which is the exact possibility Catholic canon law forecloses

The standard Catholic defence of the consultative-only vote is that a deliberative lay vote would
compromise apostolic ministry. The Anglican Communion's requirement of concurrent deliberative approval
from Bishops, Clergy and Laity for major decisions is a live counter-example within a tradition that
also claims apostolic succession — which means the objection is about institutional choice, not about
an unavoidable theological necessity.
