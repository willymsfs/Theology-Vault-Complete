## B. Short answer — three to five sentences

1. Why does Ford count a fiercely anti-modern theology as *modern* theology?
2. State the five types and place Barth, Tillich, Bultmann. Why are Types 1 and 5 limit cases?
3. What made Barth break with liberal theology, and what did he put in its place?
4. Explain "religionless Christianity" and the world come of age. Why is Bonhoeffer easy to misuse?
5. Run Tillich's correlation on finitude and on estrangement.
6. Why did de Lubac think *natura pura* produced secularism?
7. Define the supernatural existential and give one criticism of the anonymous Christian from each side.
8. Contrast Rahner and Balthasar on the starting point of theology and name two consequences.
9. What does Pannenberg gain, and risk, by making theology answerable to universal history?
10. How does Moltmann's eschatology differ from a doctrine of the last things?
11. Explain the *homoousion* as the hinge of Torrance's theological science.
12. What did Sykes accuse Ramsey of, and was he right?
13. Distinguish the two Niebuhrs in one paragraph.
14. State Lindbeck's three models and the advantage of the third.
15. Explain Frei's "eclipse of biblical narrative" and its consequence for preaching.
16. What is postliberalism accused of, and what is its answer?
17. Name three post-conciliar Catholic positions and how they differ.
18. Distinguish liberal from conservative postmodern theology; place Marion and Cupitt.
19. Name four positions on divine action and say which concede least to science.
20. Why does evolutionary suffering press toward divine passibility, and what does that cost?
21. State Milbank's charge against sociology and one objection to it.
22. How did theology and spirituality come apart, and on what terms can they be reunited?
23. Distinguish the three styles of practical theology with one figure each.
24. What is kyriarchy, and why did womanist theologians need it?
25. Give the four sources of black theology and one internal critique of the movement.
26. Trace liberation theology from the irruption of the poor to its present crisis.
27. State the Dalit critique of inculturation in your own words.
28. Explain cross-textual hermeneutics and give an Indian worked example.
29. Give postcolonialism's three charges against liberation hermeneutics.
30. What did BEM achieve, and what remains unresolved in ecumenical method?
31. Why is the essence/energies distinction necessary for Orthodox theology?
32. Why is Pentecostalism growing where liberation theology receded?
33. State D'Costa's objection to the threefold typology and his alternative.
34. What is Scriptural Reasoning, and what does it refuse to settle in advance?
35. Recite six of Ford's twelve theses and say what each answers in the book.

## C. Essay questions — twenty minutes each

1. "Modern theology is defined not by its conclusions but by its relation to modernity." Discuss with reference to Ford's five types and at least three theologians.
2. Compare Rahner and Balthasar on the starting point of theology, and assess the consequences for the theology of religions.
3. "Barth restored theology to strength but at the cost of history." Evaluate.
4. Assess Lindbeck's cultural-linguistic theory. Is it a resource or a threat for Indian contextual theology?
5. Account for the movement of the Latin American poor from base communities to Pentecostalism, and say what liberation theology should learn from it.
6. "Every liberating theology is criticized by the next one it excluded." Illustrate from womanist, Third World feminist and postcolonial critiques.
7. What does the theology–science dialogue require of the doctrine of divine action? Assess panentheism as a solution.
8. Examine the divorce and attempted reunion of theology and spirituality, and state the implications for seminary formation.
9. "African theology is danced, sung and lived rather than written." What does Maluleke's claim imply for how theology is assessed in the academy — and for Ford's inclusion criteria?
10. Take any three of Ford's twelve theses and argue a theological programme for the Indian Church in the next decade.
11. Is de Lubac right that a self-sufficient "pure nature" is the root of secularism?
12. "Postcolonial criticism makes the Bible unusable for the oppressed." Evaluate, with reference to Exodus and to the adivasi question.
13. Compare Kitamori, Sobrino and Ahn Byung Mu as theologies of suffering. Which is most usable in a Bengal parish, and why?
14. Assess Moltmann's social trinitarianism against the charge of tritheism.
15. "Theology is a broker of the arts, humanities, sciences and common sense." Test Ford's ninth thesis against any one chapter of Parts III, IV or VIII.

## D. Viva — the twelve fault lines

Take each row of [[11a - Fault Lines Since 1918]]: name the two sides with a text apiece, state what is at stake, then take a position and defend it against the strongest objection.

1. Where theology starts — Rahner or Balthasar
2. Nature and grace — *natura pura* or the natural desire for the supernatural
3. Retrieval or correlation — Lindbeck or Tracy
4. Theology and secular reason — Milbank or Troeltsch/Farley
5. God and suffering — impassibility or passibility
6. Faith and history — Pannenberg or Barth
7. Christ and the religions — inclusivism, pluralism, or trinitarian engagement
8. Culture or justice — inculturation or liberation
9. Liberation and its critics — single axis or intersecting axes
10. Church and world — Hauerwas/Yoder or Reinhold Niebuhr
11. After foundationalism — recovery or retreat
12. Theology and prayer — discipline or participation
