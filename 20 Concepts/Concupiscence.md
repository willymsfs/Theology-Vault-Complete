# Concupiscence

The disordered inclination remaining after baptism; Trent (Session V, 1546) teaches that baptism removes the guilt (reatus) of original sin, while concupiscence remains — "of sin and inclining to sin" but not itself sin.

### Matrimony course (BTh)
Augustine's account of concupiscence bears directly on marriage: in De nuptiis et concupiscentia he holds that although marriage is genuinely good, intercourse is now inevitably accompanied by concupiscence and original sin is transmitted through generation. The result was a Western tradition affirming marriage's goodness while treating its sexual expression with persistent unease — and the scholastic listing of the remedium concupiscentiae among the secondary ends of marriage. The twentieth-century personalist turn set out to correct precisely this legacy. See [[Remedium Concupiscentiae]] and [[Matrimony MOC]].
