# The Council of Jerusalem as Synodal Archetype

Acts 15:1-35 is read by the tradition as the archetype of every synod. Facing a genuine theological
crisis (must Gentile converts become Jews first?), the early Church resolves it not by apostolic decree
and not by majority vote, through five features: speaking openly from personal experience (Peter,
Barnabas, Paul); silent, respectful listening after the speaking (15:12); communal prayer seeking the
Spirit's promptings; resolution by consensus rather than counting; and acknowledgement of the Spirit's
sovereignty in the decree itself — "It has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us" (15:28). This
sequence is the direct template for [[Conversation in the Spirit]], and — independently — the method
several Indian tribal governance systems arrived at without access to the text; see
[[Indigenous Indian Models of Synodality]].
