# Conversation in the Spirit

The Synod's working method, modelled on the Council of Jerusalem (Acts 15) and resting on a robust
pneumatological ecclesiology: the Spirit is the primary protagonist of the Church's synodal journey,
not the assembly or the chair. Three postures, in order: **parrhesia** (Acts 4:31) — courageous,
transparent speech, ensuring no one is silenced; **silent, respectful listening** (Acts 15:12) — "the
entire assembly kept silence," which is a theological necessity, not an absence of debate, since it
prevents conversation degenerating into argument; **communal discernment over majoritarianism** — "It
has seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us" (Acts 15:28), authority exercised as confirmation of the
Spirit's work, not as unilateral decree.

## Claims
- [[Conversation in the Spirit is guarded against groupthink by Doss's point that the Spirit sources plurality, not only unity]]
- [[Alexander's ordering of parrhesia before silence explains why courage and stillness are both necessary and neither is sufficient alone in synodal discernment]]
- [[Culas locates the safeguard of synodal method in the silence after speech, not in the speech itself]]
