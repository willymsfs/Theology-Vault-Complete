# Divine Passibility

The claim that **God can suffer** — against the classical axiom of divine impassibility inherited from Greek metaphysics.

The twentieth century's most widely accepted doctrinal revision, driven by three pressures: the **camps** (a God who cannot suffer cannot be with the victims), **evolutionary suffering** (Peacocke, Moltmann on the "pathetic, self-emptying God"), and **contextual theologies of the oppressed** (Kitamori's pain of God, Sobrino's crucified people, minjung *han*).

**Standing objection:** if God suffers by nature, is God still free and still the ground of hope, or only the largest fellow-sufferer? Process theology accepts that price; Moltmann and Jüngel try to derive passibility from God's *freedom* to identify with the crucified Jesus rather than from a metaphysical necessity.
