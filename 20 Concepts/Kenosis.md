# Kenosis

Greek *kenōsis*, **self-emptying** (Phil 2:7). Christ laying aside the exercise of divine attributes to become fully human — and, by extension, the pattern of God's whole way of acting.

Twentieth-century theology widened the term far beyond Christology. **Bulgakov** made kenosis normative for *all* language about God in creation and redemption. **Simone Weil** read creation itself as divine renunciation. **MacKinnon** used it against establishment religion. **Koyama** turned it into a missionary posture — the crucified, not the crusading, mind. Ecological and science-facing theologies use it for a God who acts by self-limitation rather than by overriding natural process.
