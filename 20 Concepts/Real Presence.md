# Real Presence

The doctrine that Christ is truly, really and substantially present in the Eucharist under the species
of bread and wine — not symbolically, not merely spiritually, but the historical and glorified Christ
himself. [[Transubstantiation]] is the mechanism proposed to explain how this is possible without
contradiction; the two doctrines are distinct but inseparable in Catholic teaching (Trent, Session
XIII). Denied or qualified variously by [[Sacramental Union|Luther]], [[Huldrych Zwingli|Zwingli's]]
memorialism, and Calvin's dynamic presence — see [[Reformation Positions on the Real Presence]].
