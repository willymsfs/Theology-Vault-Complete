# Gallicanism

The French tradition of **national ecclesiastical liberties** against Roman centralization: the king and the French Church hold rights Rome cannot override. Classically formulated in the **Gallican Articles of 1682** — the benchmark the class notes use when they say Napoleon gave the Church "prestige it had not enjoyed since 1682."

**Its opposite** is [[Ultramontanism]]. The nineteenth century is the story of Gallicanism's defeat: [[Felicite de Lamennais]] attacked it, [[Joseph de Maistre]] wrote against it, and Vatican I (1870) closed it.

**Its afterlife**: the Civil Constitution of the Clergy (1790) was Gallicanism pushed past its own limit — a national church so national it ceased to be in communion.
