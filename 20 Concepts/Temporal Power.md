# Temporal Power

The pope's **sovereignty over territory** — the [[Papal States]] — as distinct from his spiritual authority.

**The case for it** (Cappellari, later Gregory XVI, in *The Triumph of the Holy See against the Assaults of the Innovators*, 1799; [[Joseph de Maistre]]): a pope who is any prince's subject cannot teach freely. Temporal power is the guarantee of spiritual independence. Hence too the axiom that **the absolutism of Catholicism had to be reflected in the pope's own kingdom** — which is why [[Ercole Consalvi]] could reform the administration of the Papal States but never grant a constitution.

**What actually happened**: the territory was lost in 1870, and papal **moral authority grew rather than collapsed** — the *Syllabus*, written from weakness, became a symbol of authority after 1870, and *Rerum Novarum* (1891) gave the papacy a global voice it had never had as a landholder.
