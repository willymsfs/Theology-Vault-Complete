# Christian Democracy

Catholic principles carried into democratic politics — and, in its first French form, a movement Rome had to fence in.

**Origin**: [[Leon Harmel]]'s Christian Worker Congress (1894–1900), renamed the **Congress of Christian Democracy** in 1896; a party embracing the French Republic and democracy coupled with Catholic principles; a trade union from 1899 with 600,000 members.

**The drift**: growing **anti-Masonic, anti-Protestant and anti-Semitic** tendencies, backed by *La Croix*, the largest-circulation French Catholic paper; the Christian Democrats **aligned with the extreme right**.

**The fence — *Graves de communi re* (1901)**. [[Leo XIII]]: all work for the common good **under the aegis of the Church**; socialism is not the answer. Christian Democracy must not (1) promote democracy over other forms of government, (2) belittle religion, (3) attack authority, (4) promote socialism, (5) neglect the upper classes.

**The other casualty**: **[[Le Sillon]]**, Marc Sangnier's movement for reconciliation with the Republic, condemned by Pius X in 1910 — especially for holding that Catholics owe obedience to pope and bishops **only in spiritual matters**.
