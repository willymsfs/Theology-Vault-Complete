# Social Catholicism

The nineteenth-century Catholic answer to **industrial poverty**: more than charity, less than revolution.

> [!abstract] The problem it exists to solve
> The poverty produced by industrial society **could not be remedied by traditional charity**. Communism offered a rival answer that looked like **a secularized Christianity** — heaven on earth in a workers' paradise. So: **how to produce change without encouraging revolution?**

**The builders were mostly not popes.** [[Wilhelm von Ketteler]] (state intervention on hours, Sunday rest, a factory inspectorate), [[Leon Harmel]] (the model factory at Val-des-Bois and workers' pilgrimages to Rome), **Albert de Mun** (union recognition, child- and female-labour laws, accident compensation, pensions), **Émile Keller** (absolute freedom is the freedom of the rich to exploit the poor). [[Leo XIII]] gathered their work into [[Rerum Novarum]] (1891).

**Its shadow**: French Christian Democracy drifted anti-Masonic, anti-Protestant and anti-Semitic, aligning with the extreme right — which is part of why *Graves de communi re* (1901) fenced it in.
