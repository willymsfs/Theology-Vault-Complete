# Papal States

The territory in central Italy ruled by the pope as sovereign — the material basis of the [[Temporal Power]] and the subject of the [[Roman Question]].

**Timeline**: seized by revolutionary France (Avignon and the Venaissin, then the Romagna at **Tolentino, 1797**); Rome made a republic in 1798 and Pius VI deported; **restored by the Congress of Vienna (1815)**; chronically unstable thereafter (Carbonari, 1831 revolts, the five-power reform demand of 1832, the Roman Republic of 1849); progressively absorbed by Piedmont until **1861 leaves the pope only Rome and the Patrimony of Peter**; **1870 Rome falls**.

**Governing them was itself a theological problem.** [[Ercole Consalvi]] could open administration to laymen at lower levels and plan advisory councils, but **constitutionalism was held incompatible with papal government**: the absolutism of Catholicism had to be reflected in the pope's kingdom. That is why reform in the Papal States always stopped short.
