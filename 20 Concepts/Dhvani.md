# Dhvani

From classical Indian poetics (with *vyanjana*, language's capacity for suggested meaning): "the resonating effect of a word through which it evokes in the hearer deep meanings." Soares-Prabhu adapts it as a biblical hermeneutic — parables work like dhvani, evoking rather than defining; applied intertextually it "elicits deeper meaning in the hearer which otherwise would not have been perceived at all." His tool for depth the historical-critical method cannot reach.

## Worked example (Mk 4:35-41, Vol 2)
Take the narrative as it stands; read its three oppositions — great storm/great calm, sleeping Jesus/panicking disciples, passive passenger/commanding Lord — until they evoke a limit-experience: "the experience of ultimate stability behind the turmoil of our agitated and threatened existence."
