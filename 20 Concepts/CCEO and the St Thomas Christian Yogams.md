# CCEO and the St Thomas Christian Yogams

Two indigenous and Eastern Catholic precedents showing synodality was a functioning structure before
Vatican II supplied the word — evidence against the charge that synodality is a Western import.

**The CCEO** (*Codex Canonum Ecclesiarum Orientalium*). Unlike the Latin Synod of Bishops, which is
purely advisory, the Synods of Patriarchal and Major Archiepiscopal Churches — including the
Syro-Malabar and Syro-Malankara Churches in India — hold actual legislative, executive and judicial
power: electing their own Patriarchs and Major Archbishops, erecting eparchies, and enacting particular
law without prior Roman approval.

**The Yogams.** Before the Latinisation imposed by the Synod of Diamper (1599), the St Thomas
Christians were governed by a three-tier lay-clerical assembly system: the *Palliyogam* (parish
assembly, deciding local and judicial matters), the *Desiyayogam* and *Mahayogam* (regional and general
assemblies), and the *Jathikkukarthavian* (a native lay Archdeacon holding actual executive power, with
the foreign Metropolitan restricted to spiritual duties).
