# Four Phases of Vatican Policy in the American Civil War

Alvarez's reconstruction, against the older picture of a consistently pro-Union, passive Vatican.

| Phase | Date | Position | Driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Cautious neutrality** | 1861 | Antonelli: the Church does not enter domestic quarrels, but supports law and order everywhere | Reports predicting the Union's collapse; wait and see |
| **2. Tilt to the Union** | spring 1862 | Interest in a permanent mission in Washington; a consulate in San Francisco; Antonelli wants America to "cripple England" | Hughes's lobbying; federal victories; the Papacy's own isolation and Anglophobia |
| **3. Mediation, then disillusion** | late 1862 – 1863 | Discreet offer of **papal mediation** by "a small, militarily weak power"; then anger at the Emancipation Proclamation and the Spalding report | Northern military failure; Washington's refusal of all mediation |
| **4. Ambiguity, then realignment** | 1863–65 | The Davis letter; Confederate missions received; then, after Seward's warning, "strict neutrality" and public condemnation of slavery | Rising federal fortunes and Lincoln's re-election |

**The conclusion**: papal policy was **neither consistent, passive, nor exclusively humanitarian**. Political interest (support legitimate authority; weaken Britain) and humanitarian interest (stop the killing) pulled apart as the war lengthened — and **in the end the Papacy recognized the primacy of political imperatives.**
