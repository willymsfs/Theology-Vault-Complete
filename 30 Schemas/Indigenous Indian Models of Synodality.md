# Indigenous Indian Models of Synodality

Tribal and regional governance structures reaching decisions by consensus rather than majority vote --
independently arriving at the method of the Council of Jerusalem (Acts 15), for the same underlying
reason: a majority vote leaves a defeated minority inside the community.

1. **Khasi Durbar** (Meghalaya) — multi-tiered: the *Durbar Kur* (clan council, led by the
*Rangbahkur*) and the *Durbar Shnong* (village council, led by the *Rangbah-shnong*).
2. **Tangkhul Naga** — the village headman (*Awunga*) is restrained by a council of councillors
(the *Hangva*); customary law leaves "no room for the Awunga to be despotic."
3. **Kuki chieftainship** — nominally monarchic, but the chief cannot act unilaterally; bound by
customary law enforced through a participatory village council.
4. **Munda, Ho, Santhal, Oraon village assemblies** — decisions reached by patient dialogue until
consensus, never by majority vote.

Tribal parish data: the *Mahila Sangh* (women's council) functions in 74.4% of tribal parishes — more
widespread than the parish council itself (69.8%).

**Caution.** Not an unqualified model: the Khasi Durbar and Tangkhul Hangva are structurally restricted
to male clan heads. Indigenous synodality needs its own internal conversion on the point that matters
most for gender.
