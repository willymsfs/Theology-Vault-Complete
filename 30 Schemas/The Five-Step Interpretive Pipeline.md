# The Five-Step Interpretive Pipeline

| Step | Content | Failure if omitted |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Commitment & pre-understanding | Name the Indian contextual reality and your own social location; no neutral standpoint exists | Theology that serves someone's interest unawares |
| 2. Historical-critical exegesis | Establish the text's original intentionality; base and control against eisegesis | Eisegesis; reading India into the Bible |
| 3. Social analysis | Structures of the biblical world and of contemporary India | Sentiment about suffering without naming its causes |
| 4. Dual Indian reading | Religious (dhvani, Indian scripture) AND social (liberating praxis) | Either pietism or programme, never both |
| 5. Praxis | Total liberation: social change and freedom from inner bondage (moksha) | Academic barrenness; exegetes who "fiddle while Rome burns" |

The steps are ordered so each licenses the next. Stopping at step 2 is Western exegesis; skipping to step 5 is moralism.
