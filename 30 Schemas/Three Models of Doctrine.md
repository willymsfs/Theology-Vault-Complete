# Three Models of Doctrine (Lindbeck)

| Model | Doctrine is… | Religion is… | Fatal weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cognitive-propositional** | Objective truth-claims about reality | A set of beliefs | Cannot explain doctrinal change without contradiction |
| **Experiential-expressive** | Expression of a prior inner experience or feeling | A symbolization of universal religious experience | Makes the community's actual language secondary and interchangeable |
| **Cultural-linguistic** | **Communally authoritative rules** of discourse, attitude and action | A language and a form of life that shapes experience | Charged with relativism, sectarianism, thin account of truth |

**Corollaries of the third model**
- **Rule theory** — "Jesus is Lord" functions as a second-order directive; the rule holds while formulations change, which is how doctrine is both constant and historical.
- **Intratextuality** — theology redescribes reality inside the scriptural framework: *the text absorbs the world*.
- **Ecumenical payoff** — churches may agree on the rule while differing in formulation.
