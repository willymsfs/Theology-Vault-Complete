# Schweitzer's Assessment

|  | Content |
|---|---|
| Strength — exegesis | "Exemplary"; historical criticism protects semantic autonomy, making his readings defensible to the international guild while remaining "objective, relevant and Indian" |
| Strength — prophetic autonomy | A "Barth-like respect for the gospel's autonomy"; refused "an eternal covenant" with any culture, so Indian culture too must be confronted with Kingdom values |
| Strength — the poor | Treated as a social group with its own insight, not objects of charity; extended to Dalits in the 1990s |
| Limit — feminist theology | Acknowledged women as marginalised but "did not give sustained attention to concerns of women's liberation" |
| Limit — one story | Tended to hold the Bible "finally has one story to tell," flattening canonical diversity and neglecting ecological traditions |
| Limit — doctrine | Rejected Chalcedon and Nicaea as culturally conditioned without building alternatives, leaving "an important Christian moral source untapped" |

Schweitzer's summary: any theological framework is a hybrid whose critical edge stays "precarious and provisional"; the gospel sets the church "working, half in and half out" of a culture.
