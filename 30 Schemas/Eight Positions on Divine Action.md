# Eight Positions on Divine Action

Philip Clayton's spectrum, from most compatible with strict science to most robustly interventionist.

| # | Position | Representative |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Naturalist / physicalist — no special divine action | — |
| 2 | Deism | — |
| 3 | A **single divine act** (creation as one act, no particular interventions) | Maurice Wiles |
| 4 | **Double agency** — divine and creaturely causality at different levels, non-competing | Austin Farrer, Whitehead |
| 5 | **Top-down causality** — God shapes the state of the whole, which shapes the parts | Arthur Peacocke |
| 6 | Transhistorical / mystical truths | — |
| 7 | Natural order respected **except** for the resurrection | — |
| 8 | Robust, regular miraculous intervention | C. S. Lewis, C. John Collins |

**The favoured analogy:** panentheism — the world exists *in* God — with divine action likened to the mind's top-down influence on its own body: God works **through** natural processes rather than breaking them.

**Why the question reopened:** the collapse of the reductionist programme under relativity, quantum indeterminacy and chaos theory.
