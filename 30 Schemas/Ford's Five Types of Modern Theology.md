# Ford's Five Types of Modern Theology

A spectrum measuring **how a theology relates Christian identity to modernity** — not a scale of orthodoxy.

| Type | Position | Named representatives |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Repeats a traditional theology and construes all reality in its own terms, with **no recognition** of other perspectives or of recent centuries | Strict traditionalisms, fundamentalisms |
| **2** | **Priority to the self-description of the Christian community** — identity is primary, reality construed in relation to it — yet Christianity is continually rethought and seriously engaged with modernity | Barth, Bonhoeffer, de Lubac, Balthasar |
| **3** | **Correlation**: traditional faith in open dialogue with modernity, correlating the two without subsuming either | Tillich |
| **4** | **Integration** by consistently reinterpreting Christianity through a modern philosophy, conceptuality or pressing issue | Bultmann, Pannenberg |
| **5** | **Complete priority to a secular philosophy or worldview**; Christianity assessed by external criteria and accepted only so far as it fits | Radical liberal postmoderns, non-realism |

**Rules of use**
1. It classifies a *relationship*, not quality or truth.
2. Most major figures **shift type by topic** — Rahner is Type 4 in fundamental theology and near Type 2 in ecclesiology and spirituality.
3. Types 1 and 5 are limit cases; the arguments live between 2, 3 and 4.
4. In this vault the `ford_type` frontmatter field is populated **only where Ford names the type**; see [[Theologians Index (Dataview)]].
