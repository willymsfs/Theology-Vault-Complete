# Obstacles to Synodality in the Indian Church

Seven distinct obstacle categories, not one undifferentiated "resistance to change."

1. **Caste** — upper-caste clergy dominance, casteism carried into seminaries and religious formation,
Dalit clergy sometimes adopting the dominant mindset once ordained rather than remaining in solidarity.
2. **Clericalism** — priest-centred parishes, resistance to real lay authority (not merely lay labour),
the gap between consultative sessions and actual decision-making.
3. **Women religious and gender** — exclusion from decision-making structures despite constituting the
active majority of parish life; patriarchal control extending into religious congregations themselves.
4. **Tribal-specific pressures** — land alienation and displacement, the collision between tribal
customary law and canon law, Latin-rite liturgical uniformity suppressing genuine inculturation,
anti-conversion legislation and Hindutva surveillance restricting open assembly.
5. **Structural/canonical obstacles** — see [[The Consultative Vote (Canon Law)]]; councils that exist
on paper but never meet, or are populated by appointees chosen to agree.
6. **Formation** — seminary formation not equipping priests for synodal, collaborative leadership; see
[[Susairaj]].
7. **Self-critique of synodality itself** — the charge that the process is rhetoric without structural
change, captured by an educated elite, and framed in Eurocentric theological vocabulary that does not
reach the illiterate or the Adivasi poor it claims to serve.
