# Historical Series of Definitions of Theology

How "theology" has been defined across eras (Ch 1, section B).

| Era / Author | Definition | Exact phrase |
|--------------|-----------|--------------|
| Etymology | God-talk | *theos* + *logos* |
| Greek philosophers | metaphysics | (Plato, Aristotle) |
| Church Fathers | Trinitarian/Christological + Scripture | Clement, Origen |
| **Anselm** (medieval) | faith seeking understanding | *fides quaerens intellectum* |
| Eastern Churches | prayer shapes belief | *Lex orandi, lex credendi* |
| **Rahner** (modern) | theological anthropology | "God and humanity are correlative terms" |
| **Gutierrez** | critical reflection on praxis | orthodoxy → orthopraxis |
| Hermeneutics | two-way interpretation | faith ↔ contemporary reality |
| Mythos | reflection on the Christian story | "set of symbols, rituals, narratives" |

## The book's working definition
> "Christian theology is a systematic and critical interpretation on the meaning of human life and reality... from the perspective of the Revelation in Jesus Christ... a reinterpretation of Christian Faith... in the light of the changing realities of the world, in and by the believing community."
