# Four Sources of Black Theology

Hopkins's account of what black theology of liberation actually draws on:

1. **African genealogical heritage** — the religious and cultural inheritance that survived the Middle Passage.
2. **The legacy of slavery** — including the slave religion that read the Bible against the master's reading.
3. **A common heritage of struggle** — from Marcus Garvey through the Civil Rights Movement and Black Power.
4. **The centrality of the Bible**, read through the lens of liberation, with **Exodus** as the controlling paradigm.

**First-generation positions:** Cone (liberation as the gospel's core), Cleage (a literally black Jesus leading national liberation), J. Deotis Roberts (liberation *and* reconciliation), Cecil Cone (Africanized Christianity first), Charles Long (Christianity itself as imperialist discourse).

**Later corrections:** womanist theology (Delores Williams — Hagar rather than Exodus, survival rather than liberation); African American biblical hermeneutics (Felder); pastoral theology of wholeness (Wimberly).
