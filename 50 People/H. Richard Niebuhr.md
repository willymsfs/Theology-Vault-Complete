# H. Richard Niebuhr

H. Richard Niebuhr's "radical monotheism" centres faith on God's absolute sovereignty alone, condemning "Christomonism" — the defensive absolutizing of any finite thing, even Christ, the church, or a nation — as idolatry, and reframes moral life as the "responsible self" discerning the historically "fitting" response to God's ongoing action.

## Key Terms
[[Radical Monotheism|Radical Monotheism]] · [[Theocentric Relativism|Theocentric Relativism]] · [[The Responsible Self|The Responsible Self]] · [[The Fitting|The Fitting]]

## Connections
Challenges Barth's Christological objectivism (Part I) as risking "Christomonism." His account of historical relativity directly anticipated Hans Frei's narrative theology and the whole postliberal "Yale school" (Part II.D). Engaged in a lifelong dialogical debate with his brother Reinhold Niebuhr (next chapter). Later sharply criticised by John Howard Yoder for implicitly ranking "Christ the transformer of culture" above the concrete nonviolent ethics of Jesus.
