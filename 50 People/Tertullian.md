# Tertullian

Writing against the modalist Praxeas, coined Trinitas and formulated una substantia, tres personae — one substance, three persons — the West's permanent trinitarian vocabulary, defended by prosopological exegesis of Old Testament dialogue passages.

## Sacraments (BTh course)
De Baptismo (c. 200) is the first Christian treatise devoted to a single sacrament, famous for its water theology ("we, little fishes, after the example of our Ichthys Jesus Christ, are born in water") and for describing the post-baptismal unction and hand-laying that would become Confirmation. He is also the first named opponent of infant baptism (De Bapt. 18): "Let them become Christians when they have become able to know Christ."

## Penance (BTh course)
Argues both sides of the penitential question at different periods, making him a superb illustration of the early Church's tension. De Paenitentia (Catholic period, c. 203) permits the "second repentance," describes exomologesis vividly, and urges sinners not to delay out of shame. De Pudicitia (Montanist period, c. 217) reverses course, denying the Church any power to forgive adultery and fornication and attacking a bishop — probably Callistus of Rome — for extending forgiveness to sins of the flesh.

See: [[Penance MOC]]


## Matrimony (BTh course)
Ad Uxorem II.8 contains the era's most beautiful description of Christian marriage — "which the Church arranges, the oblation confirms, the blessing seals, angels proclaim, and the Father holds as ratified... two in one flesh; where the flesh is one, one also is the spirit" — evidence of ecclesial involvement in marriage well before any required rite.
