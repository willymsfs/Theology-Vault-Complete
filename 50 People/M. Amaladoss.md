# M. Amaladoss

## Signature
Asian liberation theology; inter-religious and inculturation thought.

## Connected
[[Liberation Theology]] · [[Indian liberation theology must add caste and pluralism to class analysis]]


## Sacraments (BTh course)
In Do Sacraments Change? he proposes "a new insight into the structure and function of the sacraments as symbolic actions" to address the problems posed by attempts at adaptation. Notably, he resisted proposing a concrete Indian Rite, arguing that inculturation must first rest on "a minimum of psycho-sociological research on life situations and living symbols" — a methodological caution directly relevant to SC 65's permission for local initiation practices.

See: [[Sacraments MOC]]


## Eucharist (BTh course)
His methodological caution about inculturation — that it must begin from psycho-sociological research into living symbols rather than from a scholar's design — applies directly to the Indian Rite and Indian Anaphora debates surrounding Eucharistic adaptation in South Asia.

See: [[Eucharist MOC]]


## Penance (BTh course)
His methodological caution about inculturation applies directly to reconciliation: adaptation must proceed from actual living symbols of reconciliation within the culture — village and communal practices of dispute resolution and restored relationship — rather than from a designer's scheme.

See: [[Penance MOC]]


## Anointing of the Sick (BTh course)
His rule that inculturation must proceed from actual living symbols applies with unusual force here, since the living symbols of healing devotion in India are abundantly documented at shrines such as Velankanni — the pastoral task being neither to dismiss them as superstition nor to absorb transactional religiosity uncritically into the sacrament.

See: [[Anointing MOC]]


## Holy Orders (BTh course)
His rule that inculturation must begin from actual living symbols applies to priestly formation as much as to liturgy: a seminary curriculum transplanted unchanged from Europe forms men for a Church that does not exist in the context they will serve.

See: [[Holy Orders MOC]]


## Matrimony (BTh course)
His rule that inculturation must begin from actual living symbols bears on marriage preparation as much as on liturgy: programmes transplanted from Europe assume a companionate-marriage model that may not describe the couple's situation, and effective preparation in the Indian context engages the families and not only the couple.

See: [[Matrimony MOC]]
