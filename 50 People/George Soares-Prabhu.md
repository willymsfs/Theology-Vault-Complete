# George M. Soares-Prabhu, SJ

Indian Jesuit biblical theologian (Jnana-Deepa Vidyapeeth, Pune). Pioneer of Indian liberation hermeneutics: biblical exegesis read through poverty, caste, and pluriform religiosity.

## Signature move
The **Integral Indian Reading** — historical-critical exegesis retained as "control," then a dual reading: *religious* (dhvani, intertext with Indian scriptures) + *social* (liberating praxis for the oppressed), converging in praxis: liberation from social oppression **and** personal bondage (*moksha*).

## Core constants
- Jesus's **Abba experience** grounds all: unconditional love → solidarity with poor → conflict with powerful → cross.
- **Kingdom of God** = new society: personal freedom, communitarian fellowship, structural justice.
- **Dharma of Jesus**: Sermon on the Mount as life-style from Abba experience, not utopian law.
- The **poor (anawim)** = sociologically powerless — never spiritualized, never merely Marxist class.

## Sharpest polemics
- Western exegesis: "ideologically loaded," exegetes "fiddle while Rome burns."
- Latin American liberation theology: right on structures, blind to Asian religiosity.
- Indian Church: colonial "subsidiary of a Rome-based company"; caste in Church = Eucharist as "sacrilege."
- Ashram spirituality: "brahmanical spirituality of an affluent, privileged class."

## Sacraments (BTh course)
His judgement that caste within the Church makes the Eucharist "a sacrilege" and "parody" applies with equal force to baptism: if Galatians 3:27-28 means the font genuinely incorporates into a body where there is "neither slave nor free," then caste among the baptised contradicts the sacrament's own stated effect. This is the sharpest available application of baptismal theology in the South Asian context.

See: [[Sacraments MOC]]


## Eucharist (BTh course)
His judgement that caste practised within the Church makes the Eucharist "a sacrilege" and a "parody" rests on Paul's own logic at 1 Cor 10:17 and 11:29: if the one bread makes the many one body, then division at the table contradicts what the sacrament states. He reads Jesus's table fellowship with tax collectors and sinners as an "acted parable" of the Abba experience, placing the Eucharist in continuity with that boundary-breaking practice.

See: [[Eucharist MOC]]


## Penance (BTh course)
His judgement on caste applies to reconciliation as directly as to the Eucharist: reconciliation with the Church one has wounded by sin (LG 11) is hollow where caste division within that same Church remains unaddressed, and the category of social sin supplies the vocabulary for naming this in preaching and in the confessional.

See: [[Penance MOC]]


## Holy Orders (BTh course)
His judgement that caste within the Church makes the Eucharist a "sacrilege" and "parody" bears with particular force on Holy Orders, since the ordained exist to serve and signify the very unity that caste denies, and since it is the priest who presides at the Eucharist in question — making a caste-stratified clergy a contradiction of the sacrament in its own proper act.

See: [[Holy Orders MOC]]


## Matrimony (BTh course)
His judgement that caste in the Church parodies the Eucharist extends directly to marriage, which is the practical mechanism by which caste endogamy is reproduced in the Christian community — making caste-endogamous marriage a contradiction both of what baptism effected and of the sacrament of unity being used as the instrument of division.

See: [[Matrimony MOC]]


## Soares-Prabhu study (primary sources)
His corpus is available in the vault's linked notebooks and mapped at [[Soares-Prabhu MOC]]. Three refinements from the primary sources worth holding precisely:

**The *Abba* experience is located** — at the baptism by John, where he "identified himself with sinful Israel" — and is called "homologous to the 'enlightenment' of the Buddha under the boddhi tree, or the call-experience of the Old Testament prophets." It is "profoundly unsettling," not a "soothing anodyne."

**The Sermon on the Mount is read through three sutras** — not law but love; not law but gospel; a goal-directed norm — and as the Christian *svadharma*. Note carefully: his phrase "thoroughly utopian law" is a *reductio* of the legalistic reading, **not** his own verdict.

**The poor are blessed "not because their dehumanizing poverty is a spiritual value but precisely because it is an evil which he is about to bring to an end."** He never sacralises poverty.

**Known limitations** (Don Schweitzer): he neglected feminist theology; tended to reduce Scripture to "one story" from Exodus to Jesus, flattening its diversity; and rejected Chalcedon and Nicaea as culturally conditioned without building alternatives, leaving "an important Christian moral source untapped."

See: [[Soares-Prabhu MOC]]
