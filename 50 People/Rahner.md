# Karl Rahner

Pioneer of Theological Anthropology; leading figure before and during Vatican II.

## Signature phrase
"God and humanity are correlative terms."

## In Chapter 1
Gives the modern definition of theology as theological anthropology — study of the human being in fundamental openness to God.

## Connected
[[Theology]] · [[Religious Experience]] · [[Historical Series of Definitions of Theology]]
Fuller treatment: Ch 4 model ("supernatural existential," "absolute saviour").


## Ecclesiology (BTh course)
Cited in Unit 7 alongside Küng on the theology of an ecumenical council: both stress that a council is a privileged event of the whole Church's self-reflection under the Spirit, not a mere administrative assembly. See [[Lit - Ecclesiology Unit 7]].

## Modern Theologians (BTh course)
Rahner's "transcendental" or "anthropological" theology argues every human act of knowing carries a pre-reflective, unthematic openness to the infinite mystery of God (Vorgriff), so that grace is a universal structural feature of human existence and even non-Christians who respond to it in love can be called "anonymous Christians."

Key terms: [[Vorgriff]] · [[Übernatürliches Existenzial]] · [[Anonymer Christ]] · [[Transcendental Method]]

Connections: Fiercely opposed by von Balthasar (next chapter), who judged Rahner's anthropological turn a dilution of Christian particularity; his trinitarian axiom ("the economic Trinity is the immanent Trinity") influenced Moltmann and Pannenberg (Part II.A); his Christology is famously set against Barth's in Bruce Marshall's comparative study Christology in Conflict.

Source: [[MT - Ch05 Karl Rahner]]


## Vatican II (BTh course)
Peritus to Cardinal König; active member of the drafting commissions for Christus Dominus and Gaudium et Spes; co-authored (with the young Joseph Ratzinger) the alternative revelation schema circulated in the Council's critical early weeks. His "World Church" thesis (three macro-epochs of Christian history) frames Vatican II as Catholicism's first self-realization as a genuinely global Church.

See: [[Vatican II MOC]]


## Trinity (BTh course)
Formulated the Grundaxiom ("the economic Trinity is the immanent Trinity and vice versa"), the shared premise nearly every 20th-century Trinitarian revival builds on (Moltmann) or reacts against (Emery). Also preferred "modes of subsistence" (Subsistenzweisen) over "person" language, warning that modern "person" connotes "three centres of consciousness" and risks a heretical misunderstanding.

See: [[Trinity MOC]]
