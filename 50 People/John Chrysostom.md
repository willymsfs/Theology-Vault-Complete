# John Chrysostom

His Baptismal Instructions are among the four great patristic mystagogical catechesis collections, giving a detailed Antiochene picture of the rites, the renunciation of Satan, and the moral formation expected of neophytes.

## Eucharist (BTh course)
Contributed both the language of awe ("the fearful mysteries," "the dread table") and the sharpest social application in patristic literature: "You wish to honour the body of Christ? Do not despise him when he is naked... What use is it to weigh down Christ's table with golden cups when he himself is dying of hunger?" The altar and the poor are the same body.

## Matrimony (BTh course)
Offers the warmest patristic counterweight to Western unease about marriage, particularly in his homilies on Ephesians 5 and his sermon On Marriage, urging husbands to say to their wives "I have taken you in my arms, and I love you, and I prefer you to my life itself," and grounding marriage in love rather than procreation alone. His image of the household as a little church stands behind LG 11's ecclesia domestica.
