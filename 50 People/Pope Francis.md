# Pope Francis

> A note of the same name exists in the **Vatican II course package**: [[BTh Level Notes/VaticanII-Notes/Popes/Pope Francis (Vatican II course)|Pope Francis — Vatican II course treatment]].


Author of Evangelii Gaudium (2013) and driver of the Synod on Synodality (2021-2024); his ecclesiology is read in this course as a renewed attempt to complete Vatican II's nine paradigm shifts in institutional practice.

## Vatican II (BTh course)
Represents, per Faggioli and other reception scholars, a new macro-phase in the Council's reception — largely bypassing the academic continuity/discontinuity debate in favor of a "generative reception" through synodality and pastoral gesture.

See: [[Vatican II MOC]]


## Penance (BTh course)
The Jubilee of Mercy documents Misericordiae Vultus (2015) and Misericordia et Misera (2016) instituted the Missionaries of Mercy with special faculties and extended to all priests the faculty to absolve the sin of abortion, framing the sacrament principally in terms of divine mercy and pastoral accessibility.

See: [[Penance MOC]]


## Holy Orders (BTh course)
Has made clericalism the central critique of the clerical state, naming it in the 2018 Letter to the People of God as nullifying the baptismal grace of the laity and as a root of the abuse crisis. Declined the Amazon Synod's viri probati proposal in Querida Amazonia (2020), and opened the instituted ministries of lector and acolyte to women in Spiritus Domini (2021).

See: [[Holy Orders MOC]]


## Matrimony (BTh course)
Convened the Synods of 2014 and 2015 on the family and issued Amoris Laetitia (2016), whose chapter 8 proposes accompanying, discerning, and integrating those in irregular situations; the Buenos Aires criteria he approved were published in the AAS as authentic magisterium. Mitis Iudex Dominus Iesus (2015) reformed the annulment process, abolishing the second concordant sentence and introducing a brief process before the bishop.

See: [[Matrimony MOC]]
