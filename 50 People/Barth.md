# Barth

Theology speaks only of the God of the Gospel.

## Signature phrases
Task is "to understand and speak of 'God'"; must "renounce all external guarantees."

## Connected
[[Theology]] · [[Revelation]]


## Modern Theologians (BTh course)
Barth reconstructed Protestant theology around a single object: God's own self-initiated, sovereign act of self-revelation in Jesus Christ, known only through grace and never through general human religious experience.

Key terms: [[Actualism]] · [[Krisis]] · [[Nachdenken]] · [[Das Nichtige]]

Connections: Barth is the paradigm case of Ford's "type two" theology ("faith seeking understanding": Christian self-description as primary, yet always rethought in engagement with the modern world) — a type shared, in the book's own account, by Bonhoeffer, de Lubac, von Balthasar, Torrance, and most postliberal theologians. He directly shaped the systematic work of Jüngel, Jenson, and Gunton (Unit 9 below), and Torrance's "scientific theology"; his relation to von Balthasar (over the analogia entis) and his early influence on the young Bonhoeffer are two of the volume's central inter-chapter threads.

Source: [[MT - Ch01 Karl Barth]]


## Trinity (BTh course)
Rejects modern "person" language for the Trinity (which he judges carries misleading Cartesian-Lockean psychological connotations) in favor of "modes of being" (Seinsweisen), preserving single divine subjectivity while affirming real threeness.
