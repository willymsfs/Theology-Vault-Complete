# James H. Cone

Father of contemporary black theology; *Black Theology and Black Power* (1969) followed the 1966 "Black Power" statement of black clergy.

## Signature idea
The core message of the Bible is the **liberation of the materially poor and oppressed** — and all God-talk inevitably advances some notion of racial power. There is no neutral theology in a racialized society.

## Key terms
**Blackness** as an ideological orientation and condition of mind, not merely skin colour · **Jesus Christ Liberator**.

## Sources
African genealogical heritage; the legacy of slavery; the heritage of struggle from Garvey to the Civil Rights Movement; the Bible read through the Exodus.

## Criticism
Womanists (notably [[Delores Williams]]) charged early black theology with rendering black women invisible; Charles Long argued that using Christian categories is an "imitative game" with an imperialist discourse.
