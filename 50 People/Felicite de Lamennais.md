# Félicité de Lamennais

Priest, founder of **Catholic Liberalism** — and its casualty.

## The programme
An **[[Ultramontanism|ultramontane]]**: the Restoration had **subordinated the Church to the state**, so a strong papacy was needed to free it. With **Lacordaire OP** and **Montalembert**, the "apostles of God and liberty," he founded ***L'Avenir*** (motto: **God and Liberty**) and went to Rome in December 1831 to convince [[Gregory XVI]] that **the Church should be united with the people**.

## The condemnations
French bishops, Metternich and the Russian ambassador turned the pope against him. ***Mirari vos*** (15 August 1832) rejected freedom of conscience, freedom of the press and the idea of a just revolution; *L'Avenir* closed. He replied with ***Words of a Believer*** (1834) and was personally condemned in ***Singulari nos*** (June 1834). Metternich said he had gone mad. **He left the Church.**

## Why he matters
He is the road not taken. Rome chose **[[Joseph de Maistre]]**'s papacy — a brake on the state — over Lamennais's — an ally of the people. A Catholic liberal option was foreclosed for a century, until the questions returned as Christian Democracy and, later, Vatican II.
