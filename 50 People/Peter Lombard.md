# Peter Lombard

His Sentences Book IV (c. 1150s) first authoritatively fixed the sacraments at seven; as the standard theology textbook of the medieval university, it made the septenary the shared framework of all subsequent Western sacramental theology.

## Penance (BTh course)
Sentences IV treats Penance among the seven sacraments and transmits the framework of the penitent's acts to the whole scholastic tradition, setting the terms of the contrition/attrition debate that would run from Abelard to Trent.

See: [[Penance MOC]]


## Anointing of the Sick (BTh course)
Sentences IV d. 23 lists unctio extrema among the seven sacraments and standardises the name "extreme unction" — a designation that both recorded and reinforced the sacrament's migration from the healing of the sick to the preparation of the dying.

See: [[Anointing MOC]]


## Holy Orders (BTh course)
Sentences IV lists Orders among the seven sacraments and transmits the scheme of seven orders — porter, lector, exorcist, acolyte, subdeacon, deacon, priest — which notably omits the episcopate, since orders was defined by its relation to the Eucharist that a priest can already confect.

See: [[Holy Orders MOC]]


## Matrimony (BTh course)
Against Gratian and the Bolognese canonists, the Paris school he led held consensus facit nuptias — consent alone makes the marriage — arguing that otherwise the marriage of Mary and Joseph would not have been a true marriage. Alexander III's synthesis adopted this as to validity while making consummation the ground of absolute indissolubility.
