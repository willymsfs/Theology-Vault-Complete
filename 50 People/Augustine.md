# St Augustine of Hippo

## Historical context
Late Roman Empire; Christianity becoming intellectually influential; responded to Manichaeism and scepticism.

## Signature idea
"Believe that you may understand." Reflection on God from the standpoint of one already being saved.

## Claims in my vault
- [[Theology is faith seeking understanding, not proof seeking certainty]]

## Important works
Confessions · City of God · On the Trinity

## Trinity (BTh course)
Author of De Trinitate, source of the West's psychological analogy (mens/notitia/amor, later memoria/intelligentia/voluntas) illustrating — not proving — the Trinity from the structure of the human mind. Luigi Gioia reframes the whole treatise as an epistemology of revelation; Maarten Wisse deploys Augustine's insistence on the Trinity's radical otherness against modern relational/social trinitarian revivals.

See: [[Trinity MOC]]


## Sacraments (BTh course)
Two controversies made him the foundation of Western sacramental theology. Against the Donatists he distinguished validity (which depends on Christ — "Judas baptises, still this is he that baptises") from fruitfulness (which depends on charity and communion), and articulated the indelible character (character dominicus) that makes baptism unrepeatable. Against the Pelagians he argued from the Church's practice of baptising infants for the remission of sins that infants must have sin to remit — the classic case of lex orandi, lex credendi. The cost was his severe position on unbaptised infants, softened medievally into limbus infantium and set aside by the ITC in 2007.

See: [[Sacraments MOC]]


## Eucharist (BTh course)
The most contested patristic figure in Eucharistic theology, claimed by both Catholics and Reformers. He holds a robust sacramentum/res distinction whose res is the unity of the Body of Christ, teaches totus Christus ("you will be changed into me"), and in Sermon 272 tells the newly baptised: "If you are the body of Christ and his members, it is your own mystery that is placed on the Lord's table... Be what you see, and receive what you are." His sign-language is not modern symbolism — forcing him onto either side of the Berengarian or Reformation divide is anachronistic.

See: [[Eucharist MOC]]


## Penance (BTh course)
Contributed the framework of three kinds of penance (before baptism, for daily sins, and the grave canonical penance) and assessed deathbed penance with characteristic honesty — we cannot be sure such a person is condemned, but neither can we praise the security of the repentance. His anti-Donatist argument for the Church as corpus permixtum is the same principle the Church had already established against Novatianism.

See: [[Penance MOC]]


## Holy Orders (BTh course)
His answer to the Donatists supplied the theology of orders with its permanent structure: validity depends on Christ rather than on the minister's worthiness, and the indelible character — compared to a soldier's brand, which a deserter still bears — makes ordination unrepeatable. Three consequences follow: re-ordination is impossible, an unworthy minister ordains validly, and orders can be valid but illicit.

See: [[Holy Orders MOC]]


## Matrimony (BTh course)
De bono coniugali (401) established the three goods — proles, fides, and sacramentum — that governed Western marriage theology for fifteen centuries, written in part to correct the severity of Jerome's attack on Jovinian. But De nuptiis et concupiscentia, arguing against the Pelagians, holds that intercourse is now inevitably accompanied by concupiscence and that original sin is transmitted through generation — leaving a tradition that affirms marriage's goodness while treating its sexual expression with persistent unease.
