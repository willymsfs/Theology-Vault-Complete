# Henri de Lubac

De Lubac retrieved patristic and medieval sources (ressourcement) to argue there is no "pure nature" separate from the supernatural: every human person carries an innate, unmerited desire for the vision of God, which the modern nature/grace dualism had obscured.

## Key Terms

## Connections
De Lubac mentored and worked alongside Hans Urs von Balthasar at Lyon, who adopted his ressourcement method; alongside Rahner and Schillebeeckx he is a pioneer of the Catholic theological renewal that shaped Vatican II (Part II.D). John Milbank, the chapter's own author, builds his Radical Orthodoxy movement (Part II.E) directly on de Lubac's critique of the nature/grace divide.

## Vatican II (BTh course)
Co-founder of Sources chrétiennes (1942, with Daniélou); silenced after Humani Generis (1950), vindicated as a conciliar peritus, made cardinal in 1983; later a founding editor of Communio (1972), positioning himself against Concilium's reading of the Council.

See: [[Vatican II MOC]]


## Eucharist (BTh course)
Corpus Mysticum (1944) demonstrates that before the twelfth century corpus verum named the Church and corpus mysticum named the Eucharist, and that the Berengarian controversy caused the two terms to exchange places. His formula "the Eucharist makes the Church" reverses the resulting drift, and lies behind Lumen Gentium's communion ecclesiology and almost all subsequent Catholic sacramental ecclesiology.
