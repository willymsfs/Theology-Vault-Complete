# Jürgen Moltmann

Moltmann's "theology of hope" holds cross and resurrection together dialectically, replaces monarchical monotheism with a "social Trinity" of co-suffering, perichoretic persons, and translates directly into political solidarity with victims and ecological care for creation.

## Key Terms
[[Social Trinity|Social Trinity]] · [[Perichoresis|Perichoresis]] · [[Trinitarian Panentheism|Trinitarian Panentheism]] · [[Christologia Viae|Christologia Viae]]

## Connections
Initially followed Barth, later broke with him to stress lived experience of the Spirit over Barth's tightly bounded revelation. Colleague of Pannenberg at Wuppertal; retrieved Bonhoeffer's social ethics; drew on the Marxist philosopher Ernst Bloch's "philosophy of hope." Criticised by von Balthasar for risking the collapse of eternal, inner-trinitarian suffering into mere temporal world-process. Strongly influenced the Church of England's social theology in the 1970s-80s, though Rowan Williams later worried it risked "sacralizing" the present social order.

## Trinity (BTh course)
The Trinity and the Kingdom of God (1980) grounds divine unity in the perichoresis of three co-equal persons rather than a prior substance, with an explicit political payoff of "the democratic community of free people" against "monarchical monotheism." Critiqued by Joy Ann McDougall (risk of tritheism, covert need for the Father's monarchy) and Karen Kilby ("vicious hermeneutical circle" of projection).
