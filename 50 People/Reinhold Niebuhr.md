# Reinhold Niebuhr

Reinhold Niebuhr's "Christian Realism" rejects both liberal sentimentalism (believing love alone resolves political conflict) and dogmatic insularity, insisting that because sinful self-interest inevitably taints even collective moral action, justice requires the realistic, sober use of power, coercion, and resistance — even though sacrificial love (agape) remains history's ultimate, if humanly "impossible," norm.

## Connections
Shares Tillich's method of correlating biblical symbols with existential human analysis (Part I). Engaged in a lifelong public debate with his brother H. Richard Niebuhr (previous chapter). Paul Lehmann criticised his Christology as too cross-focused and under-pneumatological. Fiercely opposed by pacifists John Howard Yoder and Stanley Hauerwas (also this volume's chapter author for H. Richard Niebuhr) for sacralizing state violence and compromising the Church's distinct, nonviolent witness.
