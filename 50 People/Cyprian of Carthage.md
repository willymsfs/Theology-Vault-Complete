# Cyprian of Carthage

Argued in the rebaptism controversy against Pope Stephen I that baptism conferred by heretics or schismatics is invalid — "you cannot give what you do not have." Rome's contrary position prevailed and became tradition. In Ep. 64 and at the Council of Carthage (253) he decisively defended infant baptism, arguing it need not wait until the eighth day.

## Eucharist (BTh course)
Ep. 63 is the first sustained treatise on the Eucharistic cup, insisting on the mixed chalice — the water is the people and the wine is Christ, and they cannot be separated — and speaking plainly of the priest offering a sacrifice "in the place of Christ" that imitates what Christ did.

## Penance (BTh course)
De Lapsis (251) navigates the crisis of the Decian persecution between two extremes: against the confessors issuing libelli pacis on their own merits, he insists reconciliation belongs to the bishop's ordinary ministry rather than to private spiritual authority; against Novatianist rigorism, he holds that the lapsed can be forgiven after penance graded by the gravity of the lapse. The settlement he brokered is the foundation of the Church's claim that no sin is beyond forgiveness.

## Holy Orders (BTh course)
The decisive figure in the sacerdotalisation of Christian ministry, applying sacerdos and summus sacerdos to the bishop and speaking of the bishop offering sacrifice in the place of Christ (vice Christi). Since the New Testament never calls a Christian minister hiereus, this third-century development — a corollary of the Eucharist's increasingly sacrificial theology — is where the priestly vocabulary for ministers enters the tradition.
