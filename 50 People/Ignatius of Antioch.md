# Ignatius of Antioch

Writing en route to martyrdom, he called the Eucharist the "medicine of immortality" (pharmakon athanasias) and argued against the Docetists that they abstain from it "because they do not confess that the Eucharist is the flesh of our Saviour Jesus Christ" — establishing that Eucharistic realism is a corollary of Incarnational realism.

## Holy Orders (BTh course)
His letters (c. 107) give the first unambiguous evidence of the monepiscopate — a single bishop with a college of presbyters and deacons — urging that nothing pertaining to the Church be done without the bishop, and that where the bishop appears, there the people should be. His analogy places the bishop in the place of God the Father, the presbyters as the apostolic council, the deacons entrusted with the service of Jesus Christ. That he urges rather than merely describes the structure is itself evidence that it was not yet universal.
