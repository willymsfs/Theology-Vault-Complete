## Claims
- [[Tertullian's subordinationist Trinitas is the indispensable but unstable ancestor of the West's later relatio subsistens vocabulary]]
- [[Marcellus of Ancyra's condemnation, not Arius's, is what actually delayed Nicene reception in the East for a generation]]
- [[Beeley's revisionist Gregory of Nazianzus undermines the standard textbook claim that the Cappadocians spoke with one harmonious voice]]
- [[Augustine's psychological analogy and Richard of St Victor's love-based argument are not two independent proofs but answer two different theological problems]]
- [[Aquinas's and Bonaventure's disagreement over the Father's innascibility (pure negation vs. fontalis plenitudo) is a genuine, unresolved fault line inside orthodox Trinitarian theology, not a settled question]]
- [[The Council of Florence's collapse proves that a technically competent theological compromise (tamquam ex uno principio, dia tou Huiou treated as equivalent) cannot substitute for genuine reception by the wider Church]]
- [[Rahner's Grundaxiom and Emery's Thomistic ressourcement are not simply for-and-against positions but disagree about what kind of statement Trinitarian theology is allowed to make]]
- [[Moltmann's and Zizioulas's shared appeal to the Cappadocians produces opposite ecclesiologies because each retrieves a different, genuinely contested strand within Cappadocian theology itself]]
- [[Volf's distinction between 'relations of origin' and 'eternal relations of love' is a more radical dogmatic move than it first appears]]
- [[Kilby's 'vicious hermeneutical circle' critique applies with different force to Moltmann, Zizioulas, and Volf]]
- [[Panikkar's cosmotheandric vision and the PCID's 'two hands of God' framework represent genuinely incompatible, not merely differently emphasized, uses of Trinitarian theology in interfaith dialogue]]
- [[The coherence of 'person'-language for the Trinity depends entirely on which historical definition of 'person' is smuggled into the discussion]]
- [[Holmes's and Kärkkäinen's simplicity-based critique of social trinitarianism and Wisse's Augustinian critique of the same movement (Part 3) converge on an identical diagnosis from different centuries]]
