Claims

  • Innocent I's Letter to Decentius (416) is the single strongest historical argument that the reservation of anointing to priests is a development rather than an apostolic given
  • The migration of anointing to the deathbed was caused by the accidental bundling of rites in the Carolingian reform, not by any doctrinal development
  • The medieval practice created a self-reinforcing pastoral trap that only structural reform could break
  • Trent's word praesertim is what made the Vatican II reform legally possible without contradicting the Council of Trent
  • Cajetan's exegesis shows the case for reform was available within Catholic theology before the Reformation and was not merely a Protestant objection
  • Aquinas's doctrine of the reliquiae peccati is an elegant solution that nonetheless entrenched the very deathbed orientation the modern reform had to undo
  • The 1972 reform's method was ressourcement, going behind the medieval development to the scriptural text
  • Viaticum, not anointing, is the sacrament of the dying, and modern pastoral practice routinely inverts this
  • The claim that the sick contribute to the good of the Church is the sacrament's deepest insight and its greatest pastoral risk simultaneously
  • The Latin and Orthodox trajectories diverged not over doctrine but over whether a deathbed phase ever intervened
  • Indian healing devotion and the sacrament of anointing occupy different ritual spheres, and the patristic precedent is the available bridge
  • Bayly's judgement that the converters were themselves converted reframes Indian healing shrines as evidence of indigenisation rather than syncretism

  • Beeley's revisionist Gregory of Nazianzus undermines the standard textbook claim that the Cappadocians spoke with one harmonious voice
  • Augustine's psychological analogy and Richard of St Victor's love-based argument are not two independent proofs but answer two different theological problems
  • Aquinas's and Bonaventure's disagreement over the Father's innascibility (pure negation vs. fontalis plenitudo) is a genuine, unresolved fault line inside orthodox Trinitarian theology, not a settled question
  • The Council of Florence's collapse proves that a technically competent theological compromise (tamquam ex uno principio, dia tou Huiou treated as equivalent) cannot substitute for genuine reception by the wider Church
  • Rahner's Grundaxiom and Emery's Thomistic ressourcement are not simply for-and-against positions but disagree about what kind of statement Trinitarian theology is allowed to make
  • Moltmann's and Zizioulas's shared appeal to the Cappadocians produces opposite ecclesiologies because each retrieves a different, genuinely contested strand within Cappadocian theology itself
  • Volf's distinction between 'relations of origin' and 'eternal relations of love' is a more radical dogmatic move than it first appears
  • Kilby's 'vicious hermeneutical circle' critique applies with different force to Moltmann, Zizioulas, and Volf
  • Panikkar's cosmotheandric vision and the PCID's 'two hands of God' framework represent genuinely incompatible, not merely differently emphasized, uses of Trinitarian theology in interfaith dialogue
  • The coherence of 'person'-language for the Trinity depends entirely on which historical definition of 'person' is smuggled into the discussion
  • Holmes's and Kärkkäinen's simplicity-based critique of social trinitarianism and Wisse's Augustinian critique of the same movement (Part 3) converge on an identical diagnosis from different centuries

Dogmatic & Pastoral Constitutions

  • Lumen Gentium (Dogmatic Constitution on the Church) — Promulgated 1964. Defines the Church, the sensus fidelium (LG 12), the universal call to holiness, and (per the Nota Praevia) episcopal collegiality.
  • Dei Verbum (Dogmatic Constitution on Divine Revelation) — Promulgated 1965. Covers the interplay of Scripture, Tradition, and Magisterium; scopes biblical inerrancy to truths "for the sake of our salvation" (DV 11).
  • Sacrosanctum Concilium (Constitution on the Sacred Liturgy) — Promulgated 1963. The first document debated and approved; introduces the vernacular and active participation.
  • Gaudium et Spes (Pastoral Constitution on the Church in the Modern World) — Promulgated 1965. Grounds culture as a theological resource (GS 58) and engages the joys and hopes of the world.

Claims

  • The rejection of De Fontibus Revelationis is the clearest single case of ressourcement defeating neo-scholasticism at the Council
  • The Nota Praevia settled collegiality by locking in the 'moderate liberal' interpretation, foreclosing the 'extreme liberal' reading some periti planned to press after the Council closed
  • Vatican II's most undeniable discontinuity, per John O'Malley, lies in its literary genre rather than its propositional content
  • Thomas Guarino's 'masking' thesis and Bishop Barron's popular 'middle path' describe the same underlying phenomenon in academic versus pastoral registers
  • The 'Rising Laity' theme is structurally, not just topically, connected to the collegiality debate
  • Black Week (16-21 November 1964) is best studied as a single compressed unit, not five separate incidents
  • Rahner's dual role as both a drafting-commission member and a co-author of the alternative revelation schema shows periti exercised more than advisory influence
  • The four-phase reception chronology shows the 'battle for meaning' has been fought institutionally, not only academically
